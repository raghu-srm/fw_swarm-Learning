1. Why This Matters

Every law in Modules B and C ends with a vector: a velocity command (Parts 12, 13), an acceleration command (Parts 9, 14), or a reference to track (Part 8). An aircraft does not accept vectors. Its autopilot accepts a course or bank, an airspeed and an altitude, realises them through its own loops with a bandwidth and a saturation, and does so in a moving air mass. This part is the dictionary between the two worlds, and it fixes the model of the aircraft that every later part — and the course's simulator — uses:

\[ \underbrace{v_i^{\mathrm{cmd}}\in\mathbb R^2}_{\text{swarm layer}}\ \longrightarrow\ \underbrace{(\chi^c,\ V_a^c,\ h^c)}_{\text{this part}}\ \longrightarrow\ \underbrace{\dot\chi=\operatorname{clip}\big(b_\chi\operatorname{wrap}(\chi^c-\chi),\pm\omega_{\max}\big),\ \dots}_{\text{command-level model}} \]
InputVectors and rotations (A1 §12), the acceleration projection of Part 9, slot velocities of Part 13.
OutputSolve the wind triangle both ways, simulate the command-level model with saturations, read the envelope table, wrap angles correctly, and define the packet a UAV broadcasts.
Used inPart 16 (where \(\omega_{\max}\) comes from), Part 18 (course commands), Part 20 (relative kinematics), Part 24 layer L4 and the simulator's vehicle.py.

2. Frames, Angles and Speeds

  • Inertial frame: NED. \(p=(p_n,p_e,p_d)\), north–east–down, flat-earth, origin at a mission datum. Altitude \(h=-p_d\). Right-handed, which is why "down" is the third axis.
  • Body frame. \(x_b\) out the nose, \(y_b\) out the right wing, \(z_b\) down. The Euler angles roll \(\phi\) (bank), pitch \(\theta\), yaw \(\psi\) take NED to body.
  • Heading \(\psi\): the direction the nose points, measured clockwise from north (the yaw angle). Course \(\chi\): the direction the aircraft actually moves over the ground, clockwise from north. They differ by the crab angle \(\psi-\chi\) whenever there is a crosswind.
  • Airspeed \(V_a\): speed relative to the air; what the wings feel, what the autopilot's speed loop holds, what stall and structural limits refer to. Ground speed \(V_g\): speed over the ground; what moves the aircraft relative to its neighbours and its slot.
  • Flight-path angle \(\gamma\): climb angle of the ground velocity (\(\sin\gamma=\dot h/V_g\)); the air-mass-referenced \(\gamma_a\) is the climb angle relative to the air. They differ in vertical wind.
  • Wind \(w=(w_n,w_e,w_d)\): velocity of the air mass in NED (\(w_d\gt0\) is a downdraft).
The two symbols you must never swap. Guidance and formation laws work in \(\chi\) and \(V_g\) (ground-relative: that is where the slots and the neighbours are). The autopilot's inner loops work in \(\psi\), \(\phi\) and \(V_a\). The wind triangle converts between them; a controller that commands \(\psi\) where it should command \(\chi\) drifts downwind by the crab angle.

3. The Wind Triangle

3.1 Planar form and its solution

Horizontal components, with the air velocity along the heading (zero sideslip) and the ground velocity along the course:

\[ \boxed{\;V_g\begin{bmatrix}\cos\chi\\ \sin\chi\end{bmatrix}=V_a\begin{bmatrix}\cos\psi\\ \sin\psi\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}w_n\\ w_e\end{bmatrix}\;} \]

Given the desired course \(\chi\), the airspeed \(V_a\) and the wind, resolve the wind along and across the course,

\[ w_\parallel=w_n\cos\chi+w_e\sin\chi,\qquad w_\perp=-w_n\sin\chi+w_e\cos\chi, \]

and project the triangle onto the course direction and its normal. Across: \(0=V_a\sin(\psi-\chi)+w_\perp\). Along: \(V_g=V_a\cos(\psi-\chi)+w_\parallel\). Hence

\[ \boxed{\;\psi-\chi=-\arcsin\frac{w_\perp}{V_a},\qquad V_g=w_\parallel+\sqrt{V_a^2-w_\perp^2}\;}\qquad(\text{requires }|w_\perp|\le V_a). \]

The aircraft points into the crosswind by the crab angle, and a tailwind component adds directly to the ground speed. If \(|w_\perp|\gt V_a\) the course is unachievable (a 25 m/s crosswind at \(V_a=20\) m/s cannot be crabbed out).

Example A (\(V_a=25\) m/s, wind \(w=(0,6)\), i.e. 6 m/s blowing toward the east):

3.2 Vector form: from a desired ground velocity

When the swarm layer supplies a desired inertial velocity \(v_d\) (Parts 12–13), the triangle is solved by subtraction:

\[ \chi^c=\operatorname{atan2}(v_{d,e},v_{d,n}),\quad V_g^c=\|v_d\|,\qquad v_a^c=v_d-w,\quad V_a^c=\|v_a^c\|,\quad \psi^c=\operatorname{atan2}(v_{a,e}^c,v_{a,n}^c). \]

Example B: \(v_d=(20,15)\) m/s, \(w=(0,6)\): \(\chi^c=36.87^\circ\), \(V_g^c=25\), \(v_a^c=(20,9)\), \(V_a^c=21.93\) m/s, \(\psi^c=24.23^\circ\), crab \(-12.64^\circ\) (Figure 1). The scalar solution of 3.1 with \(V_a=21.93\) returns the same crab, \(V_g\) and \(\psi\) — the two forms are the same triangle.

3.3 Three dimensions

\[ v_g=V_a\begin{bmatrix}\cos\psi\cos\gamma_a\\ \sin\psi\cos\gamma_a\\ -\sin\gamma_a\end{bmatrix}+w,\qquad V_g=\|v_g\|,\quad \chi=\operatorname{atan2}(v_{g,e},v_{g,n}),\quad \sin\gamma=-\frac{v_{g,d}}{V_g} . \]

Example: \(V_a=25\), \(\gamma_a=5^\circ\), heading north, \(w=(0,6,+1)\) (a 1 m/s downdraft): \(v_g=(24.905,\ 6,\ -1.179)\), \(V_g=25.64\) m/s, \(\chi=13.55^\circ\), \(\gamma=2.63^\circ\), \(\dot h=1.18\) m/s instead of \(V_a\sin\gamma_a=2.18\) m/s — the downdraft eats a metre per second of climb. Vertical wind is why altitude is best commanded through the autopilot's altitude loop (which measures \(h\)) rather than through a pitch or \(\gamma_a\) command.

4. The Point-Mass Guidance Model

For guidance and swarm design the aircraft is a point with a velocity vector constrained by its coordinated-turn behaviour (Beard & McLain 2012, Ch. 9). In NED, with wind:

\[ \dot p_n=V_g\cos\chi\cos\gamma,\qquad \dot p_e=V_g\sin\chi\cos\gamma,\qquad \dot h=V_g\sin\gamma, \] \[ \dot\chi=\frac{g\tan\phi}{V_g}\cos(\chi-\psi)\ \approx\ \frac{g\tan\phi}{V_g},\qquad \dot V_a=\frac{T-D}{m}-g\sin\gamma_a , \]

where \((V_g,\chi,\gamma)\) follow from \((V_a,\psi,\gamma_a)\) and \(w\) by the wind triangle. The turn-rate equation is the coordinated-turn relation of Part 16 (lift's horizontal component supplies the centripetal acceleration); the \(\cos(\chi-\psi)\) factor accounts for the crab angle and is within 3 % of one for crab angles below 14°. Assumptions: no sideslip, small pitch rates, quasi-steady turns, thrust along the body axis. What the model omits — roll dynamics, the lag between a bank command and the bank, engine response — is absorbed into the next model.

This model is often called the Dubins airplane (Part 17): a unicycle in the horizontal plane with bounded turn rate \(|\dot\chi|\le g\tan\phi_{\max}/V_g\), bounded speed \(V_{\min}\le V_a\le V_{\max}\), and a bounded climb angle.

5. The Command-Level (Autopilot-Abstracted) Model

The swarm layer never commands \(\phi\) or thrust directly; it sends setpoints to an autopilot whose successive loops (roll→course, pitch→altitude, throttle→airspeed; Part 16, Part 23) realise them. Seen from outside, each loop behaves approximately as a first-order lag with a rate limit:

\[ \boxed{\; \begin{aligned} \dot\chi&=\operatorname{clip}\big(b_\chi\operatorname{wrap}(\chi^c-\chi),\ \pm\omega_{\max}\big),&\omega_{\max}&=\frac{g\tan\phi_{\max}}{V_g},\\ \dot V_a&=\operatorname{clip}\big(b_V(V_a^c-V_a),\ \pm a_{\max}\big),& V_a&\in[V_{\min},V_{\max}],\\ \dot h&=\operatorname{clip}\big(b_h(h^c-h),\ \pm\dot h_{\max}\big), \end{aligned}\;} \]

with positions integrated by the kinematics of Section 4. Representative bandwidths: \(b_\chi\approx1.5\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}\) (0.24 Hz) for a small UAV with a fast roll loop, \(0.8\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}\) (0.13 Hz) for a fast aircraft; \(b_V\approx0.8\), \(b_h\approx0.5\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}\). These are design inputs: measure them from step responses of the real autopilot (Part 24 §16, test 1) before trusting any gain chosen from them.

5.1 When the model is valid

It is the standard time-scale separation approximation: the outer (swarm) loop must be slow compared with \(b_\chi,b_V,b_h\), typically by a factor of 2–3 (Part 9 §9.3, Part 24 check D2). Within that regime the inner loops look like a lag; outside it the neglected roll dynamics and actuator limits appear as overshoot and phase lag the model does not predict. The rate limits are not optional — they are the envelope (Section 7) and dominate every large manoeuvre.

5.2 The response to a step: rate-limited, then exponential

For a course step \(\Delta\chi\) larger than the linear threshold \(\omega_{\max}/b_\chi\), the loop first turns at \(\omega_{\max}\) for \(t_{\mathrm{sat}}=(\Delta\chi-\omega_{\max}/b_\chi)/\omega_{\max}\), then decays exponentially with time constant \(1/b_\chi\):

\[ t_{1^\circ}\approx\frac{\Delta\chi-\omega_{\max}/b_\chi}{\omega_{\max}}+\frac{1}{b_\chi}\ln\frac{\omega_{\max}/b_\chi}{1^\circ} . \]

Small UAV, \(V_g=25\) m/s, \(\phi_{\max}=45^\circ\): \(\omega_{\max}=22.5^\circ\)/s, threshold \(15.0^\circ\), \(\tau_\chi=0.67\) s. A \(60^\circ\) step is rate-limited for 2.0 s and within 1° at 3.81 s; a \(150^\circ\) step, 6.0 s and 7.81 s. Doubling the bandwidth to \(b_\chi=3\) only shortens the exponential tail (3.0 s and 7.0 s in simulation): for large steps the envelope, not the autopilot, sets the time. Likewise a 7 m/s speed step with \(b_V=0.8\), \(a_{\max}=3\) m/s² saturates for 1.08 s and settles to 0.1 m/s at 5.6 s; a 20 m altitude step with \(b_h=0.5\), \(\dot h_{\max}=3\) m/s saturates for 4.7 s and settles to 0.5 m at 9.6 s.

Between 20 and 250 m/s at the same bank the turn radius grows \(156\times\) (as \(V_g^2\)) and the turn rate falls \(12.5\times\) (as \(1/V_g\)). The linear threshold of the course loop, \(\omega_{\max}/b_\chi\), shrinks accordingly: \(18.7^\circ\) for the small UAV, \(6.3^\circ\) at 60 m/s, \(8.1^\circ\) at 150 m/s with \(b_\chi=0.8\) and \(\phi_{\max}=60^\circ\), \(4.9^\circ\) at 250 m/s — and only \(1.6^\circ\) if the fast aircraft is restricted to 30° of bank. Beyond those increments every course command is rate-limited.

Speed limits: the minimum airspeed rises in a turn as \(V_{s}\sqrt{n_z}\) (Part 16): a 12 m/s stall speed becomes 14.3 m/s at 45° (17.1 m/s with a 1.2 margin); a 60 m/s stall speed becomes 84.9 m/s at 60° and 120 m/s at \(n_z=4\). The lower edge of the envelope used by the swarm layer must be the turning stall speed at the bank the formation flies.

8. What a UAV Broadcasts; Relative Kinematics

The swarm state of UAV \(i\), as used by every law in the course and carried in its packet (Part 24 §4.2):

\[ x_i=\big(p_{n,i},\ p_{e,i},\ h_i,\ V_{g,i},\ \chi_i,\ \gamma_i,\ t_{\mathrm{stamp}}\big),\qquad v_i=V_{g,i}\begin{bmatrix}\cos\chi_i\\ \sin\chi_i\end{bmatrix}\ (\text{horizontal}). \]

Ground-relative quantities only: neighbours and slots live in NED, so airspeed and heading stay on board. Relative motion between two aircraft is then

\[ p_{ij}=p_j-p_i,\qquad \dot p_{ij}=v_j-v_i,\qquad \frac{d}{dt}\|p_{ij}\|=\frac{p_{ij}^T(v_j-v_i)}{\|p_{ij}\|}\ (\text{closing speed, negative when closing}), \]

which is the quantity the safety filter of Part 20 constrains. A packet received \(\tau\) seconds late is dead-reckoned with \(\tilde p_j=p_j+v_j\tau\); the error of this straight-line extrapolation against the true arc in a turn is \(\approx\tfrac12g\tan\phi\,\tau^2\): 0.05 m for \(\tau=0.1\) s and 4.9 m for \(\tau=1\) s at 45° bank, 19 m for 1 s at \(n_z=4\) (75.5° bank) — independent of speed, which is why dead-reckoning works at high speed as long as the latency is short.

9. From a Desired Velocity to Commands

The last block before the autopilot (Part 24 layer L4), with the wind estimate \(w\) from the autopilot:

\[ \boxed{\; \chi^c=\operatorname{atan2}(v_{d,e},v_{d,n}),\qquad V_a^c=\operatorname{clip}\big(\|v_d-w\|,\ V_{\min},\ V_{\max}\big),\qquad h^c=h_c+\Delta h_i\;} \]

Command course, not heading: the autopilot's course loop (or an L1/NPFG lateral guidance, Part 18) crabs automatically. Command airspeed through the triangle, so that a formation flying into the wind holds its ground-frame slots at a higher \(V_a\). If the autopilot accepts only a heading, send \(\psi^c\) from Section 3.2 and accept that wind estimation errors become cross-track drift.

Angle wrapping

\[ \operatorname{wrap}(a)=\pi-\operatorname{mod}(\pi-a,\ 2\pi)\in(-\pi,\pi] . \]

Tests: \(270^\circ\to-90^\circ\), \(210^\circ\to-150^\circ\), \(-190^\circ\to170^\circ\), \(540^\circ\to180^\circ\), \(359.9^\circ\to-0.1^\circ\). Apply it to every angle difference — course errors, heading-estimate differences in the Part 8 estimator, slot course offsets — never to an angle alone, and never average angles directly (average the unit vectors, Part 5 §8).

10. High-Speed Notes and the CLAW

High-speed note. Three things change with speed in this part, and all three are already visible in the tables. (i) The turn radius grows as \(V_g^2\) and the turn rate falls as \(1/V_g\): a 90° course change takes 3 s for a small UAV at 45° and 40 s for a 250 m/s aircraft — formation geometry changes must be planned, not reacted to. (ii) The linear threshold \(\omega_{\max}/b_\chi\) shrinks to a few degrees, so the course loop of a fast aircraft is almost always rate-limited; the swarm layer must limit its course increments (the capstone's \(\Delta v_{\max}\)) or accept the lag of saturation. (iii) The distance flown per 100 ms grows to 15–25 m, so stale packets must be dead-reckoned (Section 8); fortunately the arc error of dead reckoning depends on bank and latency, not on speed. What does not change: the wind triangle, the model structure, and the need to command course rather than heading.
Toward the CLAW This part supplies three concrete pieces of the unified law. The vehicle model of Section 5 is the plant in every stability argument and in the simulator's vehicle.py, with \(b_\chi,b_V,b_h\) and the saturations as measured design inputs. The command mapping of Section 9 is layer L4 (Part 24 §9) verbatim, including the wind-triangle airspeed command and the wrap function. The packet of Section 8 is what L1–L3 consume, and its dead-reckoning rule is Part 24 §4.4. Part 16 next derives \(\omega_{\max}\), the roll lag behind it, and the structural and energy limits that shape the high-speed envelope.

11. Implementation

The command-level model with wind, angle wrapping and the three-UAV step experiment of Section 6 (pure NumPy, 20 Hz). Set wind = [0.0, 6.0] for the wind-on run.

import numpy as np
g = 9.81
def wrap(a):                                  # wrap angle to (-pi, pi]
    return np.pi - np.mod(np.pi - a, 2*np.pi)
def wind_triangle(Va, chi, w):                # returns Vg and crab angle psi-chi
    w_par  =  w[0]*np.cos(chi) + w[1]*np.sin(chi)
    w_perp = -w[0]*np.sin(chi) + w[1]*np.cos(chi)
    return w_par + np.sqrt(Va**2 - w_perp**2), -np.arcsin(w_perp/Va)
def step(x, cmd, prm, w, dt):
    """Command-level model.  x = [pn, pe, h, Va, chi];  cmd = [chi_c, Va_c, h_c]."""
    pn, pe, h, Va, chi = x
    chi_c, Va_c, h_c = cmd
    Vg, _ = wind_triangle(Va, chi, w)
    omega_max = g*np.tan(prm['phi_max'])/Vg                       # turn-rate envelope
    chi_dot = np.clip(prm['b_chi']*wrap(chi_c - chi), -omega_max, omega_max)
    Va_dot  = np.clip(prm['b_V']*(Va_c - Va), -prm['a_max'], prm['a_max'])
    h_dot   = np.clip(prm['b_h']*(h_c - h), -prm['hdot_max'], prm['hdot_max'])
    pn += Vg*np.cos(chi)*dt                                       # NED kinematics
    pe += Vg*np.sin(chi)*dt
    return np.array([pn, pe, h + h_dot*dt, Va + Va_dot*dt, wrap(chi + chi_dot*dt)])

prm = dict(b_chi=1.5, b_V=0.8, b_h=0.5, phi_max=np.radians(45), a_max=3.0, hdot_max=3.0)
dt, T = 0.05, 12.0                                                # 20 Hz, 12 s
wind = np.array([0.0, 0.0])                                       # set to [0.0, 6.0] for the wind-on run
X = np.array([[0., 0., 100., 25., 0.],                            # UAV 1
              [0., 100., 100., 25., 0.],                          # UAV 2
              [0., 200., 100., 25., 0.]])                         # UAV 3  (all heading north)
CMD = np.array([[np.radians(60.), 25., 100.],                     # UAV 1: course step +60 deg
                [np.radians(210.), 25., 100.],                    # UAV 2: course 210 deg (wrap -> -150 deg)
                [0., 32., 120.]])                                 # UAV 3: speed +7 m/s, altitude +20 m
n = int(round(T/dt)); t = np.arange(n + 1)*dt
hist = np.zeros((n + 1, 3, 5)); hist[0] = X
for k in range(n):
    for i in range(3):
        X[i] = step(X[i], CMD[i], prm, wind, dt)
    hist[k + 1] = X
for i in range(3):
    e_chi = np.abs(np.degrees(wrap(CMD[i, 0] - hist[:, i, 4])))
    e_V   = np.abs(CMD[i, 1] - hist[:, i, 3]);  e_h = np.abs(CMD[i, 2] - hist[:, i, 2])
    first = lambda ok: t[np.argmax(ok)] if ok.any() else np.nan
    Vg, crab = wind_triangle(X[i, 3], X[i, 4], wind)
    print(f"UAV {i+1}: chi={np.degrees(X[i,4]):8.2f} deg  Va={X[i,3]:5.2f}  Vg={Vg:5.2f}  crab={np.degrees(crab):6.2f} deg  "
          f"h={X[i,2]:6.2f}  p=({X[i,0]:7.1f},{X[i,1]:7.1f})  "
          f"t(|e_chi|<1deg)={first(e_chi < 1):5.2f}  t(|e_V|<0.1)={first(e_V < 0.1):5.2f}  t(|e_h|<0.5)={first(e_h < 0.5):5.2f}")

12. Common Pitfalls

  • Heading where course is meant. Formation slots and neighbours are in the ground frame; commanding \(\psi\) instead of \(\chi\) drifts downwind by the crab angle.
  • Sign of the crab angle. \(\psi-\chi=-\arcsin(w_\perp/V_a)\): nose into the crosswind. Check with Example A (wind to the east ⇒ heading west of the course when flying north).
  • Airspeed in the position kinematics. \(\dot p=V_g(\cos\chi,\sin\chi)\), never \(V_a\); and \(\omega_{\max}=g\tan\phi_{\max}/V_g\) with the ground speed.
  • Raw angle differences. Wrap every difference; the 210° command is a 150° left turn, not a 210° right turn.
  • Trusting the first-order model at the inner-loop bandwidth. Keep the outer loop 2–3× slower than \(b_\chi\); measure \(b_\chi\) rather than assuming it.
  • Forgetting that large steps are rate-limited. The time for a big course change is \(\Delta\chi/\omega_{\max}\) and grows with \(V_g\); bandwidth does not help.
  • Unachievable courses in strong wind (\(|w_\perp|\gt V_a\)) and the turning stall speed \(V_s\sqrt{n_z}\) as the real lower speed limit.

\(\chi^c=\operatorname{atan2}(15,20)=36.87^\circ\), \(V_g^c=25\) m/s; \(v_a^c=(20,9)\), \(V_a^c=21.93\) m/s, \(\psi^c=\operatorname{atan2}(9,20)=24.23^\circ\); crab \(-12.64^\circ\).
Exercise 3 (rate-limited step). A 60 m/s aircraft with \(\phi_{\max}=45^\circ\) and \(b_\chi=1.0\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}\) receives a \(90^\circ\) course step. How long is it rate-limited, when is it within 1°, and how far does it fly meanwhile?
Show solution
\(\omega_{\max}=9.81/60=0.1635\) rad/s \(=9.37^\circ\)/s; threshold \(9.37^\circ\); \(t_{\mathrm{sat}}=(90-9.37)/9.37=8.6\) s; \(t_{1^\circ}=8.6+\ln(9.37)/1=10.8\) s; distance \(60\times10.8=651\) m.
Exercise 4 (3-D triangle). \(V_a=25\) m/s, \(\gamma_a=5^\circ\), heading north, wind \((0,6,+1)\) m/s. Compute the ground velocity, \(V_g\), \(\chi\), \(\gamma\) and the climb rate.
Show solution
\(v_g=25(\cos5^\circ,0,-\sin5^\circ)+(0,6,1)=(24.905,\ 6,\ -1.179)\); \(V_g=25.64\) m/s; \(\chi=\operatorname{atan2}(6,24.905)=13.55^\circ\); \(\sin\gamma=1.179/25.64\Rightarrow\gamma=2.63^\circ\); \(\dot h=1.18\) m/s (the downdraft removes 1 m/s of the 2.18 m/s air-mass climb).
Exercise 5 (wrap). Show that \(\operatorname{wrap}(a)=\pi-\operatorname{mod}(\pi-a,2\pi)\) maps every real \(a\) into \((-\pi,\pi]\) and differs from \(a\) by a multiple of \(2\pi\); evaluate it at \(210^\circ\) and \(-190^\circ\).
Show solution
\(\operatorname{mod}(\cdot,2\pi)\in[0,2\pi)\), so \(\pi-\operatorname{mod}(\cdot)\in(-\pi,\pi]\); and \(\pi-\operatorname{mod}(\pi-a,2\pi)=\pi-(\pi-a-2\pi k)=a+2\pi k\). \(210^\circ\to-150^\circ\); \(-190^\circ\to170^\circ\).
Exercise 6 (dead reckoning at speed). A 200 m/s aircraft turning at \(n_z=4\) (bank 75.5°) reports its position with 1 s latency. How far has it flown, and how wrong is a straight-line extrapolation?
Show solution
Along-track \(200\) m; the arc error is \(\tfrac12g\tan(75.5^\circ)\cdot1^2=19\) m (exact: 18.97 m) — the same 19 m a 25 m/s aircraft would accumulate at that bank, because the error depends on \(g\tan\phi\,\tau^2\), not on speed.

14. Key Takeaways & Formula Sheet

  • NED frame, \(h=-p_d\); heading \(\psi\) (nose) vs course \(\chi\) (track), both clockwise from north; \(V_a\) vs \(V_g\); \(\gamma\) vs \(\gamma_a\).
  • Wind triangle \(V_g(\cos\chi,\sin\chi)=V_a(\cos\psi,\sin\psi)+(w_n,w_e)\); \(\psi-\chi=-\arcsin(w_\perp/V_a)\), \(V_g=w_\parallel+\sqrt{V_a^2-w_\perp^2}\); vector form \(v_a^c=v_d-w\).
  • Point-mass model: \(\dot p_n=V_g\cos\chi\cos\gamma\), \(\dot p_e=V_g\sin\chi\cos\gamma\), \(\dot h=V_g\sin\gamma\), \(\dot\chi\approx g\tan\phi/V_g\).
  • Command-level model: first-order lags \(b_\chi,b_V,b_h\) with rate limits \(\omega_{\max}=g\tan\phi_{\max}/V_g\), \(a_{\max}\), \(\dot h_{\max}\); valid under 2–3× time-scale separation; large steps are rate-limited for \((\Delta-\text{threshold})/\text{rate}\) then exponential.
  • Envelope: \(R\propto V_g^2\), \(\omega\propto1/V_g\); linear threshold \(\omega_{\max}/b_\chi\) shrinks with speed; turning stall speed \(V_s\sqrt{n_z}\).
  • Packet \((p_n,p_e,h,V_g,\chi,\gamma,t)\); \(\dot p_{ij}=v_j-v_i\); dead-reckon \(p_j+v_j\tau\) with arc error \(\tfrac12g\tan\phi\,\tau^2\).
  • Mapping: \(\chi^c=\operatorname{atan2}(v_e,v_n)\), \(V_a^c=\operatorname{clip}\|v_d-w\|\), wrap all angle differences with \(\pi-\operatorname{mod}(\pi-a,2\pi)\).
