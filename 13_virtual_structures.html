1. Why This Matters

Leader–follower formations (Part 8) hang the whole swarm on one aircraft: its state is the reference, its loss is the end of the formation, and every follower lags it by the structural amount \(H^{-1}\mathbf 1u_0\). The virtual structure (Lewis & Tan 1997) removes the aircraft from the reference. The formation is a rigid body that exists only as a computation: a centre, a heading, a speed and a turn rate. Each aircraft is assigned a fixed slot in that body and tracks the slot. Nobody has to fly the centre; the centre can do things no aircraft could (it can be at the middle of a vee with nothing there), and it can be made to wait for aircraft that fall behind.

\[ \text{reference }\xi=(p_c,\psi_c,V_c,\omega_c,h_c)\ \longrightarrow\ \text{slot } p_i^d=p_c+R(\psi_c)\,r_i\ \longrightarrow\ \text{each aircraft tracks its slot} \]

Three things have to be got right, and they are the three technical sections of this part: the slot's velocity (not just its position — Part 12 showed what the missing feedforward costs), whether every slot is flyable while the body turns, and how \(N\) aircraft can share one body without a central computer.

2. The Virtual Rigid Body

A planar formation is a rigid body with three degrees of freedom; a flyable one also has a speed, a turn rate and an altitude. Its state and kinematics (NED, course clockwise from north, Part 15):

\[ \xi=(p_c,\ \psi_c,\ V_c,\ \omega_c,\ h_c),\qquad \dot p_c=v_c=V_c\begin{bmatrix}\cos\psi_c\\ \sin\psi_c\end{bmatrix},\qquad \dot\psi_c=\omega_c,\qquad \omega_c=\begin{cases}0&\text{on a leg}\\ \pm\,g\tan\phi_c/V_c&\text{in a turn.}\end{cases} \]

The body's own motion is a coordinated turn of bank \(\phi_c\) (Part 16) or straight flight; \(V_c,\omega_c,h_c\) are piecewise constant mission data (a racetrack: legs of length \(\ell\) and 180° turns). Slots are fixed vectors \(r_i=(r_{n,i},r_{e,i})\) in the body frame (Part 10), plus altitude offsets \(\Delta h_i\). Positive \(\omega_c\) is a right turn (clockwise seen from above), and \(r_{e,i}\gt0\) is to the right of the centre.

3. Slot Kinematics: Position, Velocity, Acceleration

3.1 The derivative of a rotation matrix

With \(R(\psi)=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\psi&-\sin\psi\\ \sin\psi&\cos\psi\end{bmatrix}\) and \(J=\begin{bmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{bmatrix}\):

\[ \frac{dR}{d\psi}=\begin{bmatrix}-\sin\psi&-\cos\psi\\ \cos\psi&-\sin\psi\end{bmatrix} =\begin{bmatrix}\cos\psi&-\sin\psi\\ \sin\psi&\cos\psi\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{bmatrix}=R(\psi)J \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{\;\dot R(\psi)=\dot\psi\,R(\psi)\,J\;} \]

(Multiply out the middle product: first column \((\cos\psi\cdot0-\sin\psi\cdot1,\ \sin\psi\cdot0+\cos\psi\cdot1)=(-\sin\psi,\cos\psi)\) ✓, second column \((-\cos\psi,-\sin\psi)\) ✓.) Two facts used below: \(RJ=JR\) (planar rotations commute, verified numerically to \(10^{-16}\)), and \(J^2=-I\). Finite differences confirm \(\dot R=\omega RJ\) to \(3\times10^{-11}\).

3.2 Slot position and velocity

\[ p_i^d=p_c+R(\psi_c)\,r_i\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \boxed{\;v_i^d=\dot p_i^d=v_c+\omega_c\,R(\psi_c)\,J\,r_i\;} \]

The second term is the rigid-body rotation velocity: perpendicular to the slot's offset (because \(Jr_i\perp r_i\)), of magnitude \(\omega_c\|r_i\|\), rotated into the inertial frame. In the body frame (\(\psi_c=0\), \(v_c=(V_c,0)\)) it reads

\[ v_i^d\big|_{\text{body}}=\begin{bmatrix}V_c-\omega_c\,r_{e,i}\\ \omega_c\,r_{n,i}\end{bmatrix},\qquad \boxed{\;V_i=\|v_i^d\|=\sqrt{(V_c-\omega_cr_{e,i})^2+(\omega_cr_{n,i})^2}=\omega_c\,R_i\;},\qquad \chi_i^d=\psi_c+\operatorname{atan2}\!\big(\omega_cr_{n,i},\ V_c-\omega_cr_{e,i}\big), \]

where \(R_i=\sqrt{r_{n,i}^2+(r_{e,i}-R_c)^2}\) is the slot's distance from the turn centre (which sits at \(r=(0,R_c)\) in the body frame for a right turn). Three readings:

  • Inside slower, outside faster (\(r_{e,i}\gt0\) inside a right turn lowers \(V_i\)): Part 10's feasibility result, now derived from the rigid-body kinematics.
  • Slots ahead of or behind the centre fly a different course: \(\chi_i^d\ne\psi_c\) whenever \(r_{n,i}\ne0\). A trail slot's velocity is rotated toward the inside of the turn because it is on the same circle, further back.
  • Every slot's bank follows from its own speed: \(\tan\phi_i=V_i\omega_c/g\) (all slots share \(\omega_c\); Part 16).

3.3 Slot acceleration

Differentiating again, with \(\dot v_c=\dot V_c\hat t+\omega_cJv_c\) (speed change along the course, centripetal across it):

\[ a_i^d=\dot V_c\,\hat t(\psi_c)+\omega_c\,J\,v_c+\dot\omega_c\,R(\psi_c)Jr_i-\omega_c^2\,R(\psi_c)\,r_i , \]

which in a steady turn (\(\dot V_c=\dot\omega_c=0\)) is the centripetal term of the centre plus the centripetal term of the offset. Finite-difference checks on the example below agree to \(2\times10^{-6}\) m/s². Second-order laws (Part 9) and acceleration-setpoint autopilots (Part 23) consume \(a_i^d\); the velocity-level law of Part 12 needs only \(v_i^d\).

5. Flyability: Who Limits the Turn

Every slot's speed is a quadratic in \(\omega_c\): \(V_i^2=V_c^2-2V_c\omega_cr_{e,i}+\omega_c^2\|r_i\|^2\). The largest turn rate the formation can fly is the smallest positive root over the slots of

\[ \|r_i\|^2\,\omega_c^2-2V_cr_{e,i}\,\omega_c+\big(V_c^2-V_{\max}^2\big)=0\quad(\text{outer slots}),\qquad \|r_i\|^2\,\omega_c^2-2V_cr_{e,i}\,\omega_c+\big(V_c^2-V_{\min}^2\big)=0\quad(\text{inner slots}), \]

together with \(\arctan(V_i\omega_c/g)\le\phi_{\max}\). For the diamond the outer slot 3 binds: \(\omega_{c,\max}=0.1996\) rad/s \(=11.4^\circ\)/s (a grid search over \(\omega_c\) gives 0.1995), i.e. \(\phi_c\le27.0^\circ\), \(R_c\ge125.3\) m, with slot speeds \((25.0,\ 20.6,\ 32.0,\ 29.7)\) m/s and banks \((27.0^\circ,22.8^\circ,33.1^\circ,31.1^\circ)\). This is Part 10's feasibility check in its general form: there \(y_i\) was a pure lateral offset; here the along-track component \(r_{n,i}\) also raises \(V_i\), which is why the trail slot nearly binds too.

Design rule. The body's bank limit is set by the outermost and rearmost slots, not by the lead aircraft. Compute \(\omega_{c,\max}\) from the slot table before flying the body, and either reduce \(\phi_c\) (larger \(R_c\)), shrink the formation, or morph to a trail before the turn (Section 9).

High-speed example

\(V_c=150\) m/s, \(\phi_c=45^\circ\): \(\omega_c=0.0654\) rad/s \(=3.75^\circ\)/s, \(R_c=2294\) m. A slot 250 m laterally outside flies 166.3 m/s at 48.0° bank (\(n_z=1.49\)); 250 m inside, 133.7 m/s at 41.7°; a slot 400 m in trail flies 152.3 m/s at 45.4° — almost unchanged, because along-track offsets enter \(V_i\) only through \(\sqrt{V_c^2+\omega_c^2r_n^2}\), second order in \(\omega_cr_n/V_c=0.17\). Trail is the turn-friendly formation at high speed.

6. Centralised vs Synchronised Structure

In the original scheme one computer propagates \(\xi\) and broadcasts slot commands: a single point of failure and a bandwidth hog. The synchronised virtual structure (Ren & Beard 2004) gives every aircraft its own copy \(\hat\xi_i\), propagated locally with the body model and kept consistent by consensus — exactly the distributed leader-state estimator of Part 8 §7 with the body kinematics as the model:

\[ \boxed{\;\dot{\hat\xi}_i=f(\hat\xi_i)-k_\xi\Big[\sum_{j\in\mathcal N_i^{\mathrm{in}}}w_{ij}\big(\hat\xi_i\ominus\hat\xi_j\big)+b_i\big(\hat\xi_i\ominus\xi\big)\Big]\;},\qquad f(\hat\xi_i)=\big(\hat V_{c,i}\hat t(\hat\psi_{c,i}),\ \hat\omega_{c,i},\ 0,\ 0,\ 0\big), \]

where \(\ominus\) wraps the heading difference, and \(b_i\gt0\) marks the aircraft that receive the authoritative \(\xi\) (from the ground station, or whoever generates the mission). Between packets each copy flies on its own model; consensus only removes drift and distributes changes of \(V_c,\omega_c\) (turn entries) from the pinned aircraft to the rest.

6.1 How fast do the copies agree?

Four aircraft, undirected graph 1–2, 1–3, 2–4, 3–4, \(k_\xi=1\): pinning 1 and 2 gives \(\lambda(H)=\{0.382,2.382,2.618,4.618\}\), slowest time constant \(2.6\) s — 65 m of flight at 25 m/s, 393 m at 150 m/s; pinning only aircraft 1 gives \(\lambda_{\min}(H)=0.186\), time constant \(5.4\) s — 134 m / 805 m. A turn entry announced to the pinned aircraft is therefore executed by the far side of the formation a few hundred metres late at high speed unless (a) more aircraft are pinned, (b) \(k_\xi\) is raised toward its sampling limit (Part 8 §7.2), or (c) the turn is announced in advance with its start time, so that every copy switches \(\hat\omega_c\) simultaneously and consensus only corrects clock error.

6.2 Dead-reckoning error between packets

A copy whose turn-rate estimate is wrong by \(\Delta\omega\) drifts laterally by about \(\tfrac12V\Delta\omega\,T^2\) over a packet gap \(T\): \(0.9\) m for a small UAV (25 m/s, 1°/s, 2 s), but \(32.7\) m at 150 m/s with a 1°/s error over a 5 s gap, and \(6.5\) m with a 0.2°/s error. At high speed the turn-rate component of \(\hat\xi\) must be accurate to a fraction of a degree per second, or the packet gap must be short.

7. Formation Feedback

A reference that ignores the aircraft will leave a slow one behind. Formation feedback (Beard, Lawton & Hadaegh 2001; Ren & Beard 2004) lets the body respond to its members' slot errors \(\varepsilon_i=p_i-p_i^d\):

\[ \dot p_c=v_c+k_f\sum_i\varepsilon_i\qquad(\text{and similarly }\dot\psi_c=\omega_c+k_f'\textstyle\sum_i\,\text{angular slot errors}) . \]

If every aircraft lags (\(\varepsilon_i\) pointing backwards), the body slows down; if they lead, it speeds up. Along-track scalar example: \(V_c=30\) m/s but a member can only fly \(V_{\max}=28\) m/s; with slot gain \(k_p=0.5\) and \(k_f=0.05\) the member settles \(40\) m behind its slot and the body settles at exactly 28 m/s — \(\varepsilon_{ss}=(V_{\max}-V_c)/k_f\) — instead of running away at 30 m/s with an ever-growing gap. The trade-off: the body is no longer an exogenous reference; the loop body → slot → aircraft → body must be analysed for stability (it is a cascade with feedback, so the small-gain condition \(k_f\sum_i(\cdot)\) small relative to the tracking bandwidth applies), and in the synchronised version every copy must agree on the same feedback, which means the slot errors travel in the packets too.

9. Fixed-Wing Interpretation

  • Each slot is its own coordinated turn. In the turn, aircraft \(i\) flies at \(V_i=\omega_cR_i\) on the course \(\chi_i^d\) of Section 3.2, at bank \(\arctan(V_i\omega_c/g)\). The formation guidance law (Part 12, Part 24 L2) delivers this automatically through \(v_i^d\); a heading-synchronisation law would not.
  • The body's bank is limited by its worst slot (Section 5), and the limit is a speed-envelope limit before it is a bank limit.
  • Morphing before the turn. Because the slots are mission data, \(r_i(t)\) can change: a vee collapses into a trail a few seconds before a turn (\(v_i^d\) then gains the term \(R(\psi_c)\dot r_i\)), the turn is flown at the lead's bank, and the vee re-forms on the next leg. With synchronised copies all aircraft morph in step.
  • What to broadcast. \(\xi\) with a timestamp, and preferably the future \(\xi\) (turn start time, bank, speed) so that copies switch simultaneously. Pinned aircraft dead-reckon the received \(\xi\) by \(V_c\tau\) before using it (Part 8 §9.3).
  • Altitude stacking. \(h_i^d=h_c+\Delta h_i\) with \(\Delta h_i\) part of the slot table — cheap de-confliction that the safety filter (Part 20) can exploit.
High-speed note. At 150 m/s the lateral slot speeds of a 500 m-wide vee differ by ±16 m/s from the centre's (Section 5) — close to the envelope — while a 400 m trail slot differs by 2 m/s. The synchronised copies disagree for a few seconds after every turn entry, which is 400–800 m of flight (Section 6.1), and a 1°/s turn-rate error costs 33 m of lateral drift in a 5 s packet gap. Hence the high-speed pattern of the capstone (Part 24 §13): fly vees on legs, trail in turns, announce turns ahead, pin at least two aircraft, and keep \(\hat\omega_c\) accurate rather than relying on fast consensus to fix it.
Toward the CLAW This part is the capstone's reference architecture. Layer L0 generates \(\xi\) (Section 2, with the racetrack generator); layer L1 is the synchronised copy \(\hat\xi_i\) of Section 6 (the estimator of Part 8 with the body model); layer L2 tracks the slot using the position \(p_i^d\) and the velocity feedforward \(v_i^d=v_c+\omega_cR(\psi_c)Jr_i\) of Section 3 — the term whose absence produces the Part 12 lag; and check D1 of the design procedure is the flyability computation of Section 5, applied to the outermost slot. Formation feedback (Section 7) is listed as an extension because it closes a loop through the reference that the capstone's cascade proof deliberately avoids.

10. Implementation

Pure NumPy. Computes slot position/velocity/acceleration from the body state, the slot table of Section 4, the flyability limit of Section 5, and checks the velocity formula by finite differences.

import numpy as np
np.set_printoptions(precision=3, suppress=True)
g = 9.81
def R(psi):
    return np.array([[np.cos(psi), -np.sin(psi)], [np.sin(psi), np.cos(psi)]])
J = np.array([[0.0, -1.0], [1.0, 0.0]])

# structure: V_c = 25 m/s, bank 30 deg in the turns, legs of 2000 m
V_c, phi_c, leg = 25.0, np.radians(30.0), 2000.0
omega_c = g*np.tan(phi_c)/V_c            # turn rate in the turns (0 on the legs)
R_c = V_c/omega_c
slots = np.array([[0.0, 0.0], [-40.0, 30.0], [-40.0, -30.0], [-80.0, 0.0]])   # r_i (n, e) in the formation frame
V_min, V_max, phi_max = 16.0, 32.0, np.radians(45.0)

def slot_state(p_c, psi_c, V_c, omega_c, r_i):
    """slot position, velocity and acceleration from the virtual-structure state"""
    v_c = V_c*np.array([np.cos(psi_c), np.sin(psi_c)])
    p_d = p_c + R(psi_c) @ r_i
    v_d = v_c + omega_c*R(psi_c) @ J @ r_i                      # from dR/dt = omega R J
    a_d = omega_c*J @ v_c - omega_c**2*R(psi_c) @ r_i           # steady turn: omega_dot = 0
    return p_d, v_d, a_d

print("omega_c = %.4f rad/s = %.2f deg/s, R_c = %.1f m" % (omega_c, np.degrees(omega_c), R_c))
print("slot |  R_i [m] | V_i [m/s] | course-psi_c [deg] | bank [deg] |  n_z  | flyable")
for i, r_i in enumerate(slots):
    p_d, v_d, a_d = slot_state(np.zeros(2), 0.0, V_c, omega_c, r_i)
    V_i = np.linalg.norm(v_d)
    R_i = np.sqrt(r_i[0]**2 + (r_i[1] - R_c)**2)               # distance from the turn centre (right turn)
    off = np.degrees(np.arctan2(v_d[1], v_d[0]))
    phi_i = np.arctan(V_i*omega_c/g)
    ok = (V_min <= V_i <= V_max) and (phi_i <= phi_max)
    print("  %d  | %7.1f | %8.2f | %13.2f | %9.2f | %.3f | %s" % (i+1, R_i, V_i, off, np.degrees(phi_i), 1/np.cos(phi_i), ok))

# largest omega_c such that every slot stays inside the speed envelope (quadratic in omega_c)
w_ok = np.inf
for r_i in slots[1:]:                                           # slot 1 sits at p_c: always V_c
    a, b, c = r_i @ r_i, -2*V_c*r_i[1], V_c**2 - V_max**2       # |v_d|^2 = V_max^2
    w_ok = min(w_ok, (-b + np.sqrt(b*b - 4*a*c))/(2*a))
print("max flyable omega_c = %.4f rad/s (%.2f deg/s) -> phi_c = %.2f deg, R_c = %.1f m"
      % (w_ok, np.degrees(w_ok), np.degrees(np.arctan(w_ok*V_c/g)), V_c/w_ok))

# finite-difference check of the slot velocity on a turning structure
dt = 1e-5
def p_slot(t, r_i):
    psi = 0.3 + omega_c*t
    centre = R(0.3) @ np.array([0.0, R_c])                       # right turn: centre to the right
    p_c = centre + R(psi) @ np.array([0.0, -R_c])
    return p_c + R(psi) @ r_i
fd = (p_slot(dt, slots[2]) - p_slot(-dt, slots[2]))/(2*dt)
_, v_d, _ = slot_state(p_slot(0, np.zeros(2)), 0.3, V_c, omega_c, slots[2])
print("slot 3 velocity: finite difference", fd, " formula", v_d)
print("racetrack: turn time %.2f s, leg time %.1f s, lap %.1f s" % (np.pi/omega_c, leg/V_c, 2*leg/V_c + 2*np.pi/omega_c))

11. Common Pitfalls

  • Tracking the slot position without its velocity. The \(\omega_cR(\psi_c)Jr_i\) term is the difference between holding formation in a turn and lagging by \(\|v_i^d\|/k_p\) (Part 12).
  • Commanding the body heading as the slot's course. The trail slot in Section 4 flies 36° inside \(\psi_c\). Use \(\chi_i^d=\operatorname{atan2}(v_{e,i}^d,v_{n,i}^d)\).
  • Choosing \(\phi_c\) from the lead aircraft. The outermost and rearmost slots bind; compute Section 5's limit.
  • Sign conventions. Positive \(\omega_c\) = right turn, turn centre at \(r=(0,R_c)\), \(r_e\gt0\) inside the right turn. A flipped sign puts the fast slot on the wrong side.
  • Synchronising positions but not rates. Copies that agree on \(\hat p_c\) but not on \(\hat\omega_c\) diverge between packets by \(\tfrac12V\Delta\omega T^2\).
  • No timestamps. A received \(\xi\) is \(V_c\tau\) metres stale; dead-reckon before fusing.
  • Formation feedback without analysis. It closes a loop through the reference; keep \(k_f\) small relative to the slot-tracking bandwidth and send the slot errors with the packets.

12. Exercises

Exercise 1 (rotation derivative). Show that \(R(\psi)J=JR(\psi)\) and \(J^2=-I\), and use them to derive the slot acceleration in a steady turn from \(v_i^d=v_c+\omega_cR(\psi_c)Jr_i\).
Show solution
\(J\) is the rotation by \(+90^\circ\), and planar rotations commute, so \(RJ=JR\); \(J^2=R(180^\circ)=-I\). With \(\dot v_c=\omega_cJv_c\) (steady turn) and \(\dot R=\omega_cRJ\): \(a_i^d=\omega_cJv_c+\omega_c\cdot\omega_cRJ\cdot Jr_i=\omega_cJv_c+\omega_c^2RJ^2r_i=\omega_cJv_c-\omega_c^2Rr_i\).
Exercise 2 (slot in a turn). \(V_c=40\) m/s, \(\phi_c=35^\circ\), slot \(r=(-60,-45)\) m. Find \(\omega_c\), \(R_c\), the slot's radius, speed, course offset and bank.
Show solution
\(\omega_c=9.81\tan35^\circ/40=0.1717\) rad/s \(=9.84^\circ\)/s, \(R_c=232.9\) m; \(R_i=\sqrt{60^2+(-45-232.9)^2}=284.3\) m; \(V_i=\omega_cR_i=48.8\) m/s; body-frame velocity \((40+0.1717\cdot45,\ -0.1717\cdot60)=(47.7,-10.3)\), offset \(\operatorname{atan2}(-10.3,47.7)=-12.2^\circ\); bank \(\arctan(48.8\cdot0.1717/9.81)=40.5^\circ\), \(n_z=1.32\).
Exercise 3 (flyability). For \(V_c=25\) m/s, \(V_{\max}=32\) m/s and an outer-rear slot \(r=(-80,-60)\) m, find the largest \(\omega_c\), the corresponding \(\phi_c\) and \(R_c\).
Show solution
\(\|r\|^2=10\,000\), \(-2V_cr_e=3000\), \(V_c^2-V_{\max}^2=-399\): \(10\,000\,\omega^2+3000\,\omega-399=0\Rightarrow\omega_c=0.0998\) rad/s \(=5.72^\circ\)/s; \(\phi_c=\arctan(0.0998\cdot25/9.81)=14.3^\circ\); \(R_c=250.5\) m. Check: \(V_i=\|(25+0.0998\cdot60,\ -0.0998\cdot80)\|=32.0\) m/s.
Exercise 4 (synchronisation). With the 4-aircraft graph of Section 6.1 and \(k_\xi=1\), compare the slowest time constant for pinning \(\{1,2\}\) versus \(\{1\}\), and the distance flown at 150 m/s. What third option reduces the delay without touching the graph?
Show solution
\(\{1,2\}\): \(\lambda_{\min}(H)=0.382\), \(\tau=2.6\) s, 393 m; \(\{1\}\): \(0.186\), \(5.4\) s, 805 m. Announcing the turn ahead of time (start time and \(\omega_c\) in the packet) lets every copy switch at the same instant; consensus then only corrects clock disagreement, which is far smaller than a whole turn-rate step.
Exercise 5 (formation feedback). With \(V_c=30\) m/s and a member limited to 28 m/s, what \(k_f\) keeps the steady lag at 10 m? What is the body's speed then?
Show solution
\(\varepsilon_{ss}=(V_{\max}-V_c)/k_f\Rightarrow k_f=2/10=0.2\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}\). The body settles at the member's limit, 28 m/s, regardless of \(k_f\); \(k_f\) only sets how far behind the member sits.
Exercise 6 (dead reckoning). A copy's turn-rate estimate is 0.5°/s too high at 60 m/s and no packet arrives for 3 s. Estimate the lateral slot error that accumulates.
Show solution
\(\tfrac12V\Delta\omega T^2=0.5\cdot60\cdot0.00873\cdot9=2.36\) m — acceptable for a 60 m spacing, not for a 10 m one.

13. Key Takeaways & Formula Sheet

  • Body state \(\xi=(p_c,\psi_c,V_c,\omega_c,h_c)\); \(\dot p_c=V_c\hat t(\psi_c)\), \(\dot\psi_c=\omega_c=g\tan\phi_c/V_c\) in turns.
  • \(\dot R(\psi)=\dot\psi R(\psi)J\), \(RJ=JR\), \(J^2=-I\).
  • Slot: \(p_i^d=p_c+Rr_i\); \(v_i^d=v_c+\omega_cRJr_i\); \(a_i^d=\dot V_c\hat t+\omega_cJv_c+\dot\omega_cRJr_i-\omega_c^2Rr_i\).
  • Slot speed \(V_i=\omega_cR_i=\sqrt{(V_c-\omega_cr_e)^2+(\omega_cr_n)^2}\); course offset \(\operatorname{atan2}(\omega_cr_n,V_c-\omega_cr_e)\); bank \(\arctan(V_i\omega_c/g)\).
  • Flyability: smallest positive root over slots of \(\|r_i\|^2\omega^2-2V_cr_{e,i}\omega+V_c^2-V_{\max/\min}^2=0\); the outermost/rearmost slot limits \(\phi_c\).
  • Synchronised structure = Part 8 estimator with the body model; rate \(k_\xi\lambda_{\min}(H)\); drift \(\tfrac12V\Delta\omega T^2\) between packets.
  • Formation feedback \(\dot p_c=v_c+k_f\sum\varepsilon_i\): body adapts to the slowest member, \(\varepsilon_{ss}=(V_{\max}-V_c)/k_f\); closes a loop through the reference.
