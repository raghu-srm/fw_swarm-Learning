1. Why This Matters

Consensus makes a swarm agree, but on a value nobody chose: the average, or a weighted average set by the topology (Part 7), drifting under any biased disturbance (Part 6 §8). A mission needs the opposite — every aircraft converging to a reference that a mission planner, a ground station or a lead aircraft dictates: a flight level, a speed, a formation centre, a turn. Leader–follower consensus adds one term to the protocol and changes everything about its behaviour:

\[ \dot x_i=\sum_{j\in\mathcal N_i^{\mathrm{in}}}w_{ij}(x_j-x_i)\;+\;\underbrace{b_i\,(x_0-x_i)}_{\text{pinning term}},\qquad b_i\gt0\ \text{only if UAV } i \text{ hears the leader.} \]

The questions are the same as before — does it converge, how fast, to what — but a new one dominates the engineering: which aircraft should hear the leader, and what happens when the leader moves. The answers lead directly to the estimator-plus-tracker structure that the capstone's control law uses for its reference layer.

2. Static Leader and the Matrix \(H=L+B\)

Let the leader hold a constant value \(x_0\) (a commanded flight level, say). Followers run the pinned protocol above. Stack the followers' states and define the pinning matrix \(B=\operatorname{diag}(b_1,\dots,b_N)\):

\[ \dot x=-Lx-B(x-x_0\mathbf 1)=-Lx-Bx+Bx_0\mathbf 1 . \]

With the tracking error \(e=x-x_0\mathbf 1\) and \(L\mathbf 1=0\) (so \(Lx=Le\)):

\[ \boxed{\;\dot e=-(L+B)\,e=-H\,e,\qquad H=L+B\;} \]

This is the whole theory in one line. Unlike \(L\), the matrix \(H\) has no zero eigenvalue in the useful case: the pinning term removes the consensus direction \(\mathbf 1\) from the kernel, because \(H\mathbf 1=B\mathbf 1=b\ne0\). The error then decays to zero rather than to a common value — the followers converge to the leader, not merely to each other.

Interpretation: \(L\) says "move toward your neighbours", \(B\) says "and the pinned aircraft also move toward the leader". Information about \(x_0\) enters at the pinned nodes and diffuses through the graph. If the graph is connected, everybody eventually feels it.

Vector states use \(\dot e=-(H\otimes I_n)e\); directed graphs use \(L=D_{\mathrm{in}}-W\) from Part 7 (Section 8 below).

3. When Is \(H\) Positive Definite?

Theorem (undirected graphs). Let the follower graph be undirected with Laplacian \(L\), and \(B=\operatorname{diag}(b)\) with \(b\ge0\). Then \(H=L+B\) is symmetric positive definite if and only if every connected component of the follower graph contains at least one pinned node (\(b_i\gt0\)). In particular, for a connected graph one pinned aircraft suffices. Then \(e(t)=e^{-Ht}e(0)\to0\) exponentially.
Proof. For any \(x\), \[ x^THx=x^TLx+x^TBx=\tfrac12\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}(x_i-x_j)^2+\sum_ib_ix_i^2\;\ge\;0 . \] Equality requires every edge difference to vanish — \(x\) constant on each component — and \(x_i=0\) at every pinned node. If each component has a pinned node, the constant on it must be zero, so \(x=0\): \(H\succ0\). Conversely, a component with no pinned node admits \(x=\) its indicator vector with \(x^THx=0\). \(\square\)

A random check of the identity on the path 1–2–3 with \(b=(1,0,0)\) gives \(x^THx=0.679086\) both ways.

The matrix \(H\) is often called a grounded or Dirichlet Laplacian: in the limit \(b_i\to\infty\) at the pinned nodes it becomes the Laplacian with those rows and columns deleted (the pinned nodes are "grounded" at \(x_0\)); Section 4 uses this limit.

4. Convergence Rate and the Pinning Bound

With \(H\succ0\) and symmetric, the same Lyapunov argument as Part 6 (\(V=\tfrac12e^Te\), \(\dot V=-e^THe\le-\lambda_{\min}(H)\|e\|^2\)) gives

\[ \boxed{\;\|e(t)\|\le e^{-\lambda_{\min}(H)\,t}\|e(0)\|,\qquad t_{1\%}=\frac{\ln100}{\lambda_{\min}(H)}\;} \]

So the design quantity is \(\lambda_{\min}(H)\), and it is small whenever few aircraft are pinned. The Rayleigh quotient at \(\mathbf 1\) gives an upper bound that no choice of graph can beat:

\[ \boxed{\;\lambda_{\min}(H)\le\frac{\mathbf 1^TH\mathbf 1}{\mathbf 1^T\mathbf 1}=\frac{\sum_ib_i}{N}\;} \]

because \(\mathbf 1^TL\mathbf 1=0\). Pinning one aircraft with gain 1 in a swarm of \(N\) caps the convergence rate at \(1/N\), however well connected the followers are. Two levers remain:

  • Pin more aircraft. The bound grows with \(\sum_ib_i\) and the actual \(\lambda_{\min}\) grows with it — usually the better lever (Section 5).
  • Raise \(b_i\). Helps, but saturates: as \(b_i\to\infty\) at the pinned set \(\mathcal P\), \(\lambda_{\min}(H)\to\lambda_{\min}(L_{\text{grounded}})\), the smallest eigenvalue of \(L\) with the rows and columns of \(\mathcal P\) removed (Pirani & Sundaram 2016). On the path 1–2–3 pinned at node 2 that limit is 1: \(\lambda_{\min}(H)=0.268,\ 0.821,\ 0.980,\ 0.998\) for \(b_2=1,10,100,1000\).

A useful corollary of the grounded limit: the rate is ultimately controlled by how far the unpinned aircraft are, in graph terms, from the pinned ones — the diffusion time through the follower graph.

6. Dynamic Leader: the Lag

Now let the reference move: \(\dot x_0=u_0(t)\), a climb at \(u_0=0.5\) m/s. With the same follower law, the error dynamics acquire a forcing term:

\[ \dot e=\dot x-\dot x_0\mathbf 1=-He-\mathbf 1u_0 . \]

For constant \(u_0\) the error settles not at zero but at the particular solution of \(He=-\mathbf 1u_0\):

\[ \boxed{\;e_{ss}=-H^{-1}\mathbf 1\,u_0\;} \]

The vector \(H^{-1}\mathbf 1\) deserves a name: its entries are lag times. UAV \(i\) sits \((H^{-1}\mathbf 1)_i\) seconds of leader motion behind the leader. Path 1–2–3:

pinned set\(H^{-1}\mathbf 1\) [s]\(e_{ss}\) for \(u_0=0.5\) m/s [m]simulated \(e(40\,\mathrm s)\)
UAV 1(3, 5, 6)(−1.5, −2.5, −3.0)(−1.502, −2.504, −3.005)
UAV 2(4, 3, 4)(−2.0, −1.5, −2.0)(−2.000, −1.500, −2.000)
all three(1, 1, 1)(−0.5, −0.5, −0.5)(−0.5, −0.5, −0.5)

With one pinned aircraft the last follower lags six seconds of leader motion. For a 0.5 m/s climb that is 3 m — tolerable. For a reference heading turning at \(0.1\) rad/s it would be \(0.6\) rad \(=34^\circ\), which is not (Section 9). The lag scales with \(u_0\) and with graph distance from the pinned set, and no finite gain removes it. Three cures:

  1. Feedforward of \(u_0\). If every follower knows the leader's rate — it is broadcast, or it is a known mission profile — add it: \(\dot x_i=\dots+u_0\). Then \(\dot e=-He\) again and \(e\to0\) (simulated \(e(40)=(-0.003,-0.005,-0.007)\), the tail of the initial transient). Requires the leader's input, not just its state.
  2. A distributed estimator of the leader state (Section 7) — the general solution, because the followers can run it at a high gain.
  3. Integral action on \(e_i\) — removes constant lag, adds a state per aircraft and an oscillatory mode; used in Part 12 for formation offsets but not pursued here.

7. Distributed Leader-State Estimator

The lag arises because the aircraft is doing two jobs with one gain: finding out what the leader is doing, and flying there. Separate them. Each UAV keeps a local estimate \(\hat\xi_i\) of the leader state and runs consensus on the estimates — a pure computation, with no aircraft dynamics in the loop — then tracks its own estimate with a vehicle-limited gain:

\[ \boxed{\; \begin{aligned} \dot{\hat\xi}_i&=u_0^{\text{model}}-k_\xi\Big[\sum_{j\in\mathcal N_i^{\mathrm{in}}}w_{ij}(\hat\xi_i-\hat\xi_j)+b_i(\hat\xi_i-x_0)\Big] && \text{(estimator: consensus on the reference)}\\ \dot x_i&=\dot{\hat\xi}_i-k_t\,(x_i-\hat\xi_i) && \text{(tracker: follow my own estimate)} \end{aligned}\;} \]

Here \(u_0^{\text{model}}\) is whatever the follower knows about the leader's motion: the broadcast rate, a mission profile, or simply zero. Stacking the estimation errors \(\eta=\hat\xi-x_0\mathbf 1\):

\[ \dot\eta=-k_\xi H\eta+(u_0^{\text{model}}-u_0)\mathbf 1\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \eta_{ss}=-\frac{H^{-1}\mathbf 1}{k_\xi}\,(u_0-u_0^{\text{model}}) . \]

Two gains instead of one. The estimator's lag is the tracker's lag of Section 6 divided by \(k_\xi\), and it vanishes altogether when the model is right. The tracker's error \(x_i-\hat\xi_i\) obeys a first-order lag at \(k_t\) driven by nothing but the initial condition, since the tracker feeds forward \(\dot{\hat\xi}_i\).

7.2 Why \(k_\xi\) can be large and \(k_t\) cannot

\(k_t\) multiplies a position error to produce a velocity command; it is limited by the aircraft's ability to realise that velocity — the autopilot bandwidth and the envelope (Parts 15–16). \(k_\xi\) multiplies a difference between two numbers in memory; nothing physical limits it. Its only limit is the discrete-time one of Part 6, at the communication period:

\[ T_{\mathrm{comm}}\,k_\xi\,\lambda_{\max}(H)\lt2\qquad\text{(aim}\ \le0.5\text{)} . \]

Path pinned at 1, \(T_{\mathrm{comm}}=0.1\) s, \(\lambda_{\max}(H)=3.247\): \(k_\xi\lt6.16\); the spectral radius of \(I-T_{\mathrm{comm}}k_\xi H\) is \(0.980,\ 0.901,\ 0.948\) for \(k_\xi=1,5,6\) and \(1.273\) (unstable) for \(k_\xi=7\). This is where a large pinning gain hurts: \(b_2=10\) gave \(\lambda_{\max}(H)=12.2\), which would cap \(k_\xi\) at 1.6.

7.3 The structure in the CLAW

Replace the scalar \(x_0\) by the virtual-structure state \(\xi=(p_c,\psi_c,V_c,\omega_c,h_c)\) of Part 13, and \(u_0^{\text{model}}\) by its kinematic model (\(\dot p_c=V_c(\cos\psi_c,\sin\psi_c)\), \(\dot\psi_c=\omega_c\)): that is layer L1 of the capstone. Replace the tracker by the formation guidance law of Part 12: that is layer L2. The model term is what lets every follower propagate the reference between packets and through packet loss, with consensus only correcting the drift.

8. Directed Graphs and the Augmented Tree

With one-way links use \(L=D_{\mathrm{in}}-W\) (Part 7). \(H=L+B\) is no longer symmetric, so "positive definite" is replaced by "\(-H\) is Hurwitz" (all eigenvalues of \(H\) in the open right half-plane), and the graph condition becomes the spanning-tree theorem applied to an enlarged graph:

Theorem (directed leader–follower). Add the leader as node 0 with transmit edges \(0\to i\) for every pinned \(i\). Then \(\dot e=-He\to0\) for all \(e(0)\) if and only if this augmented digraph has a directed spanning tree rooted at node 0.
Proof sketch. The augmented in-degree Laplacian is \(L_{\text{aug}}=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\-b&H\end{bmatrix}\) (node 0 hears nobody). Its eigenvalues are \(0\) together with those of \(H\). By Part 7, zero is a simple eigenvalue of \(L_{\text{aug}}\) iff the augmented digraph has a spanning tree; and a spanning tree must be rooted at 0 because 0 has no in-neighbours. A simple zero for \(L_{\text{aug}}\) means \(H\) has no zero eigenvalue, and Gershgorin places all its eigenvalues in \(\operatorname{Re}\lambda\ge0\); the same imaginary-axis argument as in Part 7 excludes \(\operatorname{Re}\lambda=0\). \(\square\)

The rule of thumb: pin the aircraft from which information flows outward. In a relay line that is the head of the line; pinning the tail is useless because nothing propagates upstream. Symmetry in the undirected case hid this completely (pinning either end of the path gave the same \(\lambda(H)\)).

9. Fixed-Wing Interpretation

9.1 Who the leader is

  • A lead aircraft broadcasting its state: \(x_0\) is its altitude, speed or (via Part 13) the formation reference derived from it; \(b_i\gt0\) for aircraft within its link.
  • The ground station commanding a flight level or a mission reference: pinned aircraft are those with the long-range link, often one or two.
  • A virtual leader: a reference trajectory computed on board the pinned aircraft (or on all of them from the same plan) — no aircraft has to fly it, which removes the single point of failure of a physical lead.

9.2 Heading references need the model term

Suppose the reference is a course \(\psi_c\) that turns at \(\omega_c=0.1\) rad/s (a 25 m/s aircraft at \(14^\circ\) bank). Followers running the estimator of Section 7 with \(k_\xi=2\), pin 1, but with no model term (\(u_0^{\text{model}}=0\)) lag by \(-H^{-1}\mathbf 1\,\omega_c/k_\xi=(0.15,\,0.25,\,0.30)\) rad \(=(8.6^\circ,\,14.3^\circ,\,17.2^\circ)\). A \(0.15\) rad course error held for 10 s at 25 m/s is \(25\times10\times\sin0.15\approx37\) m of cross-track drift — the formation would smear out in every turn. Propagating \(\hat\psi_{c,i}\) with \(\hat\omega_{c,i}\) between corrections (the model term) removes the lag entirely, and consensus then only has to agree on the turn rate, which changes rarely.

9.3 Stale leader data

A leader position report that is 100 ms old is 2.5 m stale at 25 m/s and 6 m at 60 m/s; 150 ms at 150 m/s is 22.5 m. Pinned aircraft should dead-reckon \(x_0\) forward by \(V\tau\) before using it in the pinning term (Part 24 §4.4), exactly as the model term does for the estimate.

9.4 Leader loss

If the leader disappears, \(B\to0\) and the followers revert to plain consensus (Part 7): they agree on \(\gamma^T\hat\xi\), the weighted average of their current estimates, and keep propagating it with the model — the swarm continues its last known reference. That is a graceful default if the estimates had converged; it is dangerous if they had not. The capstone (Part 24 §14) re-elects a root with a ground link to restore pinning.

High-speed note. With one pinned aircraft and \(k_\xi=1\) the estimator's 1 %-settling time on the 3-aircraft path is 23.3 s: 581 m at 25 m/s, 3.5 km at 150 m/s. A reference change announced that late is a formation that falls apart in the next turn. At high speed, therefore: pin at least two aircraft (both ends of a trail, or the centre of a vee); run \(k_\xi\) near the sampling margin (\(\approx0.5/(T_{\mathrm{comm}}\lambda_{\max}(H))\)); broadcast the future reference — turn start time, bank, speed — so that every copy switches simultaneously and consensus only corrects clock error; and never pin only the tail of a relay chain (Section 8).
Toward the CLAW This part supplies layer L1 of the unified law and the proof behind it. The estimator \(\dot{\hat\xi}_i=f(\hat\xi_i)-k_\xi[\sum_jw_{ij}(\hat\xi_i\ominus\hat\xi_j)+b_i(\hat\xi_i\ominus\xi)]\) in Part 24 §6 is Section 7 with the virtual-structure model as \(u_0^{\text{model}}\); its convergence rate \(k_\xi\lambda_{\min}(H)\) and residual \(\|\varepsilon\|/(k_\xi\lambda_{\min}(H))\) are Sections 4 and 7; condition C2, "\(\operatorname{Re}\lambda_{\min}(H)\gt0\)", is Section 3 (undirected) and Section 8 (directed); and check D4, "choose the pinned set for \(\lambda_{\min}(H)\) and announce turns early", is Sections 5 and 9. The split into a high-gain estimator and a vehicle-limited tracker is the single most important architectural decision in the capstone.

10. Implementation

Pure NumPy, Euler integration at 1 ms. Reproduces the pinning table, the static and dynamic-leader runs, the estimator-plus-tracker, and the directed chain.

import numpy as np
np.set_printoptions(precision=4, suppress=True)

# Undirected path 1-2-3 (course convention A[i,j]>0 <=> i transmits to j; W = A^T)
A = np.array([[0, 1, 0], [1, 0, 1], [0, 1, 0]], float)
W = A.T
L = np.diag(W.sum(axis=1)) - W

def pinned_H(b):
    """H = L + B for pinning gains b (b_i > 0 <=> UAV i hears the leader)."""
    return L + np.diag(b)

def euler(f, z0, T, dt=1e-3):
    z, t = z0.astype(float).copy(), 0.0
    for _ in range(int(round(T / dt))):
        z += dt * f(t, z); t += dt
    return z

x_init = np.array([100.0, 140.0, 80.0])   # initial altitudes [m]
x0 = 120.0                                 # static leader altitude [m]

print("Which UAV to pin?")
for name, b in (("pin 1", [1, 0, 0]), ("pin 2", [0, 1, 0]), ("pin all", [1, 1, 1])):
    H = pinned_H(b)
    lam = np.linalg.eigvalsh(H)
    print(f"  {name}: eig(H) = {lam}, 1%-settling ln(100)/lambda_min = {np.log(100)/lam[0]:.2f} s,"
          f" bound sum(b)/N = {sum(b)/3:.3f}")

# 1. static leader: x' = -L x - B (x - x0 1)  ->  e' = -H e
H = pinned_H([1, 0, 0])
f_static = lambda t, x: -H @ (x - x0)
for T in (10.0, 20.0):
    print(f"static leader, pin 1, t={T:.0f}: x =", euler(f_static, x_init, T))

# 2. dynamic leader x0(t) = 120 + u0 t with the SAME law -> steady lag -H^-1 1 u0
u0 = 0.5
f_dyn = lambda t, x: -H @ (x - (x0 + u0 * t))
x40 = euler(f_dyn, x_init, 40.0)
print("dynamic leader, no feedforward, t=40: e =", x40 - (x0 + u0 * 40.0),
      " predicted e_ss =", -np.linalg.solve(H, np.ones(3)) * u0)

# 3. distributed leader-state estimator + local tracker (the CLAW structure)
k_xi, k_t, u0_model = 2.0, 1.0, 0.5           # try u0_model = 0.0 to see the residual lag
def f_est(t, z):
    xi_hat, x = z[:3], z[3:]
    xi0 = x0 + u0 * t
    xi_hat_dot = u0_model - k_xi * (H @ (xi_hat - xi0))   # = L xi_hat + B (xi_hat - xi0)
    x_dot = xi_hat_dot - k_t * (x - xi_hat)              # track my own estimate
    return np.concatenate([xi_hat_dot, x_dot])
z0 = np.concatenate([x_init, x_init])
for T in (10.0, 20.0):
    z = euler(f_est, z0, T)
    xi0 = x0 + u0 * T
    print(f"estimator+tracker, t={T:.0f}: estimate error = {z[:3] - xi0}, altitude error = {z[3:] - xi0}")
print("slowest estimator rate k_xi*lambda_min(H) =", k_xi * np.linalg.eigvalsh(H)[0])

# 4. directed chain 1->2->3 with the leader heard by UAV 1 only, or by UAV 3 only
A_chain = np.array([[0, 1, 0], [0, 0, 1], [0, 0, 0]], float)
Wc = A_chain.T; Lc = np.diag(Wc.sum(axis=1)) - Wc
for name, b in (("leader->1", [1, 0, 0]), ("leader->3", [0, 0, 1])):
    Hc = Lc + np.diag(b)
    print(f"directed chain, {name}: eig(H) = {np.sort_complex(np.linalg.eigvals(Hc))}, det = {np.linalg.det(Hc):.2f}")

11. Common Pitfalls

  • Pinning by convenience, not by graph position. The aircraft with the best radio is not necessarily the one that minimises the followers' graph distance to the leader. Compute \(\lambda_{\min}(H)\) for the candidates; it costs nothing.
  • Raising \(b_i\) instead of pinning more aircraft. \(\lambda_{\min}(H)\) saturates at the grounded Laplacian's, while \(\lambda_{\max}(H)\) grows without bound and eats the sampling margin.
  • Expecting zero error behind a moving leader. The lag \(-H^{-1}\mathbf 1u_0\) is structural; remove it with a model/feedforward term or the estimator, not with gain.
  • One gain for two jobs. Using the vehicle tracking gain to also chase the reference couples estimator lag to aircraft dynamics. Split estimator (\(k_\xi\), high) and tracker (\(k_t\), vehicle-limited).
  • Pinning the tail of a directed chain. Check the augmented spanning tree; symmetry intuition fails on digraphs.
  • Differencing heading estimates without wrapping. \(\hat\psi_{c,i}-\hat\psi_{c,j}\) must be wrapped to \((-\pi,\pi]\) or a \(359^\circ\) vs \(1^\circ\) disagreement sends the estimator the long way round.
  • Forgetting that leader loss turns \(H\) back into \(L\). Followers will still agree — on their own weighted average — and keep flying it; make sure that is acceptable or re-pin quickly.

12. Exercises

Exercise 1 (pinning a 4-aircraft trail). Undirected path 1–2–3–4. Compare pinning UAV 1, UAV 2, UAVs 2 and 3, and UAVs 1 and 4: \(\lambda_{\min}(H)\), the Rayleigh bound, and the lag-time vector \(H^{-1}\mathbf 1\).
Show solution
pin 1: \(\lambda_{\min}=0.121\) (bound 0.25), \(H^{-1}\mathbf 1=(4,7,9,10)\) s; pin 2: \(0.173\) (0.25), \((5,4,6,7)\); pin 2 and 3: \(0.382\) (0.5), \((3,2,2,3)\); pin 1 and 4: \(0.382\) (0.5), \((2,3,3,2)\). Two pinned aircraft triple the rate and cut the worst lag from 10 s to 3 s; the two choices with two pins tie on \(\lambda_{\min}\) but distribute the lag differently (ends vs centre).
Exercise 2 (grounded limit). On the path 1–2–3 pinned at UAV 2, compute \(\lambda_{\min}(H)\) for \(b_2=1,10,100,1000\) and identify the limit from the grounded Laplacian.
Show solution
\(0.268,\ 0.821,\ 0.980,\ 0.998\). Deleting node 2 leaves two isolated nodes, each with grounded Laplacian entry \(d_i=1\); the limit is \(\lambda_{\min}=1\): with UAV 2 nailed to \(x_0\), UAVs 1 and 3 each see a single edge of weight 1 to a fixed value, a first-order lag with rate 1.
Exercise 3 (lag budget). The leader climbs at \(2\) m/s and the followers have no model of it. With pin 1 on the path 1–2–3 and \(T_{\mathrm{comm}}=0.1\) s, what \(k_\xi\) keeps every estimator lag below \(0.5\) m, and is it admissible?
Show solution
Lag \(=H^{-1}\mathbf 1\,u_0/k_\xi=(3,5,6)\cdot2/k_\xi\); the worst is \(12/k_\xi\le0.5\Rightarrow k_\xi\ge24\). The Euler limit is \(2/(0.1\cdot3.247)=6.16\), so no: at \(k_\xi=6\) the lag is still 2 m. Either supply the model term (then the lag is zero at any \(k_\xi\)) or pin more aircraft (pin all: \(H^{-1}\mathbf 1=(1,1,1)\), lag \(2/k_\xi\), fine at \(k_\xi=4\)).
Exercise 4 (directed fix). Chain \(1\to2\to3\) with the leader heard only by UAV 2 does not converge. Add a single follower link that fixes it, write \(H\), and give its eigenvalues.
Show solution
Add \(2\to1\) (UAV 2 transmits back to UAV 1). \(L=\begin{bmatrix}1&-1&0\\-1&1&0\\0&-1&1\end{bmatrix}\), \(H=L+\operatorname{diag}(0,1,0)=\begin{bmatrix}1&-1&0\\-1&2&0\\0&-1&1\end{bmatrix}\), \(\lambda(H)=\{0.382,1,2.618\}\), \(\det H=1\); augmented tree \(0\to2\), \(2\to1\), \(2\to3\). Adding \(3\to1\) instead would also work (\(0\to2\to3\to1\)) but with a longer relay.
Exercise 5 (the pinning bound). Prove \(\lambda_{\min}(H)\le\sum_ib_i/N\) and show that equality holds iff \(\mathbf 1\) is an eigenvector of \(H\). When is that the case?
Show solution
For symmetric \(H\), \(\lambda_{\min}\le x^THx/x^Tx\) for every \(x\ne0\) (Rayleigh); take \(x=\mathbf 1\): \(\mathbf 1^TH\mathbf 1=\mathbf 1^TL\mathbf 1+\mathbf 1^TB\mathbf 1=0+\sum b_i\). Equality in the Rayleigh bound holds iff \(x\) is an eigenvector for \(\lambda_{\min}\); \(H\mathbf 1=b\), so \(\mathbf 1\) is an eigenvector iff \(b=c\mathbf 1\) — all aircraft pinned with equal gain — and then \(\lambda_{\min}(H)=c\) exactly ("pin all" row of the table: \(\lambda_{\min}=1=\sum b_i/3\)).

13. Key Takeaways & Formula Sheet

  • Pinned protocol \(\dot x_i=\sum_jw_{ij}(x_j-x_i)+b_i(x_0-x_i)\); error \(e=x-x_0\mathbf 1\) obeys \(\dot e=-He\), \(H=L+B\).
  • Undirected: \(H\succ0\) iff every component has a pinned node (\(x^THx=\tfrac12\sum a_{ij}(x_i-x_j)^2+\sum b_ix_i^2\)). Directed: \(-H\) Hurwitz iff the augmented graph has a spanning tree rooted at the leader.
  • Rate \(\lambda_{\min}(H)\); \(\|e(t)\|\le e^{-\lambda_{\min}(H)t}\|e(0)\|\); bound \(\lambda_{\min}(H)\le\sum b_i/N\); \(b\to\infty\) limit = grounded Laplacian.
  • Moving leader \(\dot x_0=u_0\): steady lag \(e_{ss}=-H^{-1}\mathbf 1u_0\); \(H^{-1}\mathbf 1\) = lag times in seconds.
  • Estimator + tracker: \(\dot{\hat\xi}_i=u_0^{\text{model}}-k_\xi[\sum_jw_{ij}(\hat\xi_i-\hat\xi_j)+b_i(\hat\xi_i-x_0)]\), \(\dot x_i=\dot{\hat\xi}_i-k_t(x_i-\hat\xi_i)\); residual \(\eta_{ss}=-H^{-1}\mathbf 1(u_0-u_0^{\text{model}})/k_\xi\); \(k_\xi\) limited only by \(T_{\mathrm{comm}}k_\xi\lambda_{\max}(H)\lt2\).
  • Pin where information flows outward; pin more aircraft rather than raising \(b\); propagate heading references with the model term; dead-reckon stale leader data.
