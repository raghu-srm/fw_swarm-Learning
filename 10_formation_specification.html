1. Why This Matters

Parts 5–9 taught the swarm to agree: states converge to a common value. A formation is the opposite request — the aircraft must hold prescribed differences. Before any formation control law (Part 12) can be written, the formation itself must be specified in a form the law can consume: which quantity each aircraft should hold with respect to which neighbour, in which frame, and whether the resulting set of constraints is even self-consistent. This lesson is that specification step.

\[ \text{Graph topology} \rightarrow \text{consensus} \rightarrow \textbf{formation specification} \rightarrow \text{formation control} \rightarrow \text{vehicle guidance} \rightarrow \text{autopilot} \]
InputConsensus on vector states and the Kronecker form (Part 5), leader–follower structure (Part 8), second-order dynamics (Part 9), the planar rotation \(R(\psi)\) and \(J\) (A1).
OutputYou can write any formation as slot vectors \(r_i\), derive consistent displacements \(d_{ij}\), choose between displacement / distance / bearing specifications, and compute whether a formation of fixed-wing aircraft can be held through a turn.
Used inPart 11 (distance specifications need rigidity), Part 12 (control laws consume \(d_{ij}\)), Part 13 (the moving formation frame), Part 19 (path-based formations), Part 22 (slot assignment).

The engineering problem is concrete. Five aircraft are to fly a vee at 25 m/s and later the same vee at 150 m/s. The lead turns. Every aircraft on the outside of the turn must fly faster than the lead, every aircraft on the inside slower, and each has its own bank angle. Whether the formation survives the turn is decided by arithmetic on the speed envelope and the turn radius — arithmetic we derive in Section 5 — not by the control law.

2. A Formation Is a Shape Modulo a Group

Definition (configuration, formation). The configuration of \(N\) aircraft is the stacked vector \(p=\operatorname{col}(p_1,\dots,p_N)\in\mathbb R^{nN}\), \(n=2\) or \(3\). A formation specification is a target configuration \(p^\ast\) together with a group \(\mathcal G\) of transformations that the designer regards as irrelevant. The formation is the set \[ \mathcal F=\{\,(\mathrm g(p_1^\ast),\dots,\mathrm g(p_N^\ast)) : \mathrm g\in\mathcal G\,\}, \] and "achieving the formation" means driving \(p\) into \(\mathcal F\), not to \(p^\ast\) itself.

The choice of \(\mathcal G\) is a sensing decision as much as a geometric one. Three choices cover almost all of the literature (the survey by Oh, Park & Ahn 2015 is organised around exactly this trichotomy):

  1. Displacement-based. \(\mathcal G\) = translations. The invariants are the relative positions \(p_i-p_j\) expressed in a common frame. Constraint: \(p_i-p_j\to d_{ij}\). Each aircraft must know where its neighbour is in a frame whose orientation both share — GPS positions plus a common north, or a shared heading reference.
  2. Distance-based. \(\mathcal G\) = translations + rotations (+ reflections). The invariants are inter-aircraft distances. Constraint: \(\|p_i-p_j\|\to d_{ij}\) (here \(d_{ij}\) is a scalar). Only ranges are needed, or relative positions in each aircraft's own body frame, which makes this the natural choice when there is no common heading reference. The price is that the constraints must be rigid — Part 11.
  3. Bearing-based. \(\mathcal G\) = translations + uniform scaling. The invariants are the unit vectors \((p_j-p_i)/\|p_j-p_i\|\) in a common frame. Constraint: bearings to neighbours. Needs a direction sensor (camera, radar) and a common orientation; the overall size of the formation is left free and must be fixed by one measured distance.

Counting constraints

In the plane the configuration space has \(2N\) dimensions and the group removes \(\dim\mathcal G\) of them, so a specification needs \(2N-\dim\mathcal G\) independent scalar constraints to pin the shape down:

\[ \text{displacement: } 2N-2,\qquad \text{distance: } 2N-3,\qquad \text{bearing: } 2N-3 . \]

Each displacement constraint \(p_i-p_j=d_{ij}\) is a 2-vector, so \(N-1\) of them suffice — exactly the edges of a spanning tree (Section 3). Each distance or planar bearing is one scalar, so \(2N-3\) are needed, and "independent" is the hard part: that is the rigidity question of Part 11.

Caution: "distance-based" also has a sign ambiguity. A reflected formation satisfies every distance constraint, so a wingman that starts on the wrong side can converge to the mirror slot (Part 11, Section 5). Displacement specifications have no such ambiguity.

3. Slot Vectors and Displacement Consistency

Definition (formation frame, slot vectors). The formation frame \(\mathcal F_c\) has origin \(p_c\in\mathbb R^2\), \(x\)-axis along the structure heading \(\psi_c\) (clockwise from north) and \(y\)-axis to starboard. The slot vector \(r_i=(x_i,y_i)^T\) is the constant position of slot \(i\) in \(\mathcal F_c\). The desired inertial position of UAV \(i\) is \[ \boxed{\,p_i^d=p_c+R(\psi_c)\,r_i\,},\qquad R(\psi)=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\psi&-\sin\psi\\ \sin\psi&\cos\psi\end{bmatrix}, \] acting on (north, east) components. Check: \(R(\psi)e_1=(\cos\psi,\sin\psi)^T\) is the unit vector along the heading and \(R(\psi)e_2=(-\sin\psi,\cos\psi)^T\) points to starboard (for \(\psi=0\) it is east, i.e. the right of north).

The desired displacement between two aircraft follows by subtraction and is independent of \(p_c\):

\[ \boxed{\,d_{ij}=p_i^d-p_j^d=R(\psi_c)\,(r_i-r_j)\,}\qquad\text{(in the formation frame: } d^{\mathcal F}_{ij}=r_i-r_j\text{)}. \]

Because the \(d_{ij}\) are differences of a single table of \(r_i\), they automatically satisfy two identities: antisymmetry \(d_{ij}=-d_{ji}\) and the cycle (triangle) identity \(d_{ij}+d_{jk}=d_{ik}\). The converse is the useful statement.

Theorem (consistency of a displacement specification). Let \(G\) be a connected undirected graph on \(N\) nodes and let vectors \(d_{ij}\), \((i,j)\in E\), with \(d_{ji}=-d_{ij}\) be given. There exist slot vectors \(r_1,\dots,r_N\) with \(r_i-r_j=d_{ij}\) on every edge if and only if the sum of the \(d\)'s around every cycle of \(G\) is zero. The \(r_i\) are then unique up to a common translation, and the values of \(d_{ij}\) on any spanning tree of \(G\) determine all the others.
Proof. (Only if) Along a cycle \(i_1\to i_2\to\cdots\to i_m\to i_1\) the sum \(\sum_k (r_{i_k}-r_{i_{k+1}})\) telescopes to zero. (If) Fix \(r_1=0\). For any node \(i\) take a path \(1=i_0,i_1,\dots,i_m=i\) and set \(r_{i_{k+1}}=r_{i_k}-d_{i_ki_{k+1}}\). Two paths to the same node differ by a cycle, whose sum vanishes, so \(r_i\) is well defined; by the same argument every non-tree edge is satisfied. If \(r\) and \(r'\) both work, \(r_i-r'_i=r_j-r'_j\) on every edge, hence constant over the connected graph: a translation. \(\square\)

In matrix form, with the incidence matrix \(B\in\mathbb R^{|E|\times N}\) (row \(e=(i,j)\) has \(+1\) in column \(i\), \(-1\) in column \(j\)), the specification reads \((B\otimes I_2)\,r=d\) and consistency is \(d\in\operatorname{range}(B\otimes I_2)\). When a specification is not consistent — typically because pairwise offsets were tuned independently — no configuration satisfies it, and the best one can do is least squares:

\[ \min_r\ \sum_{(i,j)\in E}\|(r_i-r_j)-d_{ij}\|^2 \;\Longrightarrow\; (B^TB\otimes I_2)\,r=(B^T\otimes I_2)\,d,\qquad B^TB=L . \]

The Laplacian appears, and the minimiser is unique up to translation exactly when \(G\) is connected (\(\lambda_2\gt0\), Part 4). Part 12 shows that the displacement-based controller is the gradient flow of this very cost, so an inconsistent specification does not make the swarm unstable — it quietly converges to the least-squares compromise, which is rarely what the designer drew. Exercise 3 works a numeric instance.

Who stores what on board

In practice no aircraft stores \(d_{ij}\). Every aircraft carries the same small table of slot vectors \(\{r_1,\dots,r_N\}\) and its own estimate \(\hat\psi_c\) of the structure heading (from the leader-state estimator of Part 8), and computes \(d_{ij}=R(\hat\psi_c)(r_i-r_j)\) for its in-neighbours \(j\in\mathcal N_i^{\mathrm{in}}\) on the fly. If two aircraft hold different \(\hat\psi_c\), their \(d_{ij}\) and \(d_{ji}\) are no longer antisymmetric — an inconsistency that consensus on \(\hat\psi_c\) removes but that a designer must know exists.

Rotating formations

When the structure turns, the inertial displacements rotate. Using \(\frac{d}{dt}R(\psi)=\dot\psi R(\psi)J\) with \(J=\begin{bmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{bmatrix}\):

\[ \boxed{\,\dot d_{ij}=\omega_c\,R(\psi_c)\,J\,(r_i-r_j)\,},\qquad \|\dot d_{ij}\|=|\omega_c|\,\|r_i-r_j\| . \]

For the vee of Figure 2 in the 25 m/s turn of Section 5 (\(\omega_c=0.227\) rad/s), lead and slot 5 are \(\|r_1-r_5\|=144\) m apart, so their relative inertial velocity is \(0.227\times144=32.7\) m/s. A controller that ignores \(\dot d_{ij}\) must generate that relative velocity from position error alone — the lag computed in Part 12.

5. Formation Feasibility in Coordinated Turns

Let the structure fly a steady coordinated level turn: constant speed \(V_c\) and constant heading rate \(\dot\psi_c=\omega_c\), with \(\omega_c\gt0\) a right turn. Then \(p_c\) traces a circle of radius \(R_c=V_c/|\omega_c|\), and the coordinated-turn relation (Part 16) links the structure's bank to its turn rate: \(\tan\phi_c=V_c\omega_c/g\), \(R_c=V_c^2/(g\tan\phi_c)\). In the formation frame the turn centre sits at \(c=(0,\ R_c)^T\) for a right turn (starboard) and \(c=(0,-R_c)^T\) for a left turn.

Step 1 — slot velocity

Differentiate \(p_i^d=p_c+R(\psi_c)r_i\) with constant \(r_i\), using \(\dot R=\omega_cRJ\) and \(\dot p_c=V_cR(\psi_c)e_1\):

\[ v_i^d=\dot p_c+\omega_c R(\psi_c)Jr_i=R(\psi_c)\big[V_ce_1+\omega_cJr_i\big] =R(\psi_c)\begin{bmatrix}V_c-\omega_c y_i\\ \omega_c x_i\end{bmatrix}, \]

because \(Jr_i=J(x_i,y_i)^T=(-y_i,x_i)^T\). The bracket is the slot velocity expressed in the formation frame: forward component \(V_c-\omega_cy_i\), starboard component \(\omega_cx_i\).

Step 2 — slot speed

\[ V_i=\|v_i^d\|=\sqrt{(V_c-\omega_cy_i)^2+(\omega_cx_i)^2}=|\omega_c|\sqrt{x_i^2+(R_c-y_i)^2}=|\omega_c|\,\rho_i,\qquad \rho_i=\|r_i-c\| . \]
Interpretation: a rigid formation in a steady turn is a rigid body rotating about the turn centre. Every slot moves on its own circle of radius \(\rho_i\) (its distance from the turn centre) at the common angular rate \(\omega_c\), hence at speed \(\omega_c\rho_i\). Outside slots are faster, inside slots slower, in exact proportion to their radius: \(V_i/V_c=\rho_i/R_c\).

Step 3 — slot course

The slot velocity is tangent to its own circle, so its course differs from the structure heading:

\[ \chi_i=\psi_c+\beta_i,\qquad \beta_i=\operatorname{atan2}\!\big(\omega_cx_i,\ V_c-\omega_cy_i\big) . \]

For a trailing slot (\(x_i\lt0\)) in a right turn \(\beta_i\lt0\): the aircraft points left of the lead's heading, by exactly the angle it is "behind" around the turn centre. It is flying the heading the lead had \(|\beta_i|/\omega_c\) seconds ago — the formation is rigid, not the headings.

Step 4 — bank and load factor of each slot

Slot \(i\) flies a circle of radius \(\rho_i\) at speed \(V_i\), so its centripetal acceleration is \(V_i^2/\rho_i=\omega_c^2\rho_i\). In a coordinated level turn this is supplied by \(g\tan\phi_i\):

\[ \boxed{\,\tan\phi_i=\frac{V_i\,\omega_c}{g}=\frac{\omega_c^2\rho_i}{g}=\tan\phi_c\,\frac{\rho_i}{R_c}\,},\qquad n_{z,i}=\frac1{\cos\phi_i}. \]

Step 5 — feasibility

Slot \(i\) can be held by a fixed-wing aircraft with envelope \([V_{\min},V_{\max}]\) and bank limit \(\phi_{\max}\) only if

\[ \boxed{\,V_{\min}\le|\omega_c|\rho_i\le V_{\max}\quad\text{and}\quad \rho_i\le\frac{g\tan\phi_{\max}}{\omega_c^2}\,} \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad \frac{V_{\min}}{V_c}\le\frac{\rho_i}{R_c}\le\min\!\Big(\frac{V_{\max}}{V_c},\ \frac{\tan\phi_{\max}}{\tan\phi_c}\Big). \]

The pure-lateral case

For a slot directly abeam the lead (\(x_i=0\)) write \(y_i^{\mathrm{out}}\) for the lateral offset measured toward the outside of the turn (\(y^{\mathrm{out}}_i=-y_i\) in a right turn, \(+y_i\) in a left turn). Then \(\rho_i=R_c+y_i^{\mathrm{out}}\) and

\[ V_i=V_c\Big(1+\frac{y_i^{\mathrm{out}}}{R_c}\Big),\qquad \boxed{\,-R_c\Big(1-\frac{V_{\min}}{V_c}\Big)\ \le\ y_i^{\mathrm{out}}\ \le\ R_c\Big(\frac{V_{\max}}{V_c}-1\Big)\,},\qquad \tan\phi_i=\tan\phi_c\Big(1+\frac{y_i^{\mathrm{out}}}{R_c}\Big). \]

Designing the turn to fit the formation

Invert the window. For a symmetric formation of half-width \(y_{\max}\) that must turn both ways, the pure-lateral bound requires

\[ R_c\ \ge\ R_{c,\mathrm{req}}=\frac{y_{\max}}{\min\!\big(1-V_{\min}/V_c,\ V_{\max}/V_c-1\big)},\qquad \phi_c\ \le\ \arctan\frac{V_c^2}{g\,R_{c,\mathrm{req}}} . \]

With \(y_{\max}=80\) m at 25 m/s the binding ratio is 0.280 (outside), so \(R_{c,\mathrm{req}}=286\) m and \(\phi_c\le12.6^\circ\). With \(y_{\max}=500\) m at 150 m/s the binding ratio is 0.200 (inside), so \(R_{c,\mathrm{req}}=2500\) m and \(\phi_c\le42.5^\circ\). Equivalently, for a given radius the structure speed must lie in \(V_{\min}/(1-y_{\max}/R_c)\le V_c\le V_{\max}/(1+y_{\max}/R_c)\): at \(R_c=2500\) m and \(y_{\max}=500\) m this is \(150\le V_c\le158\) m/s — a 150 m/s structure sits at the bottom of its own window.

Along-track offsets are not negligible at low speed

Expanding \(\rho_i/R_c=\sqrt{(1-y_i/R_c)^2+(x_i/R_c)^2}\) for small offsets,

\[ \frac{V_i}{V_c}\approx1-\frac{y_i}{R_c}+\frac{x_i^2}{2R_c^2}\qquad(|x_i|,|y_i|\ll R_c), \]

so the along-track offset enters only at second order and always increases the required speed. At 150 m/s the rear slots sit at \(x_i/R_c=0.35\) and the correction is modest (slot 4 of the vee: exact 128 m/s, expansion 126 m/s, lateral-only 117 m/s). At 25 m/s the same slot has \(x_i/R_c=1.09\) and the expansion is useless (exact 28.0 m/s, expansion 21.7 m/s, lateral-only 6.88 m/s): the rear-inside aircraft is actually further from the turn centre than the lead. Use the exact \(\rho_i\) — the worked example does.

Caution: a "trail" slot \(r_i=(-x,0)\) in a rigid formation is not on the lead's path. It flies a larger circle, \(\rho_i=\sqrt{x^2+R_c^2}\), faster than the lead (60 m behind at 25 m/s and 30° bank: 28.5 m/s, 13.8 % faster, course lagging 28.5°; 400 m behind at 150 m/s and 45°: 152 m/s, 1.5 % faster, lag 9.9°). A trail that follows the same path with along-path spacing needs no speed difference at all — that is a path-based formation, Part 19, and the reason it is preferred at high speed.

6. Time-Varying Formations, Reconfiguration and Spacing

Time-varying slots

Nothing above requires the \(r_i\) to be constant. With smooth \(r_i(t)\) the slot velocity acquires one extra term, \(v_i^d=\dot p_c+\omega_cR(\psi_c)Jr_i+R(\psi_c)\dot r_i\), and the displacement derivative becomes \(\dot d_{ij}=\omega_cRJ(r_i-r_j)+R(\dot r_i-\dot r_j)\). Morphing between two catalogue shapes is a linear interpolation \(r_i(t)=(1-\sigma(t))r_i^A+\sigma(t)r_i^B\) with a smooth \(\sigma:0\to1\); the slot speed \(\|\dot r_i\|\) must stay inside the aircraft's spare speed margin. Moving the outer slot from line abreast \((0,-80)\) to the vee \((-120,-80)\) in 20 s costs 6.0 m/s of along-track speed difference — a large fraction of a 25 m/s aircraft's envelope, trivial for a 150 m/s one.

Reconfiguration is an assignment problem

The specification says what the slots are, not which aircraft fills which. When the formation changes, or a member is lost, the swarm must solve

\[ \min_{\pi\in S_N}\ \sum_{i=1}^N c_{i\pi(i)},\qquad c_{ik}=\|p_i-p_k^d\|\ \text{ (or the Dubins length / flight time to the slot, Part 17)}, \]

the linear assignment problem: the Hungarian method solves it centrally in \(O(N^3)\) (Kuhn 1955); the auction and CBBA algorithms of Part 22 solve it in a distributed way. Two warnings. First, min-sum and min-max (bottleneck) assignments can differ — Exercise 4 has both. Second, the guarantee that a minimum-sum-of-squared-distance assignment yields collision-free straight-line transfers (Turpin, Michael & Kumar 2014) is for holonomic agents and does not transfer to aircraft that cannot fly straight to a slot behind them; aircraft reconfigure with Dubins manoeuvres and altitude offsets.

Minimum spacing

The spacings \(s_a,s_c\) are not free either. A defensible lower bound is

\[ s_{\min}\ \ge\ d_s+2\varepsilon_{\mathrm{GPS}}+V\,\tau+\text{wake margin}, \]

where \(d_s\) is the safety distance of the collision filter (Part 20), \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{GPS}}\) the per-aircraft position error (metres for standalone GNSS, centimetres with RTK), and \(V\tau\) the distance an aircraft flies during one communication latency \(\tau\) — the position a neighbour reports is that old. Latency times speed: 2.5 m at 25 m/s and 100 ms; 6.0 m at 60 m/s; 15 m at 150 m/s; 22.5 m at 150 m/s and 150 ms; 25 m at 250 m/s and 100 ms. With \(d_s=30\) m, \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{GPS}}=5\) m and 2.5 m of latency the small-aircraft spacing must exceed 42.5 m — the 40 m cross-track spacing of the catalogue is at the limit and relies on the altitude offsets of Section 8. At 150 m/s with \(d_s=150\) m the same budget gives 182.5 m, comfortably below the 250 m used. Wake-vortex spacing is aircraft-specific; keep wingmen outside and above the lead's wake unless formation flight for drag reduction is the goal (Pachter, D'Azzo & Proud 2001).

8. Fixed-Wing Interpretation

  • Minimum speed. A slot whose required speed \(\omega_c\rho_i\) drops below \(V_{\min}\) cannot be held by slowing down; the aircraft must S-turn or the structure must widen the turn. The window \(V_{\min}/(1-y_{\max}/R_c)\le V_c\le V_{\max}/(1+y_{\max}/R_c)\) is the design statement.
  • Turn-rate limit. All slots share \(\omega_c\), so the structure's turn rate is limited by the outermost slot: \(\tan\phi_{\mathrm{out}}=\tan\phi_c\,\rho_{\max}/R_c\le\tan\phi_{\max}\). With \(\phi_{\max}=45^\circ\) and \(\phi_c=30^\circ\) the bank limit allows \(\rho/R_c\) up to 1.73, so in both presets the speed envelope (1.27–1.28) binds before the bank limit.
  • Speed envelope. The feasible lateral window is \(R_c\) times the envelope ratios; the ratios are what the airframe gives you, \(R_c\) is what the mission planner chooses.
  • Latency × speed. Every reported neighbour position is \(V\tau\) stale (2.5 m at 25 m/s, 22.5 m at 150 m/s with 150 ms); spacing and \(d_s\) must absorb it, and dead-reckoning the neighbour forward by \(v_j\tau\) recovers most of it.
  • Altitude as a cheap de-confliction axis. A vertical offset table \(\Delta h_i\) (for instance 0, +10, −10, +20, −20 m for the five slots) costs almost nothing in energy, keeps the formation picture unchanged in plan view, and turns a lateral near-miss during a saturated turn into a vertical separation. Specify \(\Delta h_i\) with the \(r_i\).
High-speed note. At fixed bank \(R_c\propto V_c^2\), so a formation of fixed metric size occupies a ratio \(y/R_c\propto1/V_c^2\) of the turn radius — turns become relatively gentler. Three things nevertheless get harder as \(V_c\) rises. (i) The envelope ratios shrink for an aircraft operating near its ceiling or its maximum speed, and with it the window. (ii) Changing speed is slow at high speed (specific excess power, Part 16), so the speed redistribution at turn entry — every slot must change speed by \(V_c(\rho_i/R_c-1)\) within the roll-in time — lags, and the formation distorts transiently even when the steady turn is feasible. (iii) Load factor: \(n_{z,i}=1/\cos\phi_i\) with \(\tan\phi_i=\tan\phi_c\rho_i/R_c\) grows for the outer slots (1.61 at the outer-rear slot of the 150 m/s vee). The remedies are to keep \(\rho_i/R_c\) close to 1 — lateral offsets small relative to \(R_c\), i.e. shrink \(y/R_c\) as speed rises — and to prefer altitude stacking and along-path (trail) formations, where no speed difference is needed at all (Part 19).
Toward the CLAW This lesson supplies the formation table \(\{(r_i,\Delta h_i)\}_{i=1}^N\) and the two slot functions \(p_i^d=p_c+R(\psi_c)r_i\) and \(v_i^d=\dot p_c+\omega_cR(\psi_c)Jr_i\) that the formation-guidance block (Part 12, layer L2 of the course simulator) evaluates on every aircraft. It consumes the virtual-structure state \(\xi=(p_c,\psi_c,V_c,\omega_c,h_c)\) — estimated locally by the leader-state estimator of Part 8 and defined in Part 13 — and produces the slot position, slot velocity and commanded altitude \(h_i^c=h_c+\Delta h_i\) for UAV \(i\), plus the desired displacements \(d_{ij}=R(\hat\psi_c)(r_i-r_j)\) for its in-neighbours. It also supplies the turn-feasibility check \(V_{\min}\le\omega_c\rho_i\le V_{\max}\), \(\phi_i\le\phi_{\max}\), which the reference generator uses to bound \(\omega_c\) (and thus \(\phi_c\)) before the structure is allowed to turn, and the slot-assignment interface \(c_{ik}\) that Part 22 fills.

9. Implementation

Pure NumPy, 65 lines; reproduces every number in Section 7 (the full-precision output is rounded to three significant figures in the text).

import numpy as np
g = 9.81

def vee(sa, sc):
    """Slot vectors r_i (formation frame: x forward, y starboard) of a 5-ship vee."""
    return np.array([[0, 0], [-sa, sc], [-sa, -sc], [-2*sa, 2*sc], [-2*sa, -2*sc]], float)

def displacements(r):
    """d[i,j] = r_i - r_j for every pair, plus consistency checks."""
    d = r[:, None, :] - r[None, :, :]
    anti = np.abs(d + d.transpose(1, 0, 2)).max()
    N = len(r)
    cyc = max(np.abs(d[i, j] + d[j, k] - d[i, k]).max()
              for i in range(N) for j in range(N) for k in range(N))
    return d, anti, cyc

def reconstruct(d, tree, N):
    """Recover r_i (with r_1 = 0) from the displacements on a spanning tree."""
    r = np.full((N, 2), np.nan); r[0] = 0.0
    for _ in range(N):
        for i, j in tree:
            if not np.isnan(r[i]).any(): r[j] = r[i] - d[i, j]
    return r

def slot_in_turn(ri, Vc, omc):
    """Speed, course offset, bank and n_z of slot ri when the structure turns at omc."""
    x, y = ri
    vb = np.array([Vc - omc*y, omc*x])          # slot velocity in the formation frame
    Vi = np.linalg.norm(vb)                      # = |omc| * distance to turn centre
    beta = np.arctan2(vb[1], vb[0])              # course lag relative to psi_c
    phi = np.arctan(abs(omc) * Vi / g)           # coordinated level turn
    return Vi, beta, phi, 1/np.cos(phi)

def feasibility_table(r, Vc, phic, Vmin, Vmax, phimax):
    omc = g*np.tan(phic)/Vc
    print("V_c=%g m/s, phi_c=%.0f deg -> omega_c=%.4f rad/s, R_c=%.1f m"
          % (Vc, np.degrees(phic), omc, Vc/omc))
    print(" slot    V_i   beta  phi_i   n_z  ok")
    for k, ri in enumerate(r):
        Vi, beta, phi, nz = slot_in_turn(ri, Vc, omc)        # right turn (omc > 0)
        ok = Vmin <= Vi <= Vmax and phi <= phimax
        print(" %4d %6.1f %6.1f %6.1f %5.2f  %s" % (k+1, Vi, np.degrees(beta),
                                                  np.degrees(phi), nz, ok))

def max_bank(r, Vc, Vmin, Vmax, phimax):
    """Largest phi_c for which every slot is feasible in both turn directions."""
    def ok(phic):
        omc = g*np.tan(phic)/Vc
        return all(Vmin <= slot_in_turn(ri, Vc, s*omc)[0] <= Vmax and
                   slot_in_turn(ri, Vc, s*omc)[2] <= phimax for ri in r for s in (1, -1))
    lo, hi = 0.0, phimax
    for _ in range(60):
        mid = 0.5*(lo + hi)
        lo, hi = (mid, hi) if ok(mid) else (lo, mid)
    return lo

r = vee(60.0, 40.0)
d, anti, cyc = displacements(r)
print("d_12 =", d[0, 1], " d_24 =", d[1, 3], " d_14 =", d[0, 3])
print("antisymmetry error %.1e, cycle error %.1e" % (anti, cyc))
print("tree reconstruction:\n", reconstruct(d, [(0, 1), (0, 2), (1, 3), (2, 4)], 5))
feasibility_table(r, 25.0, np.radians(30), 16.0, 32.0, np.radians(45))
print("max feasible phi_c = %.1f deg" % np.degrees(max_bank(r, 25.0, 16.0, 32.0, np.radians(45))))
feasibility_table(vee(400.0, 250.0), 150.0, np.radians(45), 120.0, 190.0, np.radians(60))
print("max feasible phi_c = %.1f deg" % np.degrees(max_bank(vee(400.0, 250.0), 150.0, 120.0, 190.0, np.radians(60))))

Expected output (abridged): d_12 = [ 60. -40.] d_24 = [ 60. -40.] d_14 = [120. -80.], both consistency errors 0.0e+00, the 25 m/s table with slots 3 and 5 flagged False and max feasible phi_c = 10.5 deg, the 150 m/s table with slot 5 at 190.0 flagged False and max feasible phi_c = 45.0 deg.

10. Common Pitfalls

  • Sign of the lateral offset. \(y_i\) is positive to starboard; "inside" and "outside" swap with the turn direction. A formation that is feasible turning left can be infeasible turning right (echelon). Check both signs of \(\omega_c\).
  • Using the pure-lateral formula when \(x_i\sim R_c\). At 25 m/s and 30° bank the rear slots are further from the turn centre than the lead; the lateral-only estimate for slot 4 was 6.88 m/s against an exact 28.0 m/s. Always use \(\rho_i=\|r_i-c\|\).
  • Forgetting that \(d_{ij}\) rotates. \(\dot d_{ij}=\omega_cRJ(r_i-r_j)\) is a real relative velocity (32.7 m/s between lead and slot 5 in the small turn). Without feedforward the controller must earn it from error — Part 12.
  • Inconsistent displacements. Tuning pairwise offsets independently breaks the cycle identity; the swarm then converges to the least-squares compromise, not the drawing. Always generate \(d_{ij}\) from one slot table.
  • Mixing frames. \(r_i-r_j\) lives in the formation frame, \(d_{ij}\) in the inertial frame. Rotating twice (or not at all) produces a formation that is right only when \(\psi_c=0\).
  • Heading wrap-around. \(\hat\psi_c\) estimates differ by \(2\pi\) across aircraft without affecting \(R(\hat\psi_c)\), but any arithmetic on \(\hat\psi_c\) itself (rate limiting, consensus) must wrap differences to \((-\pi,\pi]\).
  • Rigid trail versus path trail. A rigid-body trail slot flies faster than the lead on a larger circle; a path-following trail does not. Do not specify a trail as slot vectors if what you want is "follow the leader's path".
  • Spacing without a latency budget. \(V\tau\) is 22.5 m at 150 m/s with 150 ms latency; a spacing chosen from the safety distance alone is too tight.

11. Exercises

Exercise 1 (derivation). Starting from \(d_{ij}=R(\psi_c)(r_i-r_j)\) with constant \(r_i\), derive \(\dot d_{ij}\) and show that \(\|\dot d_{ij}\|=|\omega_c|\|r_i-r_j\|\). Evaluate it for the lead and slot 5 of the vee in a 30° turn at 25 m/s.
Show solution
\(\frac{d}{dt}R(\psi_c)=\dot\psi_cR(\psi_c)J\) gives \(\dot d_{ij}=\omega_cR(\psi_c)J(r_i-r_j)\). Both \(R\) and \(J\) are orthogonal, so \(\|\dot d_{ij}\|=|\omega_c|\,\|r_i-r_j\|\). With \(\omega_c=9.81\tan30^\circ/25=0.227\) rad/s and \(\|r_1-r_5\|=\sqrt{120^2+80^2}=144\) m: \(\|\dot d_{15}\|=32.7\) m/s.

12. Key Takeaways & Formula Sheet

  • A formation is a target configuration modulo a group: translations (displacement), rigid motions (distance), translations + scale (bearing). The group is chosen by what can be sensed; it fixes how many independent constraints are needed: \(2N-2\), \(2N-3\), \(2N-3\) in the plane.
  • Slots: \(\boxed{p_i^d=p_c+R(\psi_c)r_i}\), displacements \(\boxed{d_{ij}=R(\psi_c)(r_i-r_j)}\); consistency \(d_{ij}=-d_{ji}\), \(d_{ij}+d_{jk}=d_{ik}\); a spanning tree of displacements determines all; inconsistent specs converge to the least-squares compromise \((L\otimes I_2)r=(B^T\otimes I_2)d\).
  • Rotating formation: \(\boxed{\dot d_{ij}=\omega_cR(\psi_c)J(r_i-r_j)}\), magnitude \(|\omega_c|\|r_i-r_j\|\).
  • Steady turn: \(v_i^d=R(\psi_c)[V_c-\omega_cy_i,\ \omega_cx_i]^T\), \(\boxed{V_i=|\omega_c|\rho_i}\) with \(\rho_i=\|r_i-c\|\), \(\chi_i=\psi_c+\operatorname{atan2}(\omega_cx_i,V_c-\omega_cy_i)\), \(\boxed{\tan\phi_i=\omega_cV_i/g=\tan\phi_c\,\rho_i/R_c}\).
  • Feasibility: \(\boxed{V_{\min}\le|\omega_c|\rho_i\le V_{\max},\ \rho_i\le g\tan\phi_{\max}/\omega_c^2}\); pure lateral: \(\boxed{-R_c(1-V_{\min}/V_c)\le y_i^{\mathrm{out}}\le R_c(V_{\max}/V_c-1)}\). Numbers: 25 m/s, 30°: \(R_c=110\) m, window \([-39.7,30.9]\) m; 150 m/s, 45°: \(R_c=2290\) m, window \([-459,612]\) m.
  • Design: \(R_c\ge y_{\max}/\min(1-V_{\min}/V_c,\ V_{\max}/V_c-1)\), \(\phi_c\le\arctan(V_c^2/gR_{c,\mathrm{req}})\); 5-ship vee 60×40 m at 25 m/s: \(\phi_c\le10.5^\circ\) exact; 400×250 m at 150 m/s: \(\phi_c\le45.0^\circ\).
  • Spacing: \(s_{\min}\ge d_s+2\varepsilon_{\mathrm{GPS}}+V\tau+\)wake; reconfiguration is a linear assignment problem; altitude offsets \(\Delta h_i\) are part of the specification.
