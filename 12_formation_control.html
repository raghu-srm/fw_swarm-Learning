1. Why This Matters

Part 10 told us what a formation is (slot vectors \(r_i\), displacements \(d_{ij}=r_i-r_j\), distances \(\|r_i-r_j\|\)); Part 11 told us when a set of distances pins a shape down. This part gives the control laws that make a swarm of UAVs actually fly the shape, and proves that they work. Its position in the pipeline:

\[ \text{formation spec }(r_i,\,G)\ \rightarrow\ \boxed{\text{formation law } u_i}\ \rightarrow\ \text{velocity command}\ \rightarrow\ (\chi^c_i, V^c_{g,i})\ \rightarrow\ \text{safety filter}\ \rightarrow\ \text{autopilot} \]

Three families exist, differing in what each UAV must measure and what the swarm agrees on implicitly. The displacement law needs relative positions in a common frame (GPS plus a shared north) and is linear consensus with an offset. The distance law needs only ranges and body-frame bearings, no common heading, and its guarantee is local and rests on infinitesimal rigidity. A third family uses bearings only. We derive the first two fully, state the third, and then confront them with the fact that aircraft never stop: a moving formation without velocity feedforward lags by \(V/k\), which at 60 m/s and a comfortable gain is 120 m.

InputConsensus with listening weights \(W=A^T\) (Part 7), pinning and \(H=L+B\) (Part 8), second-order consensus (Part 9), slot vectors and consistency (Part 10), the rigidity matrix \(R(p)\) (Part 11).
OutputYou can write, prove and tune the displacement and distance laws, compute the steady lag of a moving formation, choose feedforward, and map the resulting velocity command onto a fixed-wing aircraft's course and speed.
Used inPart 13 (the slot velocity that feeds the feedforward), Part 19, Part 20 (the nominal command the safety filter protects), Part 23 and the capstone CLAW (Part 24).

2. Displacement-Based Formation Control

Single-integrator model \(\dot p_i=u_i\), \(p_i\in\mathbb R^n\): the law outputs a velocity command. UAV \(i\) listens to in-neighbours \(j\in\mathcal N_i^{\mathrm{in}}\) with weights \(w_{ij}=a_{ji}\) and knows the desired displacements \(d_{ij}=r_i-r_j\) from the slot table.

Definition (formation error). With consistent displacements \(d_{ij}=r_i-r_j\) (Part 10), define the formation error of UAV \(i\) as \(e_i=p_i-r_i\): how far UAV \(i\) is from its slot, measured as if the formation frame were anchored at the origin. The formation is achieved (up to translation) iff all \(e_i\) are equal.
\[ \boxed{\;u_i=-k_c\sum_{j\in\mathcal N_i^{\mathrm{in}}}w_{ij}\big[(p_i-p_j)-d_{ij}\big]\;} \qquad k_c\gt0 \]
Theorem 1 (displacement-based formation = consensus on the formation error). Under the boxed law with consistent \(d_{ij}=r_i-r_j\), the stacked error \(e=\operatorname{col}(e_1,\dots,e_N)\) obeys \(\dot e=-k_c(L\otimes I_n)e\), where \(L=D_{\mathrm{in}}-W\) is the in-degree Laplacian. Hence \(p_i(t)-p_j(t)\to d_{ij}\) for all \(i,j\) iff the communication digraph contains a directed spanning tree (Part 7). For an undirected connected graph the common limit is \(e^\ast=\frac1N\sum_j e_j(0)\) and the disagreement decays as \(\|\delta(t)\|\le e^{-k_c\lambda_2 t}\|\delta(0)\|\).
Proof. Substitute \(d_{ij}=r_i-r_j\): \((p_i-p_j)-d_{ij}=(p_i-r_i)-(p_j-r_j)=e_i-e_j\). Since the \(r_i\) are constant, \(\dot e_i=\dot p_i=u_i\), so \[ \dot e_i=-k_c\sum_j w_{ij}(e_i-e_j)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \dot e=-k_c(L\otimes I_n)\,e . \] This is exactly the consensus protocol of Parts 5 and 7 applied to the vectors \(e_i\); each Cartesian component evolves independently under \(-k_cL\). The spanning-tree condition, the limit \(e^\ast=\gamma^Te(0)\) and the rate \(k_c\lambda_2\) (undirected case, Part 5) follow verbatim. Finally \(e_i\to e^\ast\) for all \(i\) gives \(p_i-p_j\to r_i-r_j=d_{ij}\). \(\square\)
Interpretation: the displacement law is consensus in disguise. Every convergence result you already own (switching graphs, delays, discrete-time step limits from Part 6, digraphs from Part 7) transfers unchanged, because the formation offsets cancel out of the error dynamics. The price is that \(p_i-p_j\) must be expressed in a common frame: every UAV needs the same north.
Caution (sign and consistency). \(d_{ij}=r_i-r_j\) is "where \(i\) sits relative to \(j\)". Using \(r_j-r_i\) by mistake flies the formation mirrored through its centre. The set \(\{d_{ij}\}\) must be consistent: \(d_{ij}+d_{jk}=d_{ik}\) around every cycle (Part 10). For an undirected graph the law is the gradient flow \(u=-k_c\nabla V_f\) of \(V_f(p)=\tfrac12\sum_{(i,j)\in E}a_{ij}\|(p_i-p_j)-d_{ij}\|^2\); with an inconsistent set the minimum of \(V_f\) is positive and the swarm converges to a compromise shape with permanent residual error.

2.1 Absolute placement: pin the formation to a leader

Theorem 1 fixes the shape but not where it is: \(e^\ast\) is whatever the initial errors average to. To place the formation, give some UAVs (pinning gains \(b_i\gt0\)) access to a reference position \(p_0\) (lead aircraft or GCS waypoint, index 0) and add the leader-follower term of Part 8:

\[ u_i=-k_c\sum_j w_{ij}\big[(p_i-p_j)-d_{ij}\big]-k_p\,b_i\,(p_i-p_0-r_i). \]

With \(e_i=p_i-p_0-r_i\) the same substitution gives \(\dot e=-\big[(k_cL+k_pB)\otimes I_n\big]e-\mathbf 1_N\otimes\dot p_0\). For a static reference, \(H_k=k_cL+k_pB\succ0\) whenever the graph is connected and at least one \(b_i\gt0\) (Part 8), so \(e\to0\): every UAV reaches its slot \(p_0+r_i\) exponentially at rate \(\lambda_{\min}(H_k)\). For a moving reference the extra term \(\mathbf 1\otimes\dot p_0\) is a persistent disturbance; Section 4 shows what it costs.

3. Second-Order Version (velocity terms and reference feedforward)

When the plant is a double integrator \(\dot p_i=v_i,\ \dot v_i=u_i\) (an acceleration-level interface, Part 9), the law needs a damping term on relative velocities and, if the formation moves along a reference \(p_i^d(t)=p_0(t)+r_i\), feedforward of the reference velocity \(v_i^d=\dot p_0\) and acceleration \(a_i^d=\ddot p_0\):

\[ \boxed{\;u_i=a_i^d-\sum_j w_{ij}\Big[k_p\big((p_i-p_j)-d_{ij}\big)+k_v(v_i-v_j)\Big]-b_i\Big[k_p(p_i-p_i^d)+k_v(v_i-v_i^d)\Big]\;} \]

With \(e_i=p_i-p_i^d\) (so \(\dot e_i=v_i-v_i^d\), \(\ddot e_i=u_i-a_i^d\)) the same cancellation as in Theorem 1 gives \(\ddot e=-k_p(H\otimes I_n)e-k_v(H\otimes I_n)\dot e\) with \(H=L+B\). For an undirected graph diagonalise \(H=\sum_k\mu_k v_kv_k^T\) (\(\mu_k\gt0\) when connected and pinned): each mode is a second-order system

\[ \ddot z_k+k_v\mu_k\dot z_k+k_p\mu_k z_k=0,\qquad \omega_{n,k}=\sqrt{k_p\mu_k},\qquad \zeta_k=\frac{k_v\sqrt{\mu_k}}{2\sqrt{k_p}} . \]

Numbers (verified): three UAVs on the complete graph \(K_3\), UAV 1 pinned (\(b_1=1\)): \(\mu(L+B)=\{0.268,\ 3,\ 3.73\}\). With \(k_p=1\), \(k_v=1.5\): \(\omega_{n}=\{0.518,\ 1.73,\ 1.93\}\) rad/s and \(\zeta=\{0.388,\ 1.30,\ 1.45\}\). The closed-loop eigenvalues are \(-0.201\pm0.477\mathrm i\), \(-0.774\), \(-0.814\), \(-3.69\), \(-4.82\): the slowest, under-damped mode is the whole formation sloshing relative to the leader (eigenvector along \(v_1\) of \(H\)). To speed it up you do not raise \(k_p\) (that makes the fast modes stiffer still); you raise \(\mu_{\min}(H)\) by pinning more UAVs or pinning a central one (Part 8).

Note: the damping ratio grows with \(\sqrt{\mu_k}\), so the fast graph modes are always better damped than the slow ones. Choose \(k_v\) for the slowest mode (\(\zeta_1\approx0.7\) wants \(k_v\approx1.4\sqrt{k_p/\mu_{\min}}\)) and then check that \(k_v\mu_{\max}\) is still below the autopilot bandwidth (Section 8).

4. Formation Manoeuvring: Rotating Formations and the Lag Argument

A formation that turns keeps its shape in the formation frame while the displacements rotate in the inertial frame. With the structure heading \(\psi_c(t)\) and \(R(\psi)\) the planar rotation of Section 2 of the course conventions,

\[ d_{ij}(t)=R(\psi_c(t))\,(r_i-r_j),\qquad \dot d_{ij}(t)=\omega_c\,R(\psi_c)\,J\,(r_i-r_j),\qquad \omega_c=\dot\psi_c, \]

using \(\frac{d}{dt}R(\psi)=\dot\psi R(\psi)J\) (derived step by step in Part 13). The slot of UAV \(i\) is \(p_i^d(t)=p_c(t)+R(\psi_c)r_i\) and its velocity is \(v_i^d=\dot p_i^d=v_c+\omega_cR(\psi_c)Jr_i\). The time-varying displacement law with feedforward is

\[ \boxed{\;u_i=v_i^d-k_c\sum_j w_{ij}\Big[(p_i-p_j)-\big(p_i^d-p_j^d\big)\Big]-k_p\,b_i\,(p_i-p_i^d)\;} \]

Now \((p_i-p_j)-(p_i^d-p_j^d)=e_i-e_j\) with \(e_i=p_i-p_i^d\), and \(\dot e_i=u_i-v_i^d\), so \(\dot e=-(H_k\otimes I_n)e\) exactly, whatever the formation does. The error dynamics are autonomous and exponentially stable with rate \(\lambda_{\min}(H_k)\); the only residual error comes from errors in \(v_i^d\) itself (an estimated structure state, Part 13).

4.1 Without feedforward: the lag

Drop \(v_i^d\). Then \(\dot e=-(H_k\otimes I_n)e-v^d\) where \(v^d=\operatorname{col}(v_1^d,\dots,v_N^d)\). On a straight leg every slot moves at the same \(v_d\), \(v^d=\mathbf 1_N\otimes v_d\), and the error settles at

\[ e_{ss}=-\big(H_k^{-1}\mathbf 1_N\big)\otimes v_d . \]

For a single UAV tracking its own slot, \(H_k=k\) and \(\dot\varepsilon=-k\varepsilon-v_d\) gives \(\varepsilon_{ss}=-v_d/k\): the UAV flies behind its slot by \(|v_d|/k\). Verified numbers (40 s simulation): \(V=60\) m/s, \(k=0.5\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}\) gives a lag of 120 m (simulated 119.9 m).

Through a network the lag accumulates: for \(K_3\) with UAV 1 pinned, \(k_c=k_p=0.5\), \(H_k^{-1}\mathbf 1=(6,\,8,\,8)\) s, so at 60 m/s the three UAVs settle 360 m, 480 m and 480 m behind their slots (60 s simulation: 359.9, 479.8, 479.8 m). High-speed note. The lag is \(V/k\): it grows linearly with speed, while \(k\) is capped from above by the course-loop bandwidth (time-scale separation needs \(k_c\lambda_N+k_p\) well below \(b_\chi\), Part 15) and by the sampling rule \(T_s(k_p+k_c\lambda_N)\lt2\) (Part 6). At 250 m/s and \(k=0.5\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}\) the lag is 500 m, more than most formation spacings. Feedforward is therefore not a refinement at high speed; it is the controller. Lag also appears in turns as a radial offset (the UAV cuts inside its slot's circle), which converts into a speed and bank mismatch through Part 10's feasibility relations.

5. Distance-Based Formation Control

Suppose the UAVs have no common heading (GPS-denied, magnetometer-free, or simply a formation that must be invariant to its own orientation). Each UAV \(i\) measures the relative position \(p_i-p_j\) of its neighbours in its own frame; the only coordinate-free information in it is the distance \(\|p_i-p_j\|\) and a direction that UAV \(i\) can act along. The target is now a set of scalar distances on an undirected graph \(G=(\mathcal V,E)\):

Definition (distance specification). Desired distances \(\bar d_{ij}=\|r_i-r_j\|\) for each edge \((i,j)\in E\). (The literature writes \(d_{ij}\); we keep the bar to separate it from the displacement vector.) Squared-distance errors \(e_k=\|p_i-p_j\|^2-\bar d_{ij}^2\) for the \(k\)-th edge; stacked \(e\in\mathbb R^{|E|}\). Potential \[ V(p)=\frac14\sum_{(i,j)\in E}\big(\|p_i-p_j\|^2-\bar d_{ij}^2\big)^2=\frac14\|e\|^2 . \]

Differentiate: \(\partial e_k/\partial p_i=2(p_i-p_j)^T\) for an edge \(k=(i,j)\) touching \(i\), so

\[ \frac{\partial V}{\partial p_i}=\frac14\sum_{k\ni i}2e_k\cdot2(p_i-p_j)=\sum_{j\in\mathcal N_i}\big(\|p_i-p_j\|^2-\bar d_{ij}^2\big)(p_i-p_j), \] \[ \boxed{\;u_i=-k_d\sum_{j\in\mathcal N_i}\big(\|p_i-p_j\|^2-\bar d_{ij}^2\big)(p_i-p_j)\;}\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad \boxed{\;\dot p=-k_d\,R(p)^T e\;} \]

The vector form uses the rigidity matrix of Part 11: \(R(p)=\tfrac12\,\partial g/\partial p\) has row \(k\) equal to \((p_i-p_j)^T\) in block \(i\) and \(-(p_i-p_j)^T\) in block \(j\), so \((R^Te)_i=\sum_{k\ni i}e_k(p_i-p_j)\), which is the sum above. We include a gain \(k_d\) (units \(\mathrm{m^{-2}s^{-1}}\)); \(k_d=1\) recovers the textbook law. Each term \(e_k(p_i-p_j)\) is a push or pull along the line to the neighbour: too far (\(e_k\gt0\)) pulls, too close pushes. Only \(\|p_i-p_j\|\) and the direction to \(j\) in UAV \(i\)'s own frame are needed.

Theorem 2 (local exponential stability under infinitesimal rigidity; Krick, Broucke & Francis 2009). Let the target framework \((G,p^\ast)\) be infinitesimally rigid and minimally rigid (\(|E|=2N-3\) in the plane). Then \(p^\ast\) (and every framework congruent to it) is a locally exponentially stable equilibrium of \(\dot p=-k_dR(p)^Te\), with local rate \(2k_d\lambda_{\min}\big(R(p^\ast)R(p^\ast)^T\big)\).
Proof sketch. \(\dot V=\nabla V^T\dot p=-k_d\|R(p)^Te\|^2\le0\), so \(V\) is a Lyapunov function and the squared-distance errors are bounded. Differentiate the error: \(\dot e=\frac{\partial g}{\partial p}\dot p=2R(p)\dot p\), hence \[ \dot e=-2k_d\,R(p)R(p)^T\,e . \] Infinitesimal rigidity means \(\operatorname{rank}R(p^\ast)=2N-3\); with \(|E|=2N-3\) the \(|E|\) rows are linearly independent, so \(R(p^\ast)R(p^\ast)^T\succ0\). By continuity \(R(p)R(p)^T\succ0\) in a neighbourhood of the target, and \(\dot e=-2k_dRR^Te\) is locally a linear system with a positive-definite matrix: \(\|e\|\) decays exponentially. Since \(e=0\) means every edge has its length, \(p\) converges to a framework congruent to \(p^\ast\). For a redundantly rigid graph (\(|E|\gt2N-3\)) \(e\) lives in the column space of \(R\) and the same argument applies there (Sun et al. 2016; survey in Oh, Park & Ahn 2015). \(\square\)
Interpretation: rigidity is not a side condition, it is the stability condition. If the graph is flexible, \(R R^T\) is singular and there are error directions the law cannot see (the flex of Part 11's square); if the graph is rigid, the law is locally a linear exponentially stable system in the edge errors. The matrix \(RR^T\) scales with \(\bar d^2\), so the gain must be scheduled as \(k_d\propto1/\bar d^2\) to keep the same rate when the formation is scaled up.

5.1 Invariances and undesired equilibria

  • Invariance. \(V\) depends only on distances, so the law is invariant to translation, rotation and reflection of the whole swarm. Moreover \(\sum_iu_i=0\) (each edge contributes \(e_k(p_i-p_j)+e_k(p_j-p_i)=0\)), so the centroid never moves. The law cannot place or orient the formation; that job belongs to a leader or to a virtual structure (Part 13).
  • Flip ambiguity. The mirror image of the triangle has the same distances, so it is an equally valid limit. Which one you get depends on the initial condition (globally rigid graphs, Part 11, remove this ambiguity only up to reflection in the plane).
  • Undesired equilibria. Every critical point of \(V\) with \(e\ne0\) is an equilibrium. For the triangle these are the collinear configurations: if all three UAVs start on a line, every \(u_i\) is a combination of vectors along that line, so the line is invariant and the swarm can never open into a triangle (verified in Section 7). These equilibria are saddles (unstable to out-of-line perturbations), so they are not attractors in practice, but the convergence guarantee is only local.

6. Bearing-Based Note and Comparison

A third family uses only unit-vector bearings \(g_{ij}=(p_j-p_i)/\|p_j-p_i\|\) (what a camera gives). With desired bearings \(g_{ij}^\ast\) and the orthogonal projector \(P_g=I-gg^T\), the bearing-only law of Zhao & Zelazo (2016) is \(\dot p_i=-\sum_{j\in\mathcal N_i}P_{g_{ij}}\,g_{ij}^\ast\): UAV \(i\) moves against the component of the desired bearing that is perpendicular to the current one, which rotates the line of sight toward \(g_{ij}^\ast\). Under infinitesimal bearing rigidity (Part 11) the target is almost globally stable; the formation is determined up to translation and scale, which is a feature (scale is free) or a bug (spacing is not controlled) depending on the mission.

7. Worked Example: Three UAVs, Both Laws

Slots (north, east) in metres for an equilateral vee of side 100 m: \(r_1=(0,0)\), \(r_2=(-86.6,\,50)\), \(r_3=(-86.6,\,-50)\). Initial positions \(p_1(0)=(30,10)\), \(p_2(0)=(-40,90)\), \(p_3(0)=(-120,-20)\). Communication graph \(K_3\) (every pair linked, unit weights). All numbers below come from the script in Section 9.

7.1 Displacement law, \(k_c=0.5\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}\)

\(L=\begin{bmatrix}2&-1&-1\\-1&2&-1\\-1&-1&2\end{bmatrix}\), eigenvalues \(\{0,3,3\}\), so the rate is \(k_c\lambda_2=1.5\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}\) (time constant 0.667 s). Formation errors \(e_1(0)=(30,10)\), \(e_2(0)=(46.6,40)\), \(e_3(0)=(-33.4,30)\); their mean is \(e^\ast=(14.4,\,26.7)\). Theorem 1 predicts the final positions \(r_i+e^\ast\):

\[ p_1\to(14.4,\,26.7),\qquad p_2\to(-72.2,\,76.7),\qquad p_3\to(-72.2,\,-23.3)\ \text{m}, \]

which the simulation reproduces to three decimals at \(t=8\) s. The disagreement starts at \(\|\delta(0)\|=63.5\) m and at \(t=1\) s is 14.2 m, right at the bound \(63.5e^{-1.5}=14.2\) m (for \(K_3\) all non-zero modes have the same eigenvalue, so the bound is tight). The largest slot error, 47.8 m initially, drops below 1 m after 2.58 s. Check against the closed form \(e(t)=e^{-k_cLt}e(0)\): at \(t=1\) s the simulation gives \(p_1=(17.88,22.95)\) and expm gives \((17.88,22.95)\).

7.2 Distance law, \(k_d=10^{-4}\ \mathrm{m^{-2}s^{-1}}\)

Desired distances \(\bar d_{12}=\bar d_{23}=\bar d_{13}=100\) m. At the target, \(\operatorname{rank}R=3=2N-3\) (minimally and infinitesimally rigid, Part 11) and \(R R^T=10^4\begin{bmatrix}2&0.5&0.5\\0.5&2&0.5\\0.5&0.5&2\end{bmatrix}\) with eigenvalues \(\{1.5,\,1.5,\,3\}\times10^4\ \mathrm{m^2}\). Theorem 2 predicts a local rate \(2k_d\lambda_{\min}=3.0\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}\); the simulation's decay rate of \(\|e\|\) measured between 4 and 6 s is 3.00 s\(^{-1}\). Initial distances are 106.3, 136.0, 153.0 m, so \(e(0)=(1300,\,8500,\,13400)\ \mathrm{m^2}\) and the initial commands are large (up to 300 m/s for UAV 3): the cubic growth of the law far from the target is why it must be saturated on a real aircraft (Section 8). The maximum distance error falls below 1 m after 0.906 s and below 0.1 m after 1.68 s; \(\|e\|\) goes \(15{,}900\to205\to10.2\to0.02\ \mathrm{m^2}\) at \(t=0,1,2,4\) s. Final positions \(p_1=(9.13,2.57)\), \(p_2=(-48.7,84.2)\), \(p_3=(-90.4,-6.72)\) m: all three distances 100.0 m, the centroid \((-43.3,26.7)\) unchanged from \(t=0\), and the orientation is the un-mirrored one (signed area equal to the target's). Starting from the mirrored initial condition gives the mirrored triangle. Starting collinear, \(p=(0,0),(-60,0),(-150,0)\), the swarm stays on the line for ever and settles at distances 45.9, 68.8, 114.7 m: an undesired equilibrium.

Compare: both laws reach the same shape; the displacement law also fixes the orientation (vee pointing north) and converges from anywhere, the distance law fixes neither the orientation nor the handedness and converges only from a neighbourhood, but needs no common frame.

8. Fixed-Wing Interpretation

The first-order laws output a velocity vector \(u_i\in\mathbb R^2\) (horizontal plane; altitude is commanded separately). A fixed-wing autopilot accepts a course and a ground-speed command, so the swarm layer converts

\[ \chi_i^c=\operatorname{atan2}(u_{i,e},\,u_{i,n}),\qquad V_{g,i}^c=\operatorname{clip}\big(\|u_i\|,\,V_{\min},\,V_{\max}\big), \]

and the course loop then turns at \(\dot\chi=b_\chi(\chi^c-\chi)\) saturated at \(\omega_{\max}=g\tan\phi_{\max}/V_g\) (Parts 15–16). Four things follow.

  • The feedforward is what makes \(\|u_i\|\) flyable. With \(u_i=v_i^d+\text{correction}\), the magnitude stays near \(V_c\) and the direction near \(\psi_c\). Without it, the pure correction term tends to zero as the error shrinks, below \(V_{\min}\): the aircraft cannot slow down to follow it and oscillates or circles. Saturate the correction, \(\|u_i-v_i^d\|\le\Delta v_{\max}\) (scale it down, do not clip componentwise), so that \(V^c_{g,i}\in[V_{\min},V_{\max}]\) and \(|\chi^c_i-\psi_c|\) stays small.
  • Gains are bounded by the autopilot. The error dynamics are only valid if the aircraft tracks \(\chi^c,V^c_g\) faster than the formation dynamics: \(k_c\lambda_N+k_p\lesssim b_\chi/3\). With \(b_\chi\approx1.5\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}\) and \(\lambda_N=3\) this leaves \(k_c\approx0.1\)–\(0.15\), hence the lag numbers of Section 4 are realistic, not pessimistic.
  • Formation hold in turns. With feedforward the slot velocities differ across the formation (outer slots faster, Part 13), so the feasibility of Part 10 applies: lateral offsets must satisfy \(-R_c(1-V_{\min}/V_c)\le y_i\le R_c(V_{\max}/V_c-1)\) and the outer slot's bank \(\tan\phi_i=V_i\omega_c/g\) must stay below \(\phi_{\max}\). Either limit \(\omega_c\) by the outermost slot or change formation before the turn.
  • Altitude is the cheap axis. Give neighbouring slots altitude offsets \(\Delta h_i\) (e.g. \(\pm10\) m small UAV, \(\pm30\) m high-speed): the horizontal law can then be tuned aggressively without the transient ever producing a conflict, and the distance law's flip ambiguity becomes harmless.
High-speed note. Everything in this part that is "per second" becomes "per \(V\) metres". The lag is \(V/k\); the discrete-time limit \(T_s(k_p+k_c\lambda_N)\lt2\) is unchanged but the error made in one sample is \(VT_s\) (15 m at 150 m/s and 10 Hz); the distance law's commands grow as the cube of the spacing, so at a 400 m spacing \(k_d\) must be \(16\times\) smaller than at 100 m to keep the same rate. The high-speed recipe is: slot velocity feedforward from an estimated structure (Part 13), small correction gains with saturation, altitude stacking, and along-track spacing in turns (Part 19).
Toward the CLAW This part supplies the formation guidance block (layer L2) of the unified law. It consumes the structure estimate \(\hat\xi_i=(\hat p_c,\hat\psi_c,\hat V_c,\hat\omega_c)\) from the estimator block (Parts 8 and 13), the slot table \(r_j\), and the neighbours' dead-reckoned positions \(p_j\) and produces the nominal velocity command \[ v_i^{\mathrm{cmd}}=v_i^d-k_p\,b_i\,(p_i-p_i^d)-k_c\sum_jw_{ij}\big[(p_i-p_j)-(p_i^d-p_j^d)\big],\qquad \|v_i^{\mathrm{cmd}}-v_i^d\|\le\Delta v_{\max}, \] with \(p_j^d\) computed from UAV \(i\)'s own \(\hat\xi_i\). This nominal command is handed to the safety filter of Part 20 and then mapped to \((\chi^c_i,V^c_{g,i})\) in Part 15. The distance law is the fallback block when the common frame is lost (GPS-denied mode).

9. Implementation and Interactive Demo

The script reproduces every number of Section 7 (Python 3 + NumPy; run as python3 part12_impl.py).

"""Part 12 worked example: displacement-based vs distance-based formation control, 3 UAVs."""
import numpy as np
np.set_printoptions(precision=3, suppress=True)

# slot vectors r_i (north, east) [m]: equilateral triangle, side 100 m
r = np.array([[0.0, 0.0], [-86.6025, 50.0], [-86.6025, -50.0]])
p0 = np.array([[30.0, 10.0], [-40.0, 90.0], [-120.0, -20.0]])      # initial positions
A = np.ones((3, 3)) - np.eye(3)                                     # K3 (undirected)
W = A.T                                                             # listening weights (Part 7)
E = [(0, 1), (1, 2), (0, 2)]                                        # edge list for the distance law
dbar = np.array([np.linalg.norm(r[i] - r[j]) for i, j in E])        # desired distances

def u_displacement(p, kc=0.5):
    """u_i = -kc * sum_j w_ij [ (p_i - p_j) - (r_i - r_j) ]"""
    u = np.zeros_like(p)
    for i in range(3):
        for j in range(3):
            u[i] -= kc*W[i, j]*((p[i] - p[j]) - (r[i] - r[j]))
    return u

def rigidity_matrix(p):
    """rows (p_i - p_j)^T in block i, -(p_i - p_j)^T in block j (Part 11)"""
    R = np.zeros((len(E), 2*len(p)))
    for k, (i, j) in enumerate(E):
        R[k, 2*i:2*i+2] = p[i] - p[j]
        R[k, 2*j:2*j+2] = -(p[i] - p[j])
    return R

def u_distance(p, kd=1e-4):
    """vector form  p_dot = -kd R(p)^T e,  e_k = ||p_i-p_j||^2 - dbar_k^2"""
    e = np.array([np.linalg.norm(p[i] - p[j])**2 - dbar[k]**2 for k, (i, j) in enumerate(E)])
    return -kd*(rigidity_matrix(p).T @ e).reshape(len(p), 2)

def simulate(law, T=8.0, dt=1e-3):
    p = p0.copy(); hist = [p.copy()]
    for _ in range(int(round(T/dt))):
        p = p + dt*law(p); hist.append(p.copy())
    return np.array(hist)

dt = 1e-3
# --- displacement law: consensus on e_i = p_i - r_i
L = np.diag(W.sum(axis=1)) - W
lam = np.linalg.eigvalsh(L)
e_star = (p0 - r).mean(axis=0)                 # average of the initial formation errors
H1 = simulate(u_displacement)
err1 = np.linalg.norm(H1 - (r + e_star), axis=2).max(axis=1)
print("eig(L) =", lam, " rate k_c*lambda_2 =", 0.5*lam[1], "1/s")
print("predicted final p_i = r_i + e*:\n", r + e_star)
print("simulated p(8 s):\n", H1[-1])
print("displacement law: max slot error < 1 m after %.2f s" % (np.argmax(err1 < 1.0)*dt))

# --- distance law: local rate 2 kd lambda_min(R R^T) at the target
Rs = rigidity_matrix(r)
print("rank R(r*) =", np.linalg.matrix_rank(Rs), " eig(R R^T) =", np.linalg.eigvalsh(Rs @ Rs.T))
print("local rate 2*kd*lambda_min =", 2*1e-4*np.linalg.eigvalsh(Rs @ Rs.T).min(), "1/s")
H2 = simulate(u_distance, T=12.0)
dist = lambda p: np.array([np.linalg.norm(p[i] - p[j]) for i, j in E])
err2 = np.array([np.abs(dist(p) - dbar).max() for p in H2])
print("distance law: final positions\n", H2[-1], "\n  distances =", dist(H2[-1]))
print("distance law: max distance error < 1 m after %.3f s" % (np.argmax(err2 < 1.0)*dt))
print("centroid start", p0.mean(axis=0), " end", H2[-1].mean(axis=0))

Output (abridged): eig(L) = [0 3 3], rate 1.5 1/s, final positions as in Section 7.1, max slot error < 1 m after 2.58 s; rank R = 3, eig(RR^T) = [15000 15000 30000], local rate 3.0 1/s, max distance error < 1 m after 0.906 s, centroid unchanged.

10. Common Pitfalls

  • Displacement sign. \(d_{ij}=r_i-r_j\). Build the table from the slot vectors in code and never type displacements by hand; check \(d_{ij}=-d_{ji}\) and the cycle sums (Part 10).
  • Mixing frames. The displacement law needs \(p_i-p_j\) in one frame. A body-frame relative position fed to it rotates the formation with the aircraft's heading. Use the distance law, or rotate measurements by the own heading first (which requires a heading).
  • No feedforward on a moving formation. Lag \(V/k\) per UAV, accumulating through the graph. Always feed \(v_i^d\) forward and saturate only the correction.
  • Treating the distance law as global. It is local; collinear and other saddle configurations exist, and the mirrored formation is a valid limit. Initialise near the target (e.g. after a displacement-based assembly) and stack altitudes.
  • Gain units. \(k_d\) has units \(\mathrm{m^{-2}s^{-1}}\) and the right value scales as \(1/\bar d^2\); \(k_c\) and \(k_p\) are in \(\mathrm{s^{-1}}\) and must respect \(T_s(k_p+k_c\lambda_N)\lt2\) and the autopilot bandwidth.
  • Angle wrap-around when mapping to \(\chi^c\). Compute \(\chi^c\) with atan2 and wrap \(\chi^c-\chi\) to \((-\pi,\pi]\) before feeding the course loop; a 350° error is a 10° error.
  • Stale neighbour data. \(p_j\) arrives with latency \(\tau\); at speed \(V\) it is \(V\tau\) old (6 m at 60 m/s and 100 ms). Dead-reckon \(p_j+v_j\tau\) before using it (Part 23).

11. Exercises

Exercise 1 (derivation). Show that for an undirected graph the displacement law is the gradient flow of \(V_f(p)=\tfrac12\sum_{(i,j)\in E}a_{ij}\|(p_i-p_j)-d_{ij}\|^2\), and explain what the swarm converges to when the \(d_{ij}\) are inconsistent.
Show solution
Differentiate edge by edge: \(\partial/\partial p_i\) of \(\tfrac12a_{ij}\|(p_i-p_j)-d_{ij}\|^2\) is \(a_{ij}[(p_i-p_j)-d_{ij}]\); writing the same edge from \(j\)'s side with \(d_{ji}=-d_{ij}\) gives the matching term for \(p_j\). Summing over the edges at \(i\) gives \(\partial V_f/\partial p_i=\sum_ja_{ij}[(p_i-p_j)-d_{ij}]\), so \(u=-k_c\nabla V_f\). \(V_f\) is a convex quadratic in \(p\); the flow converges to its minimisers. If the \(d_{ij}\) are consistent the minimum is zero (exact formation). If not, the minimum is positive and the limit is the least-squares compromise: residual edge errors remain, distributed according to the weights.
Exercise 2 (numeric). Four UAVs on a ring (cycle \(C_4\), unit weights) run the displacement law with \(k_c=0.4\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}\). Compute the Laplacian eigenvalues, the convergence rate, and the time for the disagreement to shrink by a factor 100.
Show solution
\(\lambda(L)=\{0,2,2,4\}\). Rate \(k_c\lambda_2=0.8\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}\). From \(\|\delta(t)\|\le e^{-0.8t}\|\delta(0)\|\), a factor 100 needs \(t=\ln(100)/0.8=5.76\) s (verified).
Exercise 3 (numeric / design). A high-speed formation at \(V=150\) m/s must keep the no-feedforward lag below 30 m. What single-UAV gain \(k\) is needed? Using the \(K_3\)-with-pinned-UAV-1 graph (\(\mu_{\max}(L+B)=3.73\)) and \(T_s=0.1\) s, is the Euler-discretised loop stable with that gain? What is the practical conclusion?
Show solution
\(k=V/\text{lag}=150/30=5\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}\). Euler stability needs \(T_sk\mu_{\max}\lt2\): \(0.1\times5\times3.73=1.87\), marginally stable on paper but with almost no margin, and far above any fixed-wing course-loop bandwidth (\(b_\chi\approx1\)–\(2\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}\)), so the time-scale separation behind the error dynamics is lost. Conclusion: the lag cannot be fixed with gain at high speed; use slot-velocity feedforward.
Exercise 4 (code). Apply the distance law with \(k_d=10^{-4}\ \mathrm{m^{-2}s^{-1}}\) to the square of side 100 m with one diagonal (Part 11: \(p^\ast=(0,0),(100,0),(100,100),(0,100)\), edges the four sides plus \((1,3)\)). Compute the rank of \(R(p^\ast)\), the local rate, and the time to bring all distance errors below 1 m from a start perturbed by Gaussian noise of 20 m (seed 3).
Show solution
\(\operatorname{rank}R=5=2N-3\) (minimally rigid). \(\lambda(RR^T)=\{7639,\ 2\times10^4,\ 2\times10^4,\ 2\times10^4,\ 52{,}361\}\ \mathrm{m^2}\), so the local rate is \(2k_d\lambda_{\min}=1.53\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}\). With numpy.random.default_rng(3) and \(\sigma=20\) m the maximum distance error drops below 1 m after 1.17 s and the final distances are \(100,100,100,100,141.4\) m (verified).
Exercise 5 (derivation). Prove that under the distance law the centroid \(\bar p=\frac1N\sum_ip_i\) is constant, and that the law is invariant under \(p_i\mapsto Qp_i+c\) for any orthogonal \(Q\) and vector \(c\).
Show solution
Each edge \((i,j)\) contributes \(-k_de_k(p_i-p_j)\) to \(u_i\) and \(-k_de_k(p_j-p_i)\) to \(u_j\); the sum is zero, so \(\sum_iu_i=0\) and \(\dot{\bar p}=0\) (the example's centroid \((-43.3,26.7)\) m is indeed unchanged). Under \(p_i\mapsto Qp_i+c\): \(\|Q(p_i-p_j)\|=\|p_i-p_j\|\) so every \(e_k\) is unchanged, and \(u_i\mapsto Qu_i\); hence if \(p(t)\) is a solution so is \(Qp(t)+c\). The law therefore cannot distinguish a formation from its rotated, translated or reflected copies.

12. Key Takeaways & Formula Sheet

  • Displacement law \(u_i=-k_c\sum_jw_{ij}[(p_i-p_j)-d_{ij}]\) with \(d_{ij}=r_i-r_j\): the error \(e_i=p_i-r_i\) obeys \(\dot e=-k_c(L\otimes I_n)e\); formation up to translation iff a directed spanning tree exists; rate \(k_c\lambda_2\) (undirected).
  • Pinning \(-k_pb_i(p_i-p_0-r_i)\) fixes the placement: \(\dot e=-(H_k\otimes I)e\), \(H_k=k_cL+k_pB\succ0\).
  • Second order: \(\ddot e=-k_p(H\otimes I)e-k_v(H\otimes I)\dot e\), modes \(\omega_{n,k}=\sqrt{k_p\mu_k}\), \(\zeta_k=k_v\sqrt{\mu_k}/(2\sqrt{k_p})\).
  • Moving formation: \(d_{ij}(t)=R(\psi_c)(r_i-r_j)\), \(\dot d_{ij}=\omega_cR(\psi_c)J(r_i-r_j)\); with slot-velocity feedforward \(v_i^d\) the error dynamics are autonomous; without it the lag is \(e_{ss}=-(H_k^{-1}\mathbf 1)\otimes v_d\), i.e. \(V/k\) per UAV (120 m at 60 m/s, \(k=0.5\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}\)).
  • Distance law \(u_i=-k_d\sum_j(\|p_i-p_j\|^2-\bar d_{ij}^2)(p_i-p_j)\), i.e. \(\dot p=-k_dR(p)^Te\), gradient of \(V=\tfrac14\|e\|^2\); \(\dot e=-2k_dRR^Te\); locally exponentially stable iff the target is infinitesimally rigid, rate \(2k_d\lambda_{\min}(RR^T)\); invariant to rotation/translation/reflection; centroid fixed; undesired saddle equilibria (collinear).
  • Fixed-wing mapping: \(\chi^c=\operatorname{atan2}(u_e,u_n)\), \(V_g^c=\operatorname{clip}(\|u\|,V_{\min},V_{\max})\); saturate the correction, keep \(k_c\lambda_N+k_p\) below the course-loop bandwidth, respect Part 10's turn feasibility, stack altitudes.
