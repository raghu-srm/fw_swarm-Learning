1. Why This Matters

Formation control (Parts 10–13) holds a prescribed shape. Many missions do not need one: a transit through unknown airspace, a search over an area, a loose escort. What they need is cohesion (stay together), separation (never too close) and alignment (fly the same way) — the three rules Reynolds (1987) used to animate bird flocks. For twenty years those rules were heuristics. Olfati-Saber (2006) turned them into a control law with a Lyapunov function: a flock is second-order consensus (Part 9) on velocity plus a gradient of a carefully shaped potential on position, and a navigation term that keeps the group from splitting.

\[ u_i=\underbrace{\sum_j\phi_\alpha(\|q_j-q_i\|_\sigma)\,n_{ij}}_{\text{separation + cohesion}}+\underbrace{\sum_ja_{ij}(q)\,(p_j-p_i)}_{\text{alignment (consensus)}}+\underbrace{(-c_1(q_i-q_r)-c_2(p_i-p_r))}_{\text{navigation}} \]
InputSecond-order consensus and the acceleration projection (Part 9), switching graphs (Part 6), the turn-rate envelope (Part 16), gradients (A1 §13).
OutputImplement Algorithm 2 from its definitions, explain each design choice, read the Hamiltonian argument, and size a flock's lattice for a given aircraft envelope.
Used inPart 20 (hard separation versus soft potentials), Part 24 §17 (flocking as an alternative formation layer).
Notation. This part uses Olfati-Saber's symbols so that the formulas match the paper: \(q_i\) is position and \(p_i\) is velocity. Elsewhere in the course they are \(p_i\) and \(v_i\). The navigation target (the "\(\gamma\)-agent") has state \((q_r,p_r)\).

2. From Reynolds' Rules to a Control Law

Reynolds ruleOlfati-Saber termMechanism
Separation: avoid crowding neighboursgradient term \(\sum_j\phi_\alpha(\|q_j-q_i\|_\sigma)n_{ij}\)repulsive when closer than \(d\), attractive when farther, zero at \(d\) and beyond the range \(r\)
Cohesion: steer toward the local centre(same potential, attractive side)
Alignment: match neighbours' velocityconsensus term \(\sum_ja_{ij}(q)(p_j-p_i)\)velocity consensus over the proximity graph (Parts 5, 9)
— (not in Reynolds)navigation term \(-c_1(q_i-q_r)-c_2(p_i-p_r)\)a virtual target every agent tracks; prevents fragmentation

Three kinds of agents appear: α-agents are the flock members; the γ-agent is the navigation target (a virtual leader in the sense of Part 13); β-agents are virtual agents projected onto obstacles, used for obstacle avoidance (Algorithm 3, not treated here — Part 20 handles collisions with guarantees).

The whole construction is a gradient-plus-damping system: \(u=-\nabla V(q)-\text{(velocity damping to neighbours and target)}\), which is why a Lyapunov function exists (Section 5). The engineering is in the shape of \(V\).

3. The Toolkit: σ-Norm, Bump, Action Function

Parameters used throughout (Olfati-Saber's example values): \(\varepsilon=0.1\), desired spacing \(d=7\), interaction range \(r=\kappa d=8.4\) (\(\kappa=1.2\)), \(a=b=5\), \(h=0.2\).

3.1 The σ-norm

\[ \|z\|_\sigma=\frac1\varepsilon\Big[\sqrt{1+\varepsilon\|z\|^2}-1\Big],\qquad \nabla_z\|z\|_\sigma=\frac{z}{\sqrt{1+\varepsilon\|z\|^2}}=\frac{z}{1+\varepsilon\|z\|_\sigma}=:\sigma_\varepsilon(z). \]

It is not a norm (no homogeneity) but it is differentiable everywhere, including at \(z=0\), unlike \(\|z\|\) — which is what lets the potential be smooth when two agents coincide. Its gradient \(\sigma_\varepsilon(z)\) is bounded by \(1/\sqrt\varepsilon=3.16\), so forces never blow up. Numerically: \(\|z\|_\sigma=8.71,\ 11.45,\ 14.29,\ 17.20\) for \(\|z\|=5,6,7,8\); \(d_\alpha=\|d\|_\sigma=14.29\), \(r_\alpha=\|r\|_\sigma=18.38\). The gradient identity checks to \(10^{-9}\) by finite differences.

3.2 The bump function and the proximity graph

\[ \rho_h(z)=\begin{cases}1,&0\le z\lt h\\ \tfrac12\big[1+\cos\big(\pi\tfrac{z-h}{1-h}\big)\big],&h\le z\le1\\ 0,&z\gt1\end{cases},\qquad a_{ij}(q)=\rho_h\!\Big(\frac{\|q_j-q_i\|_\sigma}{r_\alpha}\Big)\in[0,1]. \]

\(\rho_h\) is 1 up to a fraction \(h\) of the range, then falls smoothly to 0 at the range. With \(h=0.2\): \(\rho_h(0.5)=0.691\), \(\rho_h(0.6)=0.5\), \(\rho_h(0.9)=0.038\), \(\rho_h(1)=0\). The spatial adjacency \(a_{ij}(q)\) defines a proximity graph that changes continuously with the positions — a smoothly switching topology (Part 6) with weights that vanish as agents separate, so no term in \(u_i\) jumps when a neighbour leaves range.

3.3 The action function

\[ \sigma_1(z)=\frac{z}{\sqrt{1+z^2}},\qquad \phi(z)=\tfrac12\big[(a+b)\,\sigma_1(z+c)+(a-b)\big],\quad c=\frac{|a-b|}{\sqrt{4ab}},\qquad \boxed{\;\phi_\alpha(z)=\rho_h\!\Big(\frac z{r_\alpha}\Big)\,\phi(z-d_\alpha)\;} \]

\(\phi\) is an uneven sigmoid (parameters \(0\lt a\le b\)) with \(\phi(0)=0\) — the constant \(c\) is chosen for exactly that — and limits \(-b\) and \(+a\) (here \(\mp5\), \(c=0\) since \(a=b\)); shifted by \(d_\alpha\) it is negative (repulsive) for \(z\lt d_\alpha\) and positive (attractive) beyond; multiplied by the bump it vanishes beyond \(r_\alpha\). Its integral is the pairwise potential \(\psi_\alpha(z)=\int_{d_\alpha}^z\phi_\alpha(s)\,ds\), minimised at \(z=d_\alpha\). The force on agent \(i\) from \(j\) is \(\phi_\alpha(\|q_j-q_i\|_\sigma)\,n_{ij}\) with \(n_{ij}=\sigma_\varepsilon(q_j-q_i)\):

4. Algorithms 1 and 2

\[ \textbf{Algorithm 1:}\quad u_i=\sum_{j\in\mathcal N_i}\phi_\alpha(\|q_j-q_i\|_\sigma)\,n_{ij}+\sum_{j\in\mathcal N_i}a_{ij}(q)\,(p_j-p_i) \] \[ \boxed{\;\textbf{Algorithm 2:}\quad u_i=\sum_{j\in\mathcal N_i}\phi_\alpha(\|q_j-q_i\|_\sigma)\,n_{ij}+\sum_{j\in\mathcal N_i}a_{ij}(q)\,(p_j-p_i)-c_1\,(q_i-q_r)-c_2\,(p_i-p_r)\;} \]

with \(\mathcal N_i=\{j:\|q_j-q_i\|\lt r\}\), double-integrator agents \(\dot q_i=p_i\), \(\dot p_i=u_i\), and \(c_1,c_2\gt0\) (here \(0.4\) and \(1.2\)). The γ-agent follows its own trajectory \((q_r(t),p_r(t))\) — a waypoint path, a loiter, or the virtual structure of Part 13.

α-lattice and quasi-lattice. A configuration is an α-lattice if \(\|q_j-q_i\|=d\) for every edge of the proximity graph; a quasi-α-lattice if \(|\|q_j-q_i\|-d|\le\delta\) for a small \(\delta\). The deviation energy \(E(q)=\frac{1}{|E|+1}\sum_{(i,j)\in E}(\|q_j-q_i\|-d)^2\) measures the distance from a lattice.

Why Algorithm 1 is not enough. Without the navigation term, agents only interact within range \(r\). A group that starts spread out, or is split by a disturbance, has no force pulling the pieces together: it fragments into sub-flocks that each flock perfectly and drift apart. Olfati-Saber's paper identified this as the central failure of naive Reynolds implementations. Section 6 reproduces it.

5. Why It Works: Hamiltonian, Centre of Mass, Collisions

5.1 The moving frame and the Hamiltonian

Write \(x_i=q_i-q_r\), \(v_i=p_i-p_r\) (positions and velocities relative to the γ-agent, which here moves at constant velocity). Algorithm 2 becomes \(\dot v_i=-\nabla_{x_i}U(x)-\sum_ja_{ij}(v_i-v_j)-c_2v_i\) with the collective potential

\[ U(x)=\tfrac12\sum_i\sum_{j\ne i}\psi_\alpha(\|x_j-x_i\|_\sigma)+\tfrac{c_1}2\sum_i\|x_i\|^2,\qquad H(x,v)=U(x)+\tfrac12\sum_i\|v_i\|^2 . \]
Theorem (Olfati-Saber 2006, Thm. 2 and Lemma 3, adapted). Along Algorithm 2 with a constant-velocity γ-agent, \[ \dot H=-v^T\big(\hat L(q)\otimes I_2+c_2I\big)v\le0, \] where \(\hat L(q)\) is the Laplacian of the proximity graph. Hence \(H(t)\le H(0)\): the flock is cohesive (positions stay in a ball around \(q_r\) of radius \(\sqrt{2H(0)/c_1}\)), the velocities match the γ-agent asymptotically (LaSalle on \(v=0\)), and the configuration converges to a local minimum of \(U\) — an α-lattice or quasi-lattice.
Proof sketch. \(\dot H=\sum_i\nabla_{x_i}U\cdot v_i+\sum_iv_i\cdot\dot v_i\); the potential terms cancel (gradient system), leaving the damping terms, which are the negative of the Laplacian quadratic form of Part 3 plus \(c_2\|v\|^2\). \(\square\)

Simulation check (Section 6): \(H=545.3\) at \(t=0\), \(217.8\) at 10 s, \(217.5\) at 30 s and thereafter; the largest increase between samples is \(6\times10^{-14}\) (round-off).

5.2 The centre of mass follows a damped second-order system

Summing Algorithm 2 over \(i\): the potential forces cancel pairwise (\(\phi_\alpha\) is odd under \(i\leftrightarrow j\), \(n_{ji}=-n_{ij}\)) and so does the consensus term (symmetric \(a_{ij}\)), leaving

\[ \ddot q_c=-c_1(q_c-q_r)-c_2(\dot q_c-p_r),\qquad \omega_n=\sqrt{c_1}=0.632,\quad \zeta=\frac{c_2}{2\sqrt{c_1}}=0.949 . \]

The flock's centre tracks the γ-agent exactly like a mass on a spring-damper, independently of all interactions. The simulation reproduces this to \(10^{-13}\) over 60 s. Choosing \(c_1,c_2\) is therefore ordinary second-order tuning (Part 9), and the centre's settling time is \(\approx4/(\zeta\omega_n)=6.7\) time units.

5.3 No collisions from benign starts

Let \(c_{\text{coll}}=\psi_\alpha(0)=\int_{d_\alpha}^0\phi_\alpha(s)\,ds\) be the energy of one pair at zero distance (here \(52.4\)). Since \(H\) never increases and each colliding pair costs at least \(c_{\text{coll}}\), if \(H(0)\lt c_{\text{coll}}\) no two agents ever collide, and in general at most \(\lfloor H(0)/c_{\text{coll}}\rfloor\) pairs can (here \(10\)). This is a soft guarantee — it bounds energy, not distance — and it depends on the initial condition, which is why the capstone backs it with the hard barrier of Part 20.

7. Fixed-Wing Adaptation

7.1 Projecting the acceleration

As in Part 9, the aircraft realises \(u_i\) through its speed and course loops: \(\dot V_i=u_i\cdot\hat t_i\), \(\dot\chi_i=\operatorname{clip}\big((u_i\cdot\hat n_i)/V_i,\pm\omega_{\max}\big)\), \(V_i\in[V_{\min},V_{\max}]\). Repeating the simulation with this unicycle model (dimensionless \(V\in[1,2.5]\), \(\omega_{\max}=0.5\), target speed 1.5) gives, for two seeds, one component with 23–24 edges, minimum distance 6.3–6.6, maximum edge deviation 0.37–0.71 and zero velocity mismatch at 150 s. The flock forms despite the constraints because the γ-agent's speed sits inside the envelope and the lattice scale is large compared with the turn radius \(V/\omega_{\max}=3\) — the two conditions that matter.

7.2 Alignment is course synchronisation

For equal speeds \(V\), the consensus term's components along and across agent \(i\)'s course are

\[ (p_j-p_i)\cdot\hat n_i=V\sin(\chi_j-\chi_i),\qquad (p_j-p_i)\cdot\hat t_i=V\big(\cos(\chi_j-\chi_i)-1\big)\le0 . \]

So the cross-track part is a sine-coupled heading consensus — the Kuramoto form of Part 19 — which turns the aircraft toward its neighbours' courses, while the along-track part always slows an aircraft whose course differs from its neighbours'. Alignment of fixed-wing flocks therefore happens through turning; speed only adjusts spacing.

7.3 Sizing the lattice from the envelope

A potential can only separate two aircraft that have time to turn. Two aircraft closing head-on at \(2V\) must generate a lateral offset \(d_s\) before they meet; at lateral acceleration \(a_{\mathrm{lat}}=g\tan\phi_{\max}\) that takes \(t_s=\sqrt{2d_s/a_{\mathrm{lat}}}\) (small-angle; the exact offset at 25 m/s is 27.7 m against a 30 m target). Adding the communication latency \(\tau\), the interaction range must satisfy

\[ \boxed{\;r\ \ge\ 2V\,(\tau+t_s),\qquad d=r/\kappa\;} \]

Two further adaptations follow from the envelope: anisotropic spacing — along-track conflicts are resolved by speed changes, which are slow and weak at high speed (Part 16), so the along-track spacing should be larger than the cross-track one (stretch the σ-norm's metric along \(\hat t\)); and a hard barrier underneath — the potential's no-collision bound is energetic and initial-condition dependent, so the safety filter of Part 20 runs after the flocking law in the capstone.

High-speed note. The lattice spacing grows faster than linearly with speed (\(t_s\) is fixed by \(d_s\) and \(g\tan\phi_{\max}\), so \(r\approx2Vt_s\)): a kilometre at 150 m/s, over two kilometres at 250 m/s — and the ratio \(r/R_{\min}\) drops below one. The flock is then "stiffer" than the aircraft: an agent inside its neighbour's interaction range cannot complete a turn before leaving it, and the potential forces act over a time comparable with the turn itself. Flocking at high speed is a loose, slowly-reacting transit mode; formation slots (Part 13) or along-path spacing (Part 19) are the precise modes.
Toward the CLAW Flocking is the capstone's alternative formation layer (Part 24 §17): Algorithm 2 replaces L2 with the synchronised reference \(\hat\xi_i\) of Part 13 acting as the γ-agent, the proximity graph replacing the fixed formation graph, and L3 (the CBF filter) and L4 (projection and envelope clipping, Section 7.1) unchanged. The sizing rule of Section 7.3 sets \(d\) and \(r\) from \(V\), \(\phi_{\max}\), \(\tau\) and \(d_s\); the centre-of-mass result of Section 5.2 is what guarantees the flock follows the reference with ordinary second-order tuning of \(c_1,c_2\).

9. Implementation

Algorithm 2 in vectorised NumPy (the Section 6 numbers come from this script). Olfati-Saber's symbols: \(q\) positions, \(p\) velocities.

import numpy as np

# ---- parameters (dimensionless, as in Olfati-Saber 2006) ----
N, d, kappa, eps, a, b, h = 12, 7.0, 1.2, 0.1, 5.0, 5.0, 0.2
c1, c2 = 0.4, 1.2                    # gamma-agent (navigation) gains
r = kappa*d                          # interaction range

def sigma_norm(z):                   # ||z||_sigma, z: (..., 2)
    return (np.sqrt(1 + eps*np.sum(z**2, axis=-1)) - 1)/eps
def rho_h(z):                        # bump function
    return np.where(z < h, 1.0, np.where(z <= 1.0, 0.5*(1 + np.cos(np.pi*(z - h)/(1 - h))), 0.0))
def sigma_1(z):
    return z/np.sqrt(1 + z**2)
def phi(z):                          # uneven sigmoid, phi(0) = 0
    c = abs(a - b)/np.sqrt(4*a*b)
    return 0.5*((a + b)*sigma_1(z + c) + (a - b))
d_alpha, r_alpha = sigma_norm(np.array([d, 0.0])), sigma_norm(np.array([r, 0.0]))
def phi_alpha(z):                    # action function
    return rho_h(z/r_alpha)*phi(z - d_alpha)

def algorithm2(q, p, q_r, p_r):
    """u_i = sum_j phi_alpha(||q_j-q_i||_sigma) n_ij + sum_j a_ij (p_j-p_i) - c1 (q_i-q_r) - c2 (p_i-p_r)."""
    dq = q[None, :, :] - q[:, None, :]           # dq[i,j] = q_j - q_i
    dist2 = np.sum(dq**2, axis=-1)
    z = (np.sqrt(1 + eps*dist2) - 1)/eps         # sigma-norms
    n_ij = dq/np.sqrt(1 + eps*dist2)[..., None]  # sigma-gradient
    A = rho_h(z/r_alpha); np.fill_diagonal(A, 0.0)
    f = phi_alpha(z); np.fill_diagonal(f, 0.0)
    u_grad = np.sum(f[..., None]*n_ij, axis=1)
    u_cons = np.sum(A[..., None]*(p[None, :, :] - p[:, None, :]), axis=1)
    u_nav = -c1*(q - q_r) - c2*(p - p_r)
    return u_grad + u_cons + u_nav, A

def simulate(T=60.0, dt=0.01, seed=1, nav=True):
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    q = rng.uniform(-15, 15, size=(N, 2)); p = rng.normal(0, 0.5, size=(N, 2))
    q_r, p_r = np.array([0.0, 0.0]), np.array([1.5, 0.0])   # gamma-agent: constant velocity
    for k in range(int(round(T/dt))):
        u, A = algorithm2(q, p, q_r, p_r)
        if not nav: u += c1*(q - q_r) + c2*(p - p_r)         # Algorithm 1: drop the gamma term
        p = p + dt*u; q = q + dt*p; q_r = q_r + dt*p_r       # semi-implicit Euler
    return q, p, q_r, p_r, A

if __name__ == "__main__":
    q, p, q_r, p_r, A = simulate()
    dist = np.linalg.norm(q[:, None, :] - q[None, :, :], axis=-1)
    edges = (A > 0) & ~np.eye(N, dtype=bool)
    print("edges in proximity graph:", edges.sum()//2)
    print("min pairwise distance: %.3f  (d = %.1f)" % (dist[~np.eye(N, dtype=bool)].min(), d))
    print("max |dist - d| over edges: %.4f" % np.abs(dist[edges] - d).max())
    print("max velocity mismatch |p_i - p_r|: %.2e" % np.linalg.norm(p - p_r, axis=1).max())
    print("centre of mass - q_r:", (q.mean(axis=0) - q_r).round(4))

10. Common Pitfalls

  • Omitting the navigation term (Algorithm 1) and blaming the gains for fragmentation.
  • Using \(\|z\|\) instead of \(\|z\|_\sigma\). The potential is then non-smooth at zero distance and forces are unbounded; two coinciding agents get an undefined direction.
  • Hard-cutting the neighbourhood (\(a_{ij}\in\{0,1\}\)) instead of the bump: the control input jumps whenever an agent crosses \(r\), which the autopilot turns into chatter.
  • Dimensional confusion. Olfati-Saber's example is dimensionless; scale \(d,r\) to metres by Section 7.3 and \(c_1,c_2\) by the centre-of-mass tuning (\(\omega_n=\sqrt{c_1}\) in rad/s).
  • Treating the no-collision bound as a safety guarantee. It is an energy bound; sharp initial conditions or external disturbances can violate it. Use the CBF filter (Part 20).
  • A γ-agent outside the envelope. If \(\|p_r\|\lt V_{\min}\) or the γ-agent turns tighter than \(R_{\min}\), fixed-wing flocks orbit and oscillate around it; make the γ-agent a flyable virtual structure (Part 13).
  • Isotropic spacing at high speed. Along-track closure is resolved by weak speed control; give it more room than the cross-track direction.

11. Exercises

Exercise 1 (action function). Show that \(\phi(0)=0\) for \(0\lt a\le b\) with \(c=|a-b|/\sqrt{4ab}\), hence that \(\phi_\alpha(z)=0\) exactly at \(z=d_\alpha\) and for all \(z\ge r_\alpha\), and that the pairwise potential \(\psi_\alpha\) has its minimum at \(d_\alpha\).
Show solution
\(\sigma_1(c)=c/\sqrt{1+c^2}\) with \(c^2=(a-b)^2/(4ab)\), so \(1+c^2=(a+b)^2/(4ab)\) and \(\sigma_1(c)=\dfrac{|a-b|}{a+b}=\dfrac{b-a}{a+b}\) for \(a\le b\). Then \(\phi(0)=\tfrac12[(a+b)\sigma_1(c)+(a-b)]=\tfrac12[(b-a)+(a-b)]=0\). (For \(a=b\), \(c=0\) and \(\phi(0)=0\) trivially; the paper assumes \(0\lt a\le b\).) Hence \(\phi_\alpha(d_\alpha)=\rho_h(d_\alpha/r_\alpha)\,\phi(0)=0\), and \(\rho_h(z/r_\alpha)=0\) for \(z\ge r_\alpha\) kills \(\phi_\alpha\) beyond the range. Since \(\psi_\alpha'=\phi_\alpha\) is negative for \(z\lt d_\alpha\) and positive for \(d_\alpha\lt z\lt r_\alpha\), \(d_\alpha\) is the minimum of \(\psi_\alpha\).
Exercise 2 (σ-norm). With \(\varepsilon=0.1\), compute \(\|z\|_\sigma\) and \(\|\sigma_\varepsilon(z)\|\) for \(\|z\|=10\), and compare the latter with its asymptote.
Show solution
\(\|z\|_\sigma=(\sqrt{1+10}-1)/0.1=23.17\); \(\|\sigma_\varepsilon(z)\|=10/\sqrt{11}=3.015\), against the bound \(1/\sqrt{0.1}=3.162\). Forces saturate: a far-away neighbour pulls no harder than a moderately far one.
Exercise 3 (bump). Evaluate \(\rho_{0.2}(0.75)\) and explain what it means for a neighbour at \(0.75\,r_\alpha\) in σ-distance.
Show solution
\(\rho_h(0.75)=\tfrac12[1+\cos(\pi\cdot0.55/0.8)]=0.222\): that neighbour contributes 22 % of a full-weight edge to the alignment term and its action force is scaled by 0.222.
Exercise 4 (centre of mass). For \(c_1=0.4\), \(c_2=1.2\), give the natural frequency, damping ratio and approximate settling time of the flock's centre, and choose \(c_1,c_2\) for a 60 m/s swarm whose centre should settle in 20 s with \(\zeta=0.9\).
Show solution
\(\omega_n=0.632\), \(\zeta=0.949\), \(t_s\approx4/(\zeta\omega_n)=6.7\). For \(t_s=20\) s: \(\zeta\omega_n=0.2\Rightarrow\omega_n=0.222\) rad/s, \(c_1=\omega_n^2=0.049\ \mathrm{s^{-2}}\), \(c_2=2\zeta\omega_n=0.4\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}\). (A 100 m offset then demands \(c_1\cdot100=4.9\) m/s² — check against Part 9's envelope table.)
Exercise 5 (lattice sizing). A 60 m/s swarm with \(\phi_{\max}=45^\circ\), \(d_s=60\) m and \(\tau=0.1\) s. Size \(r\) and \(d\), and compare \(r\) with the minimum turn radius.
Show solution
\(a_{\mathrm{lat}}=9.81\) m/s², \(t_s=\sqrt{120/9.81}=3.50\) s, \(r\ge2\cdot60\cdot3.6=432\) m, \(d=360\) m; \(R_{\min}=367\) m, so \(r/R_{\min}=1.2\): an agent can just about complete a turn inside its interaction range.

12. Key Takeaways & Formula Sheet

  • σ-norm \(\|z\|_\sigma=(\sqrt{1+\varepsilon\|z\|^2}-1)/\varepsilon\), gradient \(\sigma_\varepsilon(z)=z/\sqrt{1+\varepsilon\|z\|^2}\) bounded by \(1/\sqrt\varepsilon\).
  • Bump \(\rho_h\); proximity weights \(a_{ij}(q)=\rho_h(\|q_j-q_i\|_\sigma/r_\alpha)\); action \(\phi_\alpha(z)=\rho_h(z/r_\alpha)\phi(z-d_\alpha)\), \(\phi(z)=\tfrac12[(a+b)\sigma_1(z+c)+(a-b)]\).
  • Algorithm 2: \(u_i=\sum_j\phi_\alpha n_{ij}+\sum_ja_{ij}(p_j-p_i)-c_1(q_i-q_r)-c_2(p_i-p_r)\); Algorithm 1 (no γ-term) fragments.
  • Hamiltonian \(H=U+\tfrac12\|v\|^2\) non-increasing ⇒ cohesion, velocity matching, quasi-lattice; centre of mass: \(\ddot q_c=-c_1(q_c-q_r)-c_2(\dot q_c-p_r)\); at most \(\lfloor H(0)/\psi_\alpha(0)\rfloor\) colliding pairs.
  • Fixed-wing: project \(u_i\) to \(\dot V,\dot\chi\) with envelope clipping; alignment = \(V\sin(\chi_j-\chi_i)\) heading consensus; lattice \(r\ge2V(\tau+\sqrt{2d_s/(g\tan\phi_{\max})})\); anisotropic spacing; CBF underneath.
