1. Why This Matters

Part 5 solved \(\dot x=-kLx\) exactly and showed that a connected swarm reaches the average of its initial values. That solution assumed four things no real swarm has: continuous time, a fixed graph, zero latency and noise-free links. A radio-linked fixed-wing swarm has a 5–20 Hz packet schedule, links that drop as aircraft bank and separate, 100–300 ms of latency, and measurement noise on everything. This part asks, for each of those, does consensus survive, how fast, and to what value?

\[ \text{ideal: } \dot x=-kLx \quad\longrightarrow\quad \text{real: } x[k+1]=\big(I-T_sk\,L_{\sigma(k)}\big)x[k]+\text{delays}+\text{noise} \]
InputLaplacian, eigenvalues and the disagreement vector (Parts 3–5); Lyapunov's direct method (Appendix A1).
OutputA proof technique you will reuse in every later stability argument, and a gain-selection recipe: pick \(k\) from \(\lambda_N\), the sample time and the latency.
Used inEvery sampled implementation from here on: Part 8 (estimator), Part 12, Part 19, Part 24 (checks D3–D4).

Throughout this part the graph is undirected and weighted, with \(L=D-A\) symmetric; directed graphs follow in Part 7.

2. Lyapunov Proof of Consensus

Part 5 proved convergence by diagonalising \(L\). That works for a fixed symmetric Laplacian and for nothing else. The Lyapunov argument below needs no eigenvectors, which is why it survives switching graphs, sampling and perturbations.

Lyapunov's direct method (statement). Let \(\dot z=f(z)\) with \(f(0)=0\). If a continuously differentiable \(V(z)\) satisfies \(V(0)=0\), \(V(z)\gt0\) for \(z\ne0\), and \(\dot V(z)=\nabla V\cdot f(z)\le-cV(z)\) for some \(c\gt0\), then \(\|z(t)\|\to0\) exponentially. If only \(\dot V\le0\) holds, LaSalle's invariance principle says \(z(t)\) converges to the largest invariant set inside \(\{\dot V=0\}\) (A1 §10).

2.1 The disagreement vector as the state

The consensus state itself does not go to zero (it goes to \(\bar x\mathbf 1\)), so we work with the disagreement vector of Part 5:

\[ \delta = x-\bar x\mathbf 1 = P x,\qquad P=I-\tfrac1N\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^T,\qquad \mathbf 1^T\delta=0,\qquad \dot\delta=P\dot x=-PLx=-L\delta . \]

(\(PL=LP=L\) because \(L\mathbf 1=0\) and \(\mathbf 1^TL=0\) for symmetric \(L\).) Consensus means \(\delta\to0\).

2.2 The candidate and its derivative

\[ V(\delta)=\tfrac12\delta^T\delta,\qquad \dot V=\delta^T\dot\delta=-\delta^TL\delta . \]

By the quadratic-form identity of Part 3, \(\delta^TL\delta=\tfrac12\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}(\delta_i-\delta_j)^2\ge0\), so \(\dot V\le0\): the disagreement energy never grows. To get a rate we need a lower bound on \(\delta^TL\delta\), and that is exactly what the Courant–Fischer theorem gives on the subspace orthogonal to \(\mathbf 1\):

Lemma (Rayleigh bound on \(\mathbf 1^\perp\)). For symmetric \(L\) with eigenvalues \(0=\lambda_1\le\lambda_2\le\dots\le\lambda_N\) and eigenvector \(\mathbf 1\) for \(\lambda_1\), \[ \delta^TL\delta\;\ge\;\lambda_2\,\|\delta\|^2\qquad\text{for all }\delta\perp\mathbf 1 . \] Proof. Expand \(\delta=\sum_{i\ge2}c_iv_i\) in the orthonormal eigenvectors (no \(v_1\) component because \(\delta\perp\mathbf 1\)). Then \(\delta^TL\delta=\sum_{i\ge2}\lambda_ic_i^2\ge\lambda_2\sum_{i\ge2}c_i^2=\lambda_2\|\delta\|^2\). \(\square\)

Therefore

\[ \boxed{\;\dot V\le-\lambda_2\|\delta\|^2=-2\lambda_2V\quad\Longrightarrow\quad V(t)\le e^{-2\lambda_2t}V(0),\quad \|\delta(t)\|\le e^{-\lambda_2t}\|\delta(0)\|\;} \]
Interpretation: if the graph is connected (\(\lambda_2\gt0\)) the swarm's disagreement decays at least as fast as \(e^{-\lambda_2t}\), whatever the initial condition. With gain \(k\) replace \(\lambda_2\) by \(k\lambda_2\). This is the same bound Part 5 found, now proved without any eigenvector of the trajectory — only the smallest non-zero eigenvalue of \(L\) was used.

2.3 A second Lyapunov function: the disagreement energy

\(E(x)=\tfrac12x^TLx\) also works: \(\dot E=x^TL\dot x=-x^TL^2x=-\|Lx\|^2\le0\), and on \(\mathbf 1^\perp\), \(\|Lx\|^2\ge\lambda_2\,x^TLx\), so \(E(t)\le e^{-2\lambda_2t}E(0)\). \(E\) is the quantity a swarm can actually monitor: each UAV knows its own term \(\tfrac12\sum_ja_{ij}(x_i-x_j)^2\).

Check on the Part 5 star (\(x(0)=[10,14,8]^T\), \(\lambda_2=1\)): \(V(0)=9.33\), \(\dot V(0)=-20.0\le-\lambda_2\|\delta(0)\|^2=-18.7\) ✓; \(E(0)=x^TLx/2=10\); at \(t=0.5\) and \(1\) s, \(x^TLx=6.72\) and \(2.44\), both under the bound \(20e^{-2t}=7.36\) and \(2.71\) ✓.

2.4 Why this proof generalises

The argument used only three facts: \(L\mathbf 1=0\), \(L=L^T\succeq0\), and \(\lambda_2\gt0\). Any family of Laplacians with these properties shares the same \(V\). That is the key to Section 6 (switching) and the reason Lyapunov functions, not eigen-decompositions, carry the rest of the course.

3. Convergence Rate and Settling Time

The slowest disagreement mode decays as \(e^{-k\lambda_2t}\) (Part 4). In control-engineering terms:

\[ \tau_c=\frac{1}{k\lambda_2},\qquad t_{2\%}\approx\frac{4}{k\lambda_2},\qquad t_{1\%}=\frac{\ln 100}{k\lambda_2}=\frac{4.61}{k\lambda_2}. \]

These are worst-case figures; a particular initial condition can settle faster if it has little content in the slow mode. For the star with \(k=1\) the bound predicts \(t_{2\%}=4\) s and the exact trajectory reaches 2 % at \(3.89\) s.

3.1 The trade-off hidden in \(\lambda_N\)

Raising \(k\) speeds up every mode, but Sections 4–7 will show that \(k\lambda_N\) is capped by the sample time and the latency. So the achievable rate is governed by the spectral ratio \(\lambda_N/\lambda_2\): the stiffer the graph, the more the cap on the fastest mode throttles the slowest one.

4. Discrete-Time Consensus

Radios deliver packets at discrete instants, so the law every UAV actually runs is an update, not a differential equation:

\[ \boxed{\;x_i[k+1]=x_i[k]-\varepsilon\sum_{j\in\mathcal N_i}a_{ij}\big(x_i[k]-x_j[k]\big)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x[k+1]=(I-\varepsilon L)\,x[k]=:P\,x[k]\;} \]

\(P=I-\varepsilon L\) is called the Perron matrix of the graph. Its eigenvectors are those of \(L\) and its eigenvalues are

\[ \mu_i=1-\varepsilon\lambda_i,\qquad \mu_1=1 \text{ (eigenvector } \mathbf 1\text{)} . \]

4.1 Stability condition

Writing \(x[k]=\sum_ic_i\mu_i^kv_i\), every disagreement mode must satisfy \(|\mu_i|\lt1\):

\[ |1-\varepsilon\lambda_i|\lt1\ \ (i\ge2)\iff 0\lt\varepsilon\lt\frac{2}{\lambda_i}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{\;0\lt\varepsilon\lt\frac{2}{\lambda_N}\;} \]

A UAV rarely knows \(\lambda_N\) of the whole swarm, but it knows its own degree. Gershgorin's theorem (A1 §6) gives \(\lambda_N\le2d_{\max}\), hence the local sufficient condition

\[ \boxed{\;\varepsilon\lt\frac{1}{d_{\max}}\;} \]

which also makes \(P\) entrywise non-negative (\(P_{ii}=1-\varepsilon d_i\ge0\)). Then \(P\) is doubly stochastic — rows and columns sum to one because \(L\mathbf 1=0\) and \(\mathbf 1^TL=0\) — and three things follow at once: \(x[k+1]\) is a convex combination of neighbours' values (no overshoot past the extremes), the average \(\tfrac1N\mathbf 1^Tx[k]\) is conserved, and \(P^k\to\tfrac1N\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^T\) (average consensus).

4.2 Fastest step size

The slowest disagreement mode has \(|\mu|=\max(|1-\varepsilon\lambda_2|,|1-\varepsilon\lambda_N|)\). Balancing the two (Xiao & Boyd 2004):

\[ \varepsilon^\ast=\frac{2}{\lambda_2+\lambda_N},\qquad \rho^\ast=\frac{\lambda_N-\lambda_2}{\lambda_N+\lambda_2}. \]

For a graph with all non-zero eigenvalues equal (complete graph) \(\varepsilon^\ast=1/\lambda_N\) gives deadbeat consensus in one step.

4.3 Worked example: four UAVs, complete graph, \(T_s=0.1\) s

4.4 Metropolis weights: a distributed, always-safe choice

Instead of one global \(\varepsilon\) each pair can use a weight that needs only the two degrees at its ends:

\[ \boxed{\;w_{ij}=\frac{1}{1+\max(d_i,d_j)}\ \ (j\in\mathcal N_i),\qquad w_{ii}=1-\sum_{j\in\mathcal N_i}w_{ij}\;} \]

\(W\) is symmetric, non-negative and doubly stochastic by construction (Xiao, Boyd & Lall 2005), so it converges to the average on any connected graph without anyone knowing \(\lambda_N\). For the 4-UAV graph with edges 1–2, 1–3, 1–4, 2–3 (degrees \(3,2,2,1\)):

\[ W=\begin{bmatrix}0.25&0.25&0.25&0.25\\0.25&0.4167&0.3333&0\\0.25&0.3333&0.4167&0\\0.25&0&0&0.75\end{bmatrix},\qquad \text{eig}(W)=\{1,\,0.75,\,0.083,\,0\}. \]

Its second-largest \(|\mu|\) is 0.75, against 0.667 for the uniform \(\varepsilon=1/d_{\max}\) and 0.60 for \(\varepsilon^\ast=2/(\lambda_2+\lambda_N)=0.4\) (this graph has \(\lambda=\{0,1,3,4\}\)). From \(x[0]=[4,0,2,10]^T\) the three schemes reach 1 % of the initial deviation in 17, 12 and 10 steps. Metropolis is slower but needs no global information — the usual price of robustness.

5. Sampled Implementation of \(\dot x=-kLx\)

Running the continuous law with a forward-Euler step at the communication period \(T_s\) is the discrete law with \(\varepsilon=T_sk\). The conditions of Section 4 become gain limits:

\[ \boxed{\;T_s\,k\,\lambda_N\lt2\quad(\text{necessary and sufficient}),\qquad T_s\,k\,d_{\max}\lt1\quad(\text{local, sufficient})\;} \]

In practice aim for \(T_sk\lambda_N\le0.5\): the discrete modes then stay within 10 % of their continuous-time rates and small timing jitter cannot push a mode past \(|\mu|=1\). For the 5-UAV ring at 10 Hz this means \(k\le1.38\); with \(k=1\), \(T_sk\lambda_N=0.362\) and the slowest mode contracts by \(|\mu_2|=0.862\) per step, an equivalent continuous rate of \(-\ln(0.862)/0.1=1.49\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}\) versus the ideal \(k\lambda_2=1.38\).

Where the limit really comes from. The autopilot loop usually runs faster than the radio (50 Hz vs 10 Hz). The consensus step must use the communication period, because \(x_j\) only changes when a packet arrives. Integrating at 50 Hz with 10 Hz data does not relax the bound; it just repeats the same stale correction five times, which is equivalent to a larger \(\varepsilon\).

6. Switching Topologies

Banking aircraft shadow their antennas, separation changes link budgets, and packets are lost at random, so the graph at time \(t\) is \(G_{\sigma(t)}\) drawn from a finite set:

\[ \dot x=-L_{\sigma(t)}\,x,\qquad \sigma(t)\in\{1,\dots,m\}. \]

6.1 A common Lyapunov function

Every \(L_\sigma\) is symmetric, positive semidefinite and annihilates \(\mathbf 1\). Hence with the same \(V=\tfrac12\delta^T\delta\) as before, \(\dot V=-\delta^TL_{\sigma(t)}\delta\le0\) for all \(t\), whichever graph is active, and the average is conserved. The disagreement never grows — but \(\dot V=0\) is possible whenever the active graph is disconnected and \(\delta\) is constant on its components, so convergence needs an extra condition.

Theorem (jointly connected switching — Jadbabaie, Lin & Morse 2003; Olfati-Saber & Murray 2004; Ren & Beard 2005). Suppose the switching signal has a dwell time \(\tau_d\gt0\) between switches and there is a \(T\gt0\) such that over every interval \([t,t+T)\) the union of the graphs that were active is connected. Then \(\dot x=-L_{\sigma(t)}x\) reaches average consensus exponentially.
Proof idea. On each interval of length \(T\) the map \(\delta(t)\mapsto\delta(t+T)\) is a product of contractions; because every edge of a connected union graph is active for at least \(\tau_d\), the product strictly shrinks every component of \(\delta\) that is not already constant, so \(V\) drops by a fixed fraction per interval.

Fast switching averages the Laplacians (Exercise 4).

Interpretation for radios: a packet that is lost removes an edge for one period. As long as every link works often enough that the union over a few periods is connected, consensus survives; the price is a convergence rate set by the time-averaged graph. Section 9 quantifies this by simulation.

7. Communication Delays

A neighbour's value arrives \(\tau\) seconds old. There are two different ways to write the resulting law, and they behave very differently.

7.1 Model (i): everything delayed

\[ \dot x_i(t)=\sum_{j\in\mathcal N_i}a_{ij}\big[x_j(t-\tau)-x_i(t-\tau)\big]\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \dot x(t)=-Lx(t-\tau). \]

This is the form that arises when each UAV acts on a relative measurement (a time-stamped difference) that is itself delayed. Projecting on eigenvector \(v_i\) gives the scalar delay equation \(\dot z_i=-\lambda_iz_i(t-\tau)\), whose characteristic equation \(s+\lambda_ie^{-s\tau}=0\) first crosses the imaginary axis at \(s=\mathrm j\omega\) with \(\omega=\lambda_i\) and \(\omega\tau=\pi/2\). The most fragile mode is the fastest one:

Theorem (Olfati-Saber & Murray 2004, Thm. 10). For a connected undirected graph, \(\dot x=-Lx(t-\tau)\) reaches average consensus if and only if \[ \boxed{\;0\le\tau\lt\tau^\ast=\frac{\pi}{2\lambda_N}\;} \] and with gain \(k\), \(\tau\lt\pi/(2k\lambda_N)\).

For the 3-UAV star (\(\lambda_N=3\)) \(\tau^\ast=0.524\) s. Simulating with a constant initial history:

\(\tau\) [s]max deviation at 10 sat 40 sat 70 sbehaviour
0.300.0000.0000.000converges (fast)
0.500.3710.0180.008converges, lightly damped oscillation
0.530.3761.4472.035diverges slowly
0.550.4149.6493.955 → 35.9diverges

The average is still conserved in every case (\(\mathbf 1^T\dot x=0\) because \(\mathbf 1^TL=0\)); what is lost is stability of the disagreement.

7.2 Model (ii): only received data delayed

\[ \dot x_i(t)=\sum_{j\in\mathcal N_i}a_{ij}\big[x_j(t-\tau)-x_i(t)\big] . \]

This is what a UAV does when it compares a neighbour's stale packet with its own current state — the natural implementation. Each UAV then has an undelayed, strictly stabilising self-term \(-d_ix_i(t)\), and the result is far more robust:

Theorem (delay-independent consensus — Moreau 2004; Münz, Papachristodoulou & Allgöwer 2010). For a connected undirected graph, model (ii) reaches consensus for every constant delay \(\tau\ge0\) (and for bounded heterogeneous delays \(\tau_{ij}\)). The consensus value, however, depends on the delay.

Why the value moves. Define \(Q(t)=\sum_ix_i(t)+\sum_id_i\int_{t-\tau}^{t}x_i(s)\,ds\). Differentiating and using \(\sum_i\sum_ja_{ij}x_j(t-\tau)=\sum_jd_jx_j(t-\tau)\) (undirected: column sums are degrees) gives \(\dot Q=0\). Evaluating the conserved \(Q\) at \(t=0\) (constant history) and at consensus \(x=c\mathbf 1\):

\[ \boxed{\;c=\frac{\mathbf 1^Tx(0)+\tau\,d^Tx(0)}{N+\tau\,\mathbf 1^Td}\;} \]

a blend of the plain average (\(\tau\to0\)) and the degree-weighted average (\(\tau\to\infty\)). Star, \(x(0)=[10,14,8]^T\): \(c=10.667,\ 10.600,\ 10.571,\ 10.546,\ 10.522\) for \(\tau=0,\ 0.5,\ 1,\ 2,\ 5\) s; simulation at \(t=60\) s matches to four digits for \(\tau\le2\) and the \(\tau=5\) s run reaches \(10.52\) after 350 s. For regular graphs (all degrees equal) \(c\) is the average for every \(\tau\).

Implementation rule. Always compare the neighbour's stale value with your own current value (model ii), never with your own value from the same stale time (model i). Model (i) buys exact average consensus at the cost of a hard delay limit \(\pi/(2k\lambda_N)\); model (ii) tolerates any delay and loses only a small, computable shift of the final value.

8. Disturbances and Noise

Let each UAV's update be corrupted by a bounded disturbance \(w_i(t)\) (sensor bias, quantisation, an un-modelled control input):

\[ \dot x=-kLx+w\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \dot\delta=-kL\delta+Pw,\qquad \dot{\bar x}=\tfrac1N\mathbf 1^Tw . \]

Two separate effects. The average drifts at the mean disturbance — consensus cannot prevent it, since \(\mathbf 1^T\) sees no coupling. The disagreement stays bounded: with \(V=\tfrac12\delta^T\delta\), \(\dot V\le-k\lambda_2\|\delta\|^2+\|\delta\|\|Pw\|\), which is negative whenever \(\|\delta\|\gt\|Pw\|/(k\lambda_2)\). Hence

\[ \boxed{\;\|\delta(t)\|\le e^{-k\lambda_2t}\|\delta(0)\|+\frac{\sup_s\|Pw(s)\|}{k\lambda_2}\;} \]

— an input-to-state stability (ISS) bound (A1 §10). For a constant \(w\) the exact steady state is \(\delta_{ss}=(kL)^{+}Pw\) (pseudo-inverse). Star, \(k=1\), \(w=[0.5,-0.3,0.1]^T\): \(Pw=[0.4,-0.4,0]^T\), \(\|Pw\|=0.566\), \(\delta_{ss}=[0.133,-0.267,0.133]^T\), \(\|\delta_{ss}\|=0.327\le0.566\) ✓, while the average climbs at \(0.1\) per second. Doubling \(k\) halves the residual disagreement (\(k=4\): \(0.082\le0.141\)) — one more reason to want the largest \(k\) the sampling and delay limits allow.

Zero-mean measurement noise behaves the same way in expectation; its variance accumulates in the drifting average (a random walk) while the disagreement variance stays bounded by the same \(1/(k\lambda_2)\) factor.

9. Packet Loss: Simulation

Five fixed-wing UAVs on a ring share an airspeed command at 10 Hz with \(k=1\) (\(T_sk\lambda_N=0.362\), \(4/(k\lambda_2)=2.89\) s). Initial commands \(x(0)=[62,55,60,66,57]\) m/s, average \(60\). Each packet is lost independently with probability \(p\); a lost packet removes that link for the step (switching topology, Section 6). Two hundred random seeds per case:

loss model\(p\)2 % settling time, meanmax over seedsfinal value
symmetric (link outage)0.02.20 s2.20 s60.000 (average)
symmetric0.33.22 s3.90 s60.000 (average)
symmetric0.65.70 s7.10 s60.000 (average)
asymmetric (per direction)0.3——\(60\pm0.31\) m/s (std), worst \(0.88\) m/s off

Three observations, each tying back to a theorem:

  • Loss slows, it does not destabilise (Section 6): with 60 % loss the swarm still converges, roughly 2.6× slower, consistent with a time-averaged Laplacian \((1-p)L\).
  • Symmetric loss preserves the average, because every instantaneous graph is undirected and the Perron matrix stays doubly stochastic.
  • Asymmetric loss shifts the final value: when UAV \(i\) hears \(j\) but not vice versa the instantaneous graph is directed, the instantaneous \(P\) is only row-stochastic, and the consensus value becomes a random weighted average — the subject of Part 7.

Straddling the Euler limit with no loss confirms Section 5: \(k=5.4\) still converges (\(3\times10^{-4}\) m/s deviation after 20 s), \(k=5.6\) diverges to 762 m/s.

10. Fixed-Wing Interpretation and Design Recipe

10.1 What the shared variable is

In a fixed-wing swarm the consensus state is rarely a position — aircraft cannot park at an agreed point. It is an airspeed or Mach command, a flight level, a mission clock or path parameter (Part 19), or the estimate of a shared reference (Part 8). The disturbance of Section 8 is then a gust-induced speed error or an autopilot bias, and the drift of the average is harmless as long as a leader or a mission reference anchors it (Part 8).

10.2 The recipe: 5-UAV ring, 10 Hz radio, 100 ms latency

  1. Graph: \(\lambda_2=1.382\), \(\lambda_N=3.618\), \(d_{\max}=2\).
  2. Sampling limit: \(k\lt2/(T_s\lambda_N)=5.53\); local Gershgorin \(k\lt1/(T_sd_{\max})=5.0\); margin rule \(k\le0.5/(T_s\lambda_N)=1.38\).
  3. Delay limit (model i, worst case): \(k\lt\pi/(2\tau\lambda_N)=4.34\); margin rule \(k\le\pi/(8\tau\lambda_N)=1.09\). With model (ii) this limit disappears but the value shift of Section 7.2 should be checked.
  4. Choose \(k=1\): \(T_sk\lambda_N=0.362\), \(\tau k\lambda_N=0.362\ll\pi/2\), \(\tau_c=0.72\) s, \(4\tau_c=2.9\) s.
  5. Verify by the packet-loss simulation of Section 9 (settles in 2.2 s clean, 3.2 s at 30 % loss).

At 200 m/s a consensus transient of 2.9 s is 579 m of flight and a 100 ms packet is 20 m stale, so (a) the shared variables must be agreed well before the manoeuvre that needs them, (b) positions carried in packets must be dead-reckoned by \(V_g\tau\) before use (Part 24 §4.4), and (c) the gain should sit as close to the margin rule as the radio jitter allows, because every factor of two in \(k\) halves both the transient distance and the disturbance-induced disagreement.
Toward the CLAW This part fixes the timing and gain rules of every consensus-type block in the unified law. The capstone's check D3 is Section 5 verbatim (\(T_s(k_p+k_c\lambda_{\max})\lt0.5\), \(T_{\mathrm{comm}}k_\xi\lambda_{\max}(H)\lt0.5\)); its handling of stale packets — compare the neighbour's dead-reckoned value with your own current state, drop links whose packets are older than \(T_{\mathrm{stale}}\), and rely on the union graph staying connected — is Sections 6 and 7.2; and its ISS-style convergence statement (Part 24 §11) is Section 8 applied layer by layer. The packet-loss simulator of Section 11 is the seed of the course's reference simulator's comms.py.

11. Implementation

The script reproduces Section 9 and the Euler-limit check of Section 5. Pure NumPy; the switching topology is built from the packets that actually arrive in each slot.

import numpy as np

def ring(N):
    A = np.zeros((N, N))
    for i in range(N):
        A[i, (i+1) % N] = A[(i+1) % N, i] = 1.0
    return A

def simulate(A, x0, k, Ts, p_loss, T=20.0, symmetric=True, seed=1, tol=0.02):
    """Euler consensus x <- x - Ts*k*L_k x, where L_k is built from the packets
    that actually arrived in this comm slot (packet loss = switching topology)."""
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    N = len(x0); x = x0.copy(); xbar = x0.mean()
    e0 = np.abs(x0 - xbar).max(); t_settle = None
    for n in range(int(T/Ts)):
        if symmetric:                       # link outage: both directions lost
            keep = np.triu(rng.random((N, N)) >= p_loss, 1)
            keep = keep | keep.T
        else:                               # each direction lost independently
            keep = rng.random((N, N)) >= p_loss
        Ak = A * keep                       # Ak[i,j] = 1: UAV i heard UAV j now
        Lk = np.diag(Ak.sum(1)) - Ak
        x = x - Ts*k*(Lk @ x)
        if t_settle is None and np.abs(x - xbar).max() < tol*e0:
            t_settle = (n+1)*Ts
    return x, t_settle

12. Common Pitfalls

  • Integrating at the autopilot rate with radio-rate data. The effective step is still the communication period; see the warning in Section 5.
  • Using \(d_i\) instead of \(d_{\max}\) in the local rule. \(\varepsilon\lt1/d_i\) only guarantees that your own row of \(P\) is non-negative; stability needs the bound at the busiest node, or Metropolis weights.
  • Reading "consensus survives 60 % loss" as "loss is harmless". The rate drops with \((1-p)\), and asymmetric loss moves the agreed value (Section 9).
  • Applying the delay limit \(\pi/(2k\lambda_N)\) to the wrong model. It is for model (i). If you implement model (ii) you have no hard limit but a delay-dependent consensus value.
  • Forgetting the drift of the average. Consensus bounds disagreement, not the common value; a biased disturbance walks the whole swarm away (Section 8). Anchor the average with a leader (Part 8).
  • Choosing \(k\) at the Euler limit. At \(k=4.9\) on \(K_4\) the modes alternate sign (\(\mu=-0.96\)); the aircraft receive a chattering command and any jitter in \(T_s\) tips it over. Keep \(T_sk\lambda_N\le0.5\).
  • Mixing up dwell time and union period. The jointly-connected theorem needs both: links must stay up long enough to act (\(\tau_d\)) and the union over \(T\) must be connected.

13. Exercises

Exercise 1 (step size). Five UAVs in trail form a path graph; \(T_s=0.1\) s. Give the Euler and Gershgorin gain limits, the optimal step \(\varepsilon^\ast\), the corresponding spectral radius, and the number of steps to reach 1 % of the initial disagreement at \(\varepsilon^\ast\) and at \(k=1\).
Show solution
Path-5: \(\lambda=\{0,0.382,1.382,2.618,3.618\}\), \(d_{\max}=2\). Euler: \(k\lt2/(0.1\cdot3.618)=5.53\); Gershgorin: \(k\lt1/(0.1\cdot2)=5\). \(\varepsilon^\ast=2/(0.382+3.618)=0.5\Rightarrow k=5\), \(\rho^\ast=(3.618-0.382)/4=0.809\), steps to 1 %: \(\ln0.01/\ln0.809=21.7\) (2.2 s). At \(k=1\): \(\rho=\max|1-0.1\lambda_i|=0.962\), 118 steps (11.8 s). The path's poor \(\lambda_2\) is what makes it slow; the optimal step merely balances the two extreme modes.
Exercise 2 (delays). For \(K_4\) with gains \(k=1\) and \(k=2\), compute the model-(i) delay limit. Then show that under model (ii) the consensus value of \(K_4\) is the plain average for every \(\tau\), and explain why.
Show solution
\(\lambda_N=4\): \(\tau^\ast=\pi/8=0.393\) s for \(k=1\) and \(\pi/16=0.196\) s for \(k=2\). Model (ii): \(c=(\mathbf 1^Tx(0)+\tau d^Tx(0))/(N+\tau\mathbf 1^Td)\); in \(K_4\) all \(d_i=3\), so \(d^Tx(0)=3\,\mathbf 1^Tx(0)\) and \(\mathbf 1^Td=3N\), giving \(c=\mathbf 1^Tx(0)/N\) — the degree-weighted and plain averages coincide for any regular graph. Check: \(x(0)=[1,-2,0.5,3]\Rightarrow c=0.625\) for \(\tau=0.2\) and \(1.0\).
Exercise 3 (Metropolis). Compute the Metropolis weight matrix of the 3-UAV star and show it equals \(I-L/3\). What are its eigenvalues, and which uniform \(\varepsilon\) would it correspond to?
Show solution
Degrees \((2,1,1)\): \(w_{12}=w_{13}=1/(1+2)=1/3\), \(w_{11}=1/3\), \(w_{22}=w_{33}=2/3\). So \(W=\begin{bmatrix}1/3&1/3&1/3\\1/3&2/3&0\\1/3&0&2/3\end{bmatrix}=I-L/3\), eigenvalues \(\{1,\,0.667,\,0\}\): the uniform step \(\varepsilon=1/3=1/(1+d_{\max})\). On a star the hub's degree sets every weight, so Metropolis reduces to the conservative uniform rule.
Exercise 4 (fast switching). Repeat the Section 6 example with dwell times \(0.5\), \(0.1\) and \(0.01\) s and compute the effective convergence rate from the one-period map. What does it converge to as the dwell time shrinks?
Show solution
Rates \(0.279\), \(0.292\), \(0.293\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}\). They approach \(\lambda_2\big(\tfrac12(L_1+L_2)\big)=0.293\), the algebraic connectivity of the time-averaged Laplacian — not \(\lambda_2(L_1+L_2)=0.586\) of the union, because each link is only active half the time. Fast switching (averaging theory) lets you design with the average graph; slow switching costs a little extra.
Exercise 5 (ISS bound). Prove \(\|\delta(t)\|\le e^{-k\lambda_2t}\|\delta(0)\|+\|Pw\|_\infty/(k\lambda_2)\) for \(\dot x=-kLx+w\), and evaluate the steady-state disagreement of the star example with \(k=4\).
Show solution
With \(V=\tfrac12\|\delta\|^2\): \(\dot V=-k\delta^TL\delta+\delta^TPw\le-k\lambda_2\|\delta\|^2+\|\delta\|\,\|Pw\|\). Let \(r=\|\delta\|\); then \(\dot r\le-k\lambda_2r+\|Pw\|\) wherever \(r\gt0\), and the comparison lemma gives \(r(t)\le e^{-k\lambda_2t}r(0)+(1-e^{-k\lambda_2t})\|Pw\|_\infty/(k\lambda_2)\), which implies the claim. Star, \(k=4\), \(w=[0.5,-0.3,0.1]^T\): bound \(0.566/4=0.141\); exact \(\delta_{ss}=(4L)^+Pw\) has norm \(0.082\).

14. Key Takeaways & Formula Sheet

  • Lyapunov function \(V=\tfrac12\delta^T\delta\): \(\dot V=-\delta^TL\delta\le-\lambda_2\|\delta\|^2\Rightarrow\|\delta(t)\|\le e^{-k\lambda_2t}\|\delta(0)\|\). Needs only \(L\mathbf 1=0\), \(L=L^T\succeq0\), \(\lambda_2\gt0\).
  • Settling: \(\tau_c=1/(k\lambda_2)\), \(t_{2\%}\approx4/(k\lambda_2)\); achievable speed limited by \(\lambda_N/\lambda_2\).
  • Discrete time \(x[k+1]=(I-\varepsilon L)x[k]\): stable iff \(0\lt\varepsilon\lt2/\lambda_N\); local rule \(\varepsilon\lt1/d_{\max}\) (doubly stochastic \(P\), average preserved); optimal \(\varepsilon^\ast=2/(\lambda_2+\lambda_N)\); Metropolis \(w_{ij}=1/(1+\max(d_i,d_j))\).
  • Sampled CT law: \(T_sk\lambda_N\lt2\) (aim \(\le0.5\)); use the communication period.
  • Switching graphs: common \(V\); consensus if the union over bounded windows is connected (with dwell time); rate ≈ \(\lambda_2\) of the time-averaged Laplacian.
  • Delays: model (i) \(\dot x=-Lx(t-\tau)\) stable iff \(\tau\lt\pi/(2k\lambda_N)\); model (ii) (stale neighbour, current self) stable for all \(\tau\), value \(c=(\mathbf 1^Tx_0+\tau d^Tx_0)/(N+\tau\mathbf 1^Td)\).
  • Disturbances: \(\|\delta\|_{ss}\le\|Pw\|/(k\lambda_2)\); the average drifts at \(\mathbf 1^Tw/N\).
  • Packet loss slows convergence (\(\approx(1-p)\) factor) and, if asymmetric, shifts the consensus value.
